# Verification model

This document states the intended shape of an Anneal result without committing
to a final annotation syntax, outcome taxonomy, or Lean encoding. It should be
read with the [trust model](trust-model.md) and
[settled requirements](settled-requirements.md).

The [worked byte-buffer example](worked-example.md) applies this model without
ratifying a concrete syntax or proof architecture.

## The claim is about a concrete artifact

Initial V2 verifies one concrete Cargo compilation artifact. Target, features,
`cfg` values, dependencies, panic strategy, generated source, and relevant
environment are fixed inputs to the claim. Verification of a library across a
matrix of possible consumers or targets requires checking multiple artifacts
or future support for a quantified claim.

Proc-macro expansion and Rust code generated by build machinery are included
when they appear in the compiler-resolved artifact ingested by Charon. Anneal
does not trust generated Rust merely because its source text was not checked in.

The distinction between the compilation subject and the identity of the
evidence and claim is detailed in
[verification subject and result identity](verification-artifact.md).

## Layers of a claim

Several related claims must not be collapsed into the word “verified”:

1. **Lean theorem:** Lean's kernel checked a proposition about the generated
   model, relative to the declarations and axioms in that environment.
2. **Rust-artifact claim:** source extraction, translation, obligation
   generation, and adequacy assumptions connect that theorem to one concrete
   Rust compilation artifact.
3. **Abstraction claim:** where the subject is a safe API, contextual
   refinement establishes that every supported type-checked safe client use
   preserves the declared soundness and other guarantees.
4. **Execution claim:** relating the Rust artifact to a binary running on real
   hardware additionally depends on rustc, LLVM, linking, ABI, platform, and
   hardware assumptions or proofs.

These are dependency layers, not a ratified set of command modes or assurance
grades. A result must say which layer it claims and expose the assumptions that
connect it to the preceding layers.

“Supported client use” is not permission to leave the quantification vague. A
whole-artifact result may cover only the concrete call sites and generic
instances included in that compilation subject. A reusable safe-abstraction
claim is stronger: it must quantify over every type-correct safe use within a
declared semantic and compilation envelope, so clients in that envelope can
rely on the interface without rechecking the private implementation.

Verifying one library artifact does not automatically establish that stronger
claim for every downstream target, feature set, compiler revision,
monomorphization, or separately compiled crate. How V2 states, exports, and
checks quantified library contracts across separate compilation remains open.
Until that mechanism exists, a result must report the narrower whole-artifact
claim rather than overstate its client scope. This reporting distinction does
not weaken Rust's underlying requirement: any API presented as safe must in
fact remain sound for every type-correct safe use.

## Local obligations

For an item `f` and property kind `P`, the schematic local claim is:

1. assuming `f`'s applicable preconditions, invariants, capabilities, and
   environmental assumptions;
2. every call or primitive operation made by `f` satisfies the selected
   obligations of the invoked operation; and
3. every relevant behavior of `f` establishes the guarantee required by its
   contract and the selected verification policy.

For an ordinary normal-return contract this specializes to the familiar claim
that preconditions imply postconditions. It does not imply that every property
requires normal termination. A soundness-only policy may permit divergence or
a safe panic while still requiring every executed prefix and unwind cleanup to
remain sound.

The final architecture for outcomes and policies is open. In particular, V2
has not committed to making soundness, panic freedom, and termination three
built-in axes; nor has it committed to treating every property uniformly. See
[property kinds and outcomes](open-questions/property-kinds-and-outcomes.md).

## Global composition

Suppose every non-leaf item in an artifact satisfies its local obligations and
every leaf is either proved against a deeper model or listed with an adequate
trusted specification. Then the local results compose into a whole-artifact
claim relative to those trusted specifications and the recorded environment.

For soundness, Anneal is responsible for the specifications assigned to
primitive unsafe leaves. If those specifications are sufficient conditions for
Rust soundness, and all uses establish them, the artifact is sound within the
documented semantic scope.

The same compositional pattern applies to user-defined properties, including
dependencies between property kinds. A function's soundness may rely on a
callee's protocol or correctness guarantee; such an edge is part of the proof
dependency graph rather than an informal convention.

For example, a caller may use a callee's ordinary arithmetic guarantee that an
index is below a buffer length to discharge the soundness precondition of a raw
pointer operation. This does not make soundness optional: the functional fact
participates in the proof that the caller remains sound. Mutually recursive
property dependencies may require a joint proof; their general representation
remains open.

## The source/model adequacy problem

Aeneas and Anneal only promise that the Lean model corresponds to sound Rust.
Yet Anneal intends to use that model to prove soundness. V2 must therefore
justify the model without assuming the conclusion unchecked.

At minimum, the architecture needs these facts:

- every source operation that could violate the modeled Rust semantics is
  identified;
- the model remains faithful for the execution being reasoned about while the
  required validity conditions hold;
- a proof obligation guards every such operation; and
- Anneal rejects or reports incomplete coverage if any obligation is absent.

Satisfying those facts rules out an unaccounted invalid operation and thereby
supports the correspondence used by the rest of the proof. The precise
metatheorem, extraction point, and division of responsibility among rustc,
Charon, Aeneas, and Anneal remain research and engineering questions.

Two candidate encodings illustrate that the semantic requirement is distinct
from its spelling:

- Translate precondition proofs as propositional arguments of Lean functions,
  so an ill-justified call is not type-correct.
- Leave Aeneas definitions unchanged and prove complete sidecar theorems that
  every call or operation satisfies its requirements, with Anneal enforcing
  coverage.

Either can be adequate if completeness is enforced; either can be inadequate
if obligations can be skipped. Proof arguments align naturally with a
type-system-extension mental model. Sidecar theorems align with Aeneas's
existing WP specifications and tactics. V2 has not chosen between them.

See [source/model adequacy](open-questions/source-model-adequacy.md).

## Specification adequacy

Lean can prove a weak or vacuous user theorem. That cannot be the end of
Anneal's responsibility for soundness. Primitive semantics and other
non-axiomatic sources of Rust requirements must generate or constrain the
obligations that user contracts compose.

There is a bright-line distinction:

- Rust soundness has an external meaning. Anneal authors are responsible for
  adequately formalizing the supported part of that meaning.
- A user-defined correctness property derives its intended meaning from the
  user's specification. Anneal can ensure that an axiom is applied and
  propagated correctly, but cannot determine whether it expresses the user's
  actual business or cryptographic intent.

An axiom can still be wrong relative to its claimed external semantics. The
audit ledger must expose that trust rather than presenting it as a proved fact.

## Invariants

Type and trait invariants are parameterized conceptually by property kind, even
though their final representation is open.

A sound type-invariant mechanism must control every operation that can make the
invariant relevant: construction, access, mutation, moves or borrows where
appropriate, and destruction. V1's `isValid` was unsound because ordinary safe
field mutation could bypass re-establishment. V2 may use Rust unsafe fields,
Anneal-specific field analysis, refinement wrappers, explicit opening
operations, or another mechanism, but may not retain that gap.

A trait invariant imposes an obligation on each relevant implementation and
provides a consumable assumption wherever the trait bound is known. V1's
`isSafe` suggested this shape without enforcing it. V2 must enforce both sides.

If an invariant contains resources rather than freely reusable propositions,
the rules for accessing and re-establishing it must preserve those resource
semantics. Merely adding a reusable Lean hypothesis is insufficient.

See [contracts and invariants](open-questions/contracts-and-invariants.md).

## Panic, unwind, and divergence

Anneal must support programs that:

- return normally;
- panic without causing undefined behavior;
- unwind through cleanup code relevant to invariants and soundness;
- catch a panic and resume normal execution; or
- execute indefinitely while preserving invariants on every finite prefix.

This requires the semantic model to distinguish enough behavior to prove the
selected claim. It does not yet decide which distinctions are first-class
property kinds, standard policies, effects in a WP monad, or user-defined
predicates.

A model/tool failure is not a program outcome and must not be accepted as proof
that a property holds. V2 should report unsupported semantics and verification
incompleteness separately from source-level panic or divergence.

## Relationship to Aeneas

Aeneas already combines execution and correctness through WP specifications
and supplies tactics such as `step` and `step*`. V1's separate progress and
conditional-correctness proofs often duplicated work and did not compose
naturally with this machinery. V2 should prefer a shared symbolic execution
when it can express the required behaviors, while leaving room to extend
Aeneas's monad or standard library for resources, unsafe operations, unwind,
concurrency, or other effects.

A shared symbolic execution that emits obligations for several selected
properties is a promising experiment because it may avoid V1's duplicated
work. It is not a settled architectural requirement: property-specific
semantics or proof backends may require distinct reasoning. Any approach must
preserve distinctions that affect the claim.

## What a successful result should expose

The final command interface is open, but a result must make clear:

- the artifact and configuration checked;
- the properties selected and every property dependency or assumption on which
  the result actually relies;
- the local obligations covered;
- the guarantees proved for each relevant behavior;
- trusted leaves and axioms;
- incomplete, prose-justified, opaque, or skipped obligations; and
- toolchain and environment assumptions.

Different command profiles may choose different exit policies, but none may
label a conditional or incomplete result simply "verified" without exposing
the condition.
