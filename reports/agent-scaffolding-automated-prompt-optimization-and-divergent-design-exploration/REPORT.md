# Agent Scaffolding Boundaries, Automated Prompt and Workflow Optimization, and Divergent Design-Space Exploration

## Summary

Primary-source inspection of 25 peer-reviewed papers, technical reports, and open publisher supplements (1956–2026), six primary engineering studies, 14 pinned public GitHub repositories, and a deterministic four-probe verification suite establishes five operational invariants spanning agent scaffolding boundaries, automated prompt/workflow optimizers, and structured divergent exploration:

1. **Deterministic Scaffolding Splits into Retired Model Crutches vs. Enduring System-Boundary Guards (2024 → 2026):** In 2024-era coding agents (`SWE-agent` on `GPT-4 Turbo`), deterministic Agent-Computer Interface (ACI) affordances delivered large lifts over raw shell (`18.00%` vs. `11.00%` SWE-bench Lite; removing `edit` dropped `-7.7 pp`, disabling atomic `flake8` rollback dropped `-3.0 pp`, `6`-run SD = `0.49 pp`), `Aider`'s `udiff` format + flexible applier lifted `gpt-4-1106-preview` refactoring **`20% → 61%`** while emotional-appeal prose *worsened* scores and disabling the applier multiplied errors **`9×`**, and `Agentless`'s deterministic localization + reproduction-test pipeline beat autonomous `SWE-agent` on the same `GPT-4o` (`32.00%` @ `$0.70` vs. `18.33%` @ `$2.53`). Longitudinal source audits across `SWE-agent@3ea751c0` → `mini-swe-agent@04d809ce` → `OpenHands/software-agent-sdk@3a893a1b` show that **model-crutch guards** (atomic post-edit syntax rollback, stateful line-windowed viewers, bespoke search paginators, regex action parsers, 1-shot trajectory demos) were systematically retired as frontier tool-calling matured, whereas **four system-boundary guards survived or hardened**: output truncation + steering notices (`10K–50K` chars), absolute-path / stateless-`cwd` canonicalization + exact-match edit uniqueness, step/cost/wall-time/format-error/stuck-loop circuit breakers, and container isolation + shell security rails + executable verification gates.
2. **Automated Prompt/Workflow Optimizers Require Verifiable Oracles and Offer Zero Native Intra-Prompt Structural Invariant Locking:** Across `DSPy`/`MIPROv2@226d534d`, `TextGrad@75e912e2`, `GEPA@ce5b0d6e`, `ADAS@2702bee8`, and `AFlow@3f457218`, reflective trace evolution (`GEPA`) outperforms `GRPO` by `+5.94 pp` avg on `Qwen3-8B` (`54.85%` vs. `48.91%`; `6.1×` avg total rollout reduction `3,936` vs. `24,000`, and up to `35.4×` time-to-optimal on `IFBench` at `678` rollouts) and lifts `GPT-4.1-Mini` `+12.19` to `+13.33 pp` with `9.2×` shorter prompts than `MIPROv2`. However, direct source audits establish that **none of `textgrad`, `gepa`, or `dspy` natively locks frozen safety/authority sections inside an optimized prompt** (every built-in proposer performs full-component string replacement; only `dspy.GEPA` enforces a syntactic `max_chars` check). Hard invariant isolation therefore requires external factorization outside the optimized variable map or custom `ProposalFn`/`AcceptanceCriterion` wrappers.
3. **Code-vs.-Paper Discrepancies and Archive-Selection Failure Modes Govern Real Optimizer Behavior:** Source audits uncover concrete failure mechanisms absent from headline claims: `ADAS`'s `_arc/search.py` runs four unsandboxed host `exec()`/`eval()` calls (`L177/L221` `exec(code, {}, local_vars)`, `L373` `exec(forward_str, globals(), namespace)`, `L407` `eval(res)`) with zero container configuration despite paper §6 claiming containerized execution; `TextGrad`'s `TextualGradientDescentwithMomentum._update_prompt` (`L233–258`) omits `return prompt` (returning `None`); `AFlow`'s paper swaps $(\alpha, \lambda)$ internally between §3 Eq. 3 (`0.4, 0.2`) and Appendix A Alg. 1 (`0.2, 0.4`), leaves score scale `[0,1]` vs. `[0,100]` unspecified, lacks visit-count exploration, and its `get_top_rounds` (`data_utils.py` L40–59) silently drops Round 1 once outside top-`sample` (`4`). Equal-footed surrogate tests (`Probe 1`, `[toy model / mechanistic surrogate]`) show visit-count `Archive UCB` beats visit-free `AFlow Top-4` on deceptive 2-basin trees (`94.0–99.7%` vs. `16.3%`, with `98.1%` Round-1 drop), while `AFlow Top-4` wins a stochastic-mutation unimodal ridge (`25.5–46.1%` vs. `7.1–10.9%`). Meanwhile, `GEPA`'s per-instance Pareto archive (`Probe 2`, `[toy model / mechanistic surrogate]`) preserves specialists that `SelectBest` drops (`100%` vs. `0%`) and that `TopKPareto(K=5)` drops whenever `#generalists >= K`, but suffers post-entry noise pollution under both continuous (`44.8%` inferior sample weight at $\sigma = 0.15$) and binary stochastic (`23.2%` Bernoulli) scores.
4. **Post-Training Typicality Bias Creates an "Artificial Hivemind" That Naive Ensembling and Aggressive Decoding Cannot Cure:** Controlled human-AI studies (`Padmakumar & He`, `Doshi & Hauser`) and RLHF evaluations (`Kirk et al.`) show feedback-tuned models boost individual ideation (`+8.1–9.0%`) while collapsing per-input and collective diversity (`-8.9%` to `-10.7%` similarity range). On `Infinity-Chat` (`Artificial Hivemind`, `26,070` open-ended queries), intra-model cosine similarity exceeds `0.8` in `79%` of cases (`61.2%` even under `T=2.0, min-p=0.1`), cross-family pairs converge (`DeepSeek-V3` / `qwen-max-2025-01-25` = **`0.82`**; `DeepSeek-V3` / `gpt-4o-2024-11-20` = **`0.81`**; `~8` distinct models per top-50 cluster), and LM judges/RMs lose human correlation on pluralistic alternatives. `Verbalized Sampling` traces this collapse to annotator typicality bias ($\hat{\alpha} = 0.569$, `OR = 1.42` on `HelpSteer` ties) and recovers **`1.6×–2.1×`** diversity at **`1.12×`** token cost (with **`1.5×–2×`** larger gains on frontier models, §5.1 Fig. 3e–f), yet remains bounded by a single unfactored pretraining prior.
5. **Externalized Morphological Structure (GMA/CCA), Non-Myopic Experimental Design (BED/EIG), and Multi-Epoch Tradespaces Guard Against Premature Convergence and Model Misspecification:** Zwicky General Morphological Analysis + Ritchey Cross-Consistency Assessment prunes an exponential Cartesian field ($\prod v_i$) by `90–99%+` using only $O(n^2)$ pairwise logical/empirical checks (`Probe 3 [A]`: exact $1 - (1-p)^{15}$ across `240` cells vs. `4,096` configs), separating feasibility discovery from normative preferences (`Probe 3 [C]` constructive witness: fixed univariate bans algebraically commute with CCA, but choosing bans blind to tradespace coupling eliminates `2/2` multi-epoch `PT = 3/3` optima). In active empirical probing (`Lindley`, `Rainforth et al.`, `Probe 4`), non-myopic 2-step lookahead DP dominates both optimal static (`3.2485` vs. `2.4646` bits EIG; `88.1%` vs. `65.4%` MAP) and myopic EIG/cost (`2.6259` bits, `69.0%`) when well-specified, whereas model misspecification exposes two distinct failure modes: **selective-confounder overconfidence** (`Probe 4 [B]`: `88.1%` wrong-branch confidence, `NLL = 5.12`, though still below misspecified `Static (Opt Assumed)` `NLL = 6.21`) and **repeated-extrema collapse** (`Rainforth et al.` §5.3, `Probe 4 [C]`: linear EIG places `6/6` queries at `{-1, +1}` with `0` interior points, blind to quadratic curvature with `0.0144` in-sample linear residual vs. `0.7114` under an interior-covering grid).

## Findings

### 1. The Scaffolding Boundary: Deterministic ACI vs. Prose and the 2024 → 2026 Shift

`Basis: source + documentation + derived`

#### 1.1 Empirical Value of Deterministic Interfaces, Fixed Pipelines, Edit Formats, and Code Actions

1. **Deterministic ACI vs. Raw Shell and Demonstrations (`SWE-agent`, `arXiv:2405.15793v3`, Tables 1–3, 10, §5.1):**
   - Holding the base model fixed (`GPT-4 Turbo`), `SWE-agent`'s custom ACI resolves **`18.00%` (`54/300`)** on `SWE-bench Lite` (`12.47% = 286/2,294` Full; `87.7% / 89.7% / 87.9%` `pass@1` on `HumanEvalFix` Python/JS/Java) vs. **`11.00%`** for an interactive Linux `Shell-only` agent (**`+7.0 pp` / `+64%` relative**) and **`2.67%`** for BM25 RAG. Removing the 1-shot trajectory demo drops `Shell-only` **`11.00% → 7.33%`** (`-3.67 pp`), vs. only **`18.0% → 16.3%`** (`-1.7 pp`) for `SWE-agent`.
   - *Table 3 component ablations (baseline `18.0%`; note Table 10 6-run repeatability on `SWE-bench Lite` has mean `17.94%`, range `17.33%–18.67%`, **SD = `0.49 pp`**, so single-run deltas of `-1.7` to `-2.3 pp` sit near run-to-run variance while large drops are unambiguous):*
     - Removing `edit` (falling back to raw `sed`/shell): **`18.0% → 10.3%` (`-7.7 pp`)**.
     - Replacing summarized search (capped at `50` matches in the 2024 paper; drifted to `>100` in pinned 2026 `tools/search/bin/{search_dir,search_file}`) with an IDE-style **Iterative (`next`)** paginator: **`18.0% → 12.0%` (`-6.0 pp`)** — *worse than removing search tools entirely (`15.7%`, `-2.3 pp`)* because agents exhaustively page through matches until budget exhaustion.
     - Viewing full files (`12.7%`, `-5.3 pp`) or overly narrow 30-line windows (`14.3%`, `-3.7 pp`) both trail the 100-line window (`18.0%`).
     - Disabling the pre/post-edit `flake8` rollback guard (`15.0%`, **`-3.0 pp`**) and disabling 5-turn observation collapsing (`15.0%`, **`-3.0 pp`**) produce identical drops.
2. **Fixed Deterministic Pipelines vs. Autonomous Agent Loops (`Agentless`, `arXiv:2407.01489v2`, Tables 1–4, 6, §6.1; `OpenAutoCoder/Agentless@5ce5888b`):**
   - A non-agentic 3-stage pipeline (hierarchical Localization → sampled Repair → test-based Validation) on `GPT-4o` solves **`32.00%` (`96/300`)** on `SWE-bench Lite` at **`$0.70` / `78,166` tokens** per issue — outperforming autonomous `SWE-agent` on the same `GPT-4o` (**`18.33%`**, `$2.53`, `498,346` tokens) and `SWE-agent` on `Claude 3.5 Sonnet` (`23.00%`, `$1.62`, `521,208` tokens), while reaching **`38.80%` (`194/500`)** on `SWE-bench Verified` (highest `GPT-4o` system in Table 6; note on `SWE-bench Lite` Table 1, closed-source `GPT-4o` agent `MentatBot` achieved `38.00% > 32.00%`).
   - *Internal ablation drivers (Tables 2 & 4):* Skeleton-compressed files (class/method signatures only) beat full-file localization (**`58.33%` @ `$0.02`** vs. **`53.67%` @ `$0.15`**, both higher accuracy and `7.5×` cheaper). On 40 sampled patches, majority voting alone solves `25.67%` (`77/300`), existing regression tests lift this to `27.00%` (`81/300`, `+$0.01`), and generated reproduction tests filtered to fail pre-patch lift resolution to **`32.00%` (`96/300`, `+$0.25`)** — a **`+6.33 pp` (`+24.7%` relative)** gain from deterministic execution filtering. Benchmark hygiene audit (§6.1): `4.3%` of `SWE-bench Lite` issues include the exact ground-truth patch in text, `10.0%` lack necessary info for hidden tests, and `5.0%` contain misleading solutions.
3. **Edit Format Mechanics vs. Emotional Exhortations (`Aider`, `Aider-AI/aider@5dc9490b:aider/coders/udiff_coder.py` L29–90; `aider.chat/docs/unified-diffs.html`):**
   - On Aider's 89-task Python AST refactoring benchmark (methods with 100–250+ AST nodes across 9 OSS repos), switching `gpt-4-1106-preview` from `SEARCH/REPLACE` blocks to simplified **unified diffs (`udiff`)** raised pass rate **`20% → 61%`** (`+41 pp` / `3.05×`; `gpt-4-0613`: `26% → 59%`) and cut lazy placeholder omissions `3×` (`12 → 4` tasks).
   - **Prose exhortation null/negative result:** Adding emotional-appeal prose (*"user is blind, has no hands, will tip $2000..."*) **degraded** scores for *both* `SEARCH/REPLACE` and `udiff`.
   - **Parser/formatter ablations:** Dropping whole-block "high-level diff" prompting increased edit errors `30–50%`, and disabling Aider's **flexible diff applier** (`udiff_coder.py` L69–90: hunk normalization, missing-`+` recovery, relative whitespace matching, hunk splitting, context sliding) increased editing errors **`9×`** on the 133-task Exercism Python benchmark.
4. **Executable Code Actions vs. JSON/Text Schemas (`CodeAct`, `arXiv:2402.01030v4` Tables 2–3; `OpenHands`, `arXiv:2407.16741v3` Table 4):**
   - On multi-turn multi-tool composition (`M3ToolEval`, 17 LLMs), unified Python `CodeAct` wins on **`12/17` models** for both success rate and fewest turns: `gpt-4-1106-preview` **`74.4%` (`5.5` turns)** vs. JSON `52.4%` (`7.6` turns, `+22.0 pp`) and Text `53.7%` (`7.7` turns); `claude-2` `54.9%` vs. `39.0%` JSON (`+15.9 pp`). *Capability floor boundary:* weaker models reverse (`claude-instant-1`: `20.7%` CodeAct vs. `31.7%` JSON; `Mistral-7B`: `0.0%` vs. `3.7%`). On `OpenHands` (`CodeActAgent v1.8`, bash + IPython + event stream), a single domain-agnostic prompt reached `26.0%` SWE-bench Lite on `Claude 3.5 Sonnet` (`$1.10/task`) and `22.0%` on `GPT-4o` (`$1.72/task`).
5. **Poka-Yoke ACI Design and Long-Running Harnesses (`Anthropic` 2024–2025 Engineering Reports):**
   - Reaching **`49.0%`** on `SWE-bench Verified` with `Claude 3.5 Sonnet` using only two general tools (`bash` + `str_replace_editor`), Schluntz & Zhang report spending **"more time optimizing our tools than the overall prompt"**: requiring **absolute paths** in the tool schema eliminated relative-path `cd`-drift errors entirely; avoiding JSON code-escaping and exact diff line-count headers removed autoregressive cognitive overhead; Claude Code caps tool outputs at `25,000` tokens with pagination-steering error messages. For multi-session work (`anthropic-long-running`), context compaction alone fails even on frontier models via premature one-shotting or premature completion claims, requiring an explicit **Initializer + Incremental Coding** harness (`init.sh`, progress log, git commits, structured `feature-list.json`).

#### 2.2 Zero Native Intra-Prompt Structural Invariant Locking Across `textgrad`, `gepa`, and `dspy`

Direct inspection of every built-in proposer and acceptance gate in `zou-group/textgrad@75e912e2`, `gepa-ai/gepa@ce5b0d6e`, and `stanfordnlp/dspy@226d534d` proves a shared architectural boundary: **none of these three frameworks natively locks frozen safety, authority, or hook clauses inside an optimized prompt string.**

- **`zou-group/textgrad@75e912e2`:** `TextualGradientDescent(..., constraints: List[str])` (`optimizer.py` L86, L115–117, L126–134) injects constraints strictly as soft prompt prose (`<CONSTRAINTS>...</CONSTRAINTS>`, `optimizer_prompts.py` L58–61, L99–100; confirmed verbatim in *Nature* `MOESM1` Supp. Note 2.2). `step()` (L176–186) unconditionally calls `parameter.set_value(new_value)` with zero post-check; `GuidedTextualGradientDescent` (`guidance_optimizer.py` L60–82) constrains only outer XML tags. *Coarse escape hatch:* isolate frozen text in a separate `Variable(..., requires_grad=False)` (`variable.py` L17, L28) omitted from `Optimizer(parameters=[...])`, as done for Chameleon's `EasyOCR` (`MOESM1` L2026–2028).
- **`gepa-ai/gepa@ce5b0d6e`:** `ReflectiveMutationProposer.propose()` (`reflective_mutation.py` L595–598) overwrites every mutated component verbatim (`new_candidate[name] = text`). Built-in `AcceptanceCriterion` (`acceptance.py` L44–66) checks only scalar subsample sums (`StrictImprovementAcceptance` / `ImprovementOrEqualAcceptance`), and built-in selectors (`component_selector.py` L10–36) visit all keys in `seed_candidate`. *Coarse escape hatch:* keep frozen invariants outside `seed_candidate` (injecting them deterministically inside `GEPAAdapter.evaluate`), or write custom `module_selector` + `ProposalFn` + `AcceptanceCriterion` hooks enforcing exact substring/AST preservation.
- **`stanfordnlp/dspy@226d534d`:** `GEPA.compile()` (`dspy/teleprompt/gepa/gepa.py` L649–651) exports every predictor's full `signature.instructions` into `seed_candidate`, and `DspyAdapter.build_program()` (`gepa_utils.py` L219–221) overwrites it whole (`with_instructions(candidate[name])`). In `dspy/teleprompt/gepa/instruction_proposal.py`, `additional_instructions` (L352–357) and `skills` (L358–365) are soft prompt text; the **sole** hard validator is `max_chars` via Pydantic `StringConstraints` (L339–341, `_enforce_length` L429–438, whose retry signature `CompressInstruction` L206–208 only asks in prose to *"Preserve every strategy, rule, and constraint"*). `MIPROv2`'s `GroundedProposer` (`dspy/propose/grounded_proposer.py` L335–417) has zero `constraints`, zero frozen-predictor filter, and zero post-validation.

`Basis: source + documentation + derived + execution`

#### 3.1 Empirical Homogenization Mechanisms and Inference-Time Mitigation Limits

1. **RLHF vs. Base Models (`Padmakumar & He`, ICLR 2024, `arXiv:2309.05196v3` Tables 3 & 10; `Kirk et al.`, ICLR 2024, `arXiv:2310.06452v3` Tables 11–13):**
   - Co-writing essays with feedback-tuned `InstructGPT` significantly ($p < 0.05$) reduces unique 5-grams (`0.991` Solo → `0.977`), key-point semantic diversity (`0.941 → 0.877` at Rouge-L `0.6`), and raises inter-author corpus homogenization (`BertScore 0.6044 → 0.6318`), whereas base `GPT-3` (`0.988`, `0.927`, `0.6130`) does not significantly reduce diversity vs. Solo.
   - `Kirk et al.` show RLHF improves OOD generalization over SFT while collapsing **per-input diversity** (`OPT 6.7B`: EAD **`0.07` vs. `0.79`**, SentBERT **`0.06` vs. `0.45`**, NLI **`-1.54` vs. `+0.06`**) and shrinking **across-input** diversity; Best-of-$N$ preserves much higher diversity (`EAD 0.86`, `SentBERT 0.71`, `NLI 0.30`) at higher inference cost.
2. **Individual Gain vs. Collective Diversity Social Dilemma (`Doshi & Hauser`, *Science Advances* 2024, `DOI:10.1126/sciadv.adn5290`):**
   - In a preregistered RCT ($N = 293$ writers, $N = 600$ blind raters; `88.4%` AI uptake), access to 5 `GPT-4` ideas lifts individual novelty **`+8.1%`** ($p < 0.001$; 1 idea `+5.4%`) and usefulness **`+9.0%`** ($p < 0.001$), concentrated in low-DAT writers (`+10.7%` novelty, `+26.6%` quality), while **shrinking collective story diversity** (higher similarity to condition centroid: 1 idea `b = 0.871, p < 0.001` = `10.7%` of Human-only range; 5 ideas `b = 0.718, p = 0.003` = `8.9%`). Sampling 5 unstructured ideas from an LLM still anchors the collective pool.
3. **Intra-Model Repetition, Inter-Model Homogeneity, and Judge Blindness (`Artificial Hivemind`, NeurIPS 2025 D&B Oral, `arXiv:2510.22954v2` §§2–4, Figs. 4–8):**
   - Across `Infinity-Chat` (`26,070` open-ended queries, `15.2%` Brainstorming & Ideation; `31,250` annotations = `18,750` absolute + `12,500` pairwise across 25 raters/item), intra-model cosine similarity exceeds **`0.8` in `79%` of cases** (`T=1.0, top-p=0.9`), and even under aggressive `min-p` decoding (`T=2.0, min-p=0.1, top-p=1.0`), **`81%`** exceed `0.7` and **`61.2%`** still exceed `0.8`.
   - **Naive cross-family ensembling fails:** Distinct model families converge on identical metaphors (`"time is a river / weaver"`) and high cosine similarity — **`DeepSeek-V3` and `qwen-max-2025-01-25` reach `0.82`** while **`DeepSeek-V3` and `gpt-4o-2024-11-20` reach `0.81`**, with an average of **`~8` unique models** co-occupying top-50 similarity clusters.
   - **LM-judge / RM miscalibration (§4.3):** Across `56` LMs, `6` top RewardBench RMs, and `4` LM judges, human correlation drops sharply on comparable-quality alternatives and high-disagreement items, invalidating automated scalar-judge prompt optimization on pluralistic design tasks.
4. **Typicality Bias & Inference-Time Recovery (`Verbalized Sampling`, `arXiv:2510.01171v4` §§4–5.1, Tables 2–4, 25, 30, Fig. 3e–f, App. B & D):**
   - On `HelpSteer` correctness-tied pairs (`6,874` ties / `28,283` adjusted, Table 4), human annotators significantly prefer higher base-model log-probability text (**$\hat{\alpha} = 0.569 \pm 0.073$, `OR = 1.42`, $\Delta P = +0.17$** on `Llama-3.1-405B`; **$\hat{\alpha} = 0.649$, `OR = 1.47`** on `GLM-4.5`), which RLHF amplifies into single-mode or fixed-bestseller collapse.
   - Prompting for a **distribution** (*"generate 5 distinct responses with their corresponding probabilities"*, optionally $p < \tau$) lifts creative diversity **`1.6×–2.1×`** (Table 2: Poem `1.90 → 2.39`, Story `2.74 → 3.06`, Joke `1.83 → 3.01`) at **`1.12×`** token cost for `2.11×` diversity (Table 30), retains safety within `0.31–0.77 pp` (`97.45–97.91%` vs. `98.22%`, Table 25; KL `0.12`), and rescues synthetic math training (`Qwen3-4B` Table 3: Baseline `40.7%` → Direct `34.5%` → Sequence `42.1%` → VS `45.5%`).
   - **Capability-scaling boundary (§5.1 Fig. 3e–f; App. B):** Larger frontier models (`GPT-4.1`, `Gemini-2.5-Pro`) achieve **`1.5×–2×`** greater diversity gains than smaller models (`GPT-4.1-Mini`, `Gemini-2.5-Flash`), while weak models suffer quality drop from probability-estimation burden. Crucially, VS only samples a single model's unfactored pretraining prior; it cannot guarantee orthogonal coverage across explicit architectural dimensions.

#### 3.2 Formal Foundations: Zwicky GMA + Ritchey CCA, Modern BED/EIG, and Co-Evolution / MATE-EEA

1. **Zwicky GMA + Ritchey CCA (`Zwicky 1969`; `Ritchey 2006 JORS`, `2011`; `Probe 3`, `[exact analytical / constructive witness]`):**
   - Factoring a design space into $P$ orthogonal dimensions with $v_i$ states yields an exponential Cartesian product $\prod_{i=1}^P v_i$, whereas pairwise Cross-Consistency Assessment evaluates only $\sum_{1 \le i < j \le P} v_i v_j$ cells ($O(n^2)$). Ritchey documents `90%–99%+` feasible-space reductions from a few hundred pairwise checks (scaling to $10^5$–$7\times 10^8$ formal configs) while retaining an explicit elimination audit trail.
   - *Exact combinatorial verification (`Probe 3 [A]`, `6 dims × 4 states = 4,096` configs, `240` pairwise cells):* Under independent pairwise incompatibility density $p$, expected reduction follows exact closed-form $1 - (1 - p)^{\binom{6}{2}} = 1 - (1 - p)^{15}$, closely matching 20-trial empirical means: `p=0.10` (`80.53%` vs. exact `79.41%`), `p=0.15` (`91.55%` vs. `91.26%`), `p=0.20` (`96.40%` vs. `96.48%`), `p=0.25` (`98.46%` vs. `98.66%`), `p=0.30` (`99.69%` vs. `99.53%`), `p=0.35` (`99.88%` vs. `99.84%`).
   - *Constructive witness — separating feasibility discovery from normative preferences (`Probe 3 [B–C]`):* Ritchey distinguishes **(1) logical contradictions**, **(2) empirical implausibilities**, and **(3) normative constraints**, advising applying only (1)–(2) first. Algebraically, any **fixed** univariate normative ban $N$ commutes with pairwise CCA (`Probe 3 [C]` verifies `|CCA(N(X))| = |N(CCA(X))| = 89`). The real failure mode is **tradespace-blind normative selection**: fixing `ban {D0=1, D4=1}` before seeing how feasibility couples dimensions eliminates **`2/2`** multi-epoch `PT = 3/3` Pareto-optimal architectures (dropping best survivor `mean_u 1.5567 → 1.2533`, `mean_c 0.8733 → 1.2467`, `true_PT = 0/3`), whereas inspecting the `194`-config CCA tradespace first and applying a **tradespace-informed** filter (`ban dominated D0=3`) preserves **`2/2`** `PT = 3/3` optima.
2. **Modern Bayesian Experimental Design (BED/EIG) & Two Misspecification Pathologies (`Lindley 1956`; `Rainforth et al. 2024`, `arXiv:2302.14545v2` §§2–5.3; `Probe 4`, `[exact analytical / toy model]`):**
   - BED chooses probe $\xi$ maximizing Expected Information Gain $\text{EIG}_\theta(\xi) = \mathbb{E}_{p(\theta)p(y|\theta,\xi)}[\log p(\theta|y,\xi) - \log p(\theta)] = I(\theta; y \mid \xi)$ (Eq. 2). Naive Nested Monte Carlo suffers slow $\mathcal{O}(C^{-2/3})$ convergence; static designs waste probes on invalidated branches; greedy myopic adaptive BED ($\arg\max \text{EIG}(\xi_t | h_{t-1})$) misses multi-step lookahead (motivating non-myopic policy methods such as DAD, §4).
   - *Exact analytic evaluation (`Probe 4 [A]`, `H = 16` hypotheses, `4.000` bits, `K = 8` non-repeatable probes, budget `B = 5`):* Under a well-specified likelihood, **`Non-Myopic 2-Step DP`** (`EIG = 3.2485 bits`, `MAP_acc = 88.1%`, `NLL = 0.7515`) strictly dominates **`Myopic EIG/cost`** (`2.6259 bits`, `69.0%`, `1.3741`), **`Myopic Raw EIG`** (`2.4854 bits`, `46.7%`, `1.5146`), and **`Static (Opt Assumed)`** (`2.4646 bits`, `65.4%`, `1.5354`).
   - *Misspecification Mode 1 — Selective-Confounder Overconfidence (`Probe 4 [B]`):* When high-assumed-SNR probes `P0..P3` suffer an unmodeled confounder on odd variants while modest-SNR `P4..P7` remain unconfounded (all 4 policies reported transparently):

| Policy under Selective Confounding (`Probe 4 [B]`, non-repeatable `used_mask`) | `MAP_acc` | `E[P(true)]` | `NLL` (bits) | `E[conf \| wrong]` |
