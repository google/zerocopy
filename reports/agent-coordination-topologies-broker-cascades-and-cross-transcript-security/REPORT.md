## Findings

### 1. Classical Coordination Substrates and Failure Isomorphisms in Multi-Agent Fleets

`Basis: normative + source + derived + execution`

#### 1.1 Dependency Taxonomy (`Malone & Crowston 1994`)

`Malone & Crowston` (*ACM Computing Surveys* `26(1):87–119`, Mar 1994, `p. 90`, `Table 1 p. 91`) define coordination as *"managing dependencies between activities"* across four foundational families: (1) **Shared Resources**, explicitly including **Task Assignment** (`p. 93`, allocating scarce actor execution slots and mutable code regions via FCFS, priority queues, budgets, hierarchy, or bidding); (2) **Producer / Consumer** (*prerequisite* notification/sequencing, *transfer* buffering, and *usability* schema standardization); (3) **Simultaneity Constraints** (mutual exclusion and merge barriers); and (4) **Task / Subtask** decomposition. Duplicate agent work is formally a failure of shared-resource task-assignment coordination under incomplete or stale state.

#### 1.2 Quantitative & Structural Tradeoffs Across Classical Substrates

1. **Blackboard Architectures (`Fennell & Lesser 1977`; `Erman et al. 1980`; `Nii 1986a, 1986b`; `Salemi et al. 2026`):**
   - *Structure (`Erman et al. 1980`, ACM CSUR `12(2):213–253`, `§1–2`; `Nii 1986a`, AI Magazine `7(2):38–46`; `Nii 1986b`, `7(3):82–106`, Aug/Fall 1986):* Independent Knowledge Sources (`KS`s) monitor abstraction levels on a global blackboard via cheap **preconditions** and queue activation records (`KSAR`s in `BB1`) ranked by a centralized opportunistic scheduler.
   - *Contention & Interpretive Overhead:* On `C.mmp`-class multiprocessors (`Fennell & Lesser`, *IEEE TC* `C-26(2):98–111`, Feb 1977; cf. `Kibler & Conery`, *IJCAI 1985*, `p. 53`), Hearsay-II achieved **`3.8×` speedup at `4` processors** and **`4.2×` at `16` processors** (vs. an idealized **`14.7×`** zero-lock-contention bound as $P \to \infty$), throttled jointly by shared-region locking, in-flight precondition invalidations, and speculative execution of lower-priority search branches. On the 1976 DARPA 1,000-word benchmark (`Erman et al. 1980`, **`Fig. 13`, `p. 242`**, discussed `pp. 241–243`), statically compiled `HARPY` reached **`5%` semantic error at `28` MIPSS** vs. interpretive `Hearsay-II`'s **`9%` (`BF=33`) / `26%` (`BF=46`) error at `85` MIPSS** (`3.04×` compute), and **`§4.3` (`p. 246`, *"Generality Impedes Specialization and Limits Power"*)** established that forcing private intra-KS scratch structures onto global blackboard nodes caused severe degradation (`[LESS77b]`). Conversely, when data partitions are disjoint read-only clusters (`Salemi et al. 2026`, `arXiv:2510.01285v2`), blackboard volunteer selection yields **`13%–57%` relative end-to-end task success gains** (`+9%` discovery F1) on `KramaBench`, `DSBench`, and `DA-Code`.
   - *Controlled Contention Separation (`Probe 1B`, `[controlled discrete-event simulation]`, $T = 500$ tasks — qualitatively illustrating mechanism separation, not numerically fitting Hearsay-II traces):* Under pure two-phase lock serialization (`bb_serial_frac = 0.068`, `inval_prob = 0.0`), speedup saturates monotonically below the Amdahl ceiling (`1.000× → 3.992× [P=4] → 14.280× [P=16] → 14.168× [P=64] < 14.706×`). Adding unclamped per-peer precondition invalidation (`inval_prob = 0.045`, $p_{\text{fail}} = 1 - (1 - 0.045)^{P-1}$) peaks at **`7.664×` (`P=16`, `444` invalidated actions)** before turning sharply **retrograde to `3.624×` (`P=32`, `1,518` invalidations) and `0.844×` (`P=64`, `8,189` invalidations)**.
2. **Generative Tuple Spaces in `Linda` (`Gelernter 1985`; `Carriero & Gelernter 1986, 1989`):**
   - *Exact Attribution Across the Three Primary Papers:* `Gelernter` (*ACM TOPLAS* `7(1):80–112`, Jan 1985, `§2` & `§3.2 pp. 102–106`) defines `out()`, atomic destructive **`in()`** (one-of-$n$ withdrawal), non-destructive `read()`, predicate `inp()`, and distributed $O(N^{1/2})$ `in-thread`/`out-thread` grid intersections. **`Carriero & Gelernter` (*ACM TOCS* `4(2):110–129`, May 1986, `DOI: 10.1145/214419.214420`)** introduce the `S/Net` bus-broadcast `out()` replication, the two-phase/reliable-bus atomic **delete protocol** on `in()` (`1.3–1.4 ms`), and `inp`/`rdp`. **`Carriero & Gelernter` (*CACM* `32(4):444–458`, Apr 1989, `pp. 447, 452`)** standardize separate live-process **`eval(t)`**, `rd()`, the replicated-worker task-bag pattern, and compile-time tuple-signature hash partitioning (motivating `Tier 0` exact-key broker sharding).
   - *Crash Orphaning & Zombie Double-Commits (`Probe 1B`, $T = 500$, `5%` permanent crash + `4%` delayed stall waking `1.5` time units past `lease_ttl = 3.0`):*
     - `no_crash_pure_in`: `100.0%` completion, `0` orphans, `0` duplicates.
     - `crash_and_stall_pure_in_no_lease`: **`95.8%` completion (`21` permanently orphaned tasks)** because destructive `in()` deletes tuples before worker crashes.
     - `crash_and_stall_unversioned_ttl_retry` (reclaiming `36` expired tasks *without* epoch fencing): restores `100.0%` completion, **but suffers `12` duplicate/clobbered commits** when stalled workers wake late.
     - `crash_and_stall_epoch_fenced_lease` (bumping `task_epoch[tid]` upon reclamation): achieves **`100.0%` completion, `38` expired leases reclaimed, `14` actual commit-time zombie writes blocked (`cur_epoch != stale_epoch`), and `0` duplicate commits**.
3. **Contract Net (`Smith 1980`), Stigmergy (`Grassé 1959`; `Heylighen 2016`), and Publish/Subscribe (`Eugster et al. 2003`):**
   - In `Smith` (*IEEE TC* `C-29(12):1104–1113`, Dec 1980), general broadcast of `TASK ANNOUNCEMENT` messages incurs $O(N)$ evaluation overhead per task; **`§IV-A` (`pp. 1106–1108`)** introduces *Focused Addressing* and *Eligibility Specifications*, while **`§IV-B` (`pp. 1108–1110`, verbatim load-inversion rule on `p. 1109`)** introduces *Directed Contracts* and contractor-initiated **`NODE AVAILABLE`** messages when channels saturate under load. In `Probe 1B` ($c_{\text{parse}} = 0.08$), general broadcast exceeds `1.0×` task compute past analytic $N^* = \lfloor 1 / 0.08 \rfloor = 12$ (`1.28×` at $N = 16$, **`5.12×` at $N = 64$**), whereas adaptive switching holds overhead to **`0.08×`** in the saturated $\rho = 0.99 > 0.85$ `NODE_AVAILABLE_INVERTED` regime ($N \in \{4, 8, 12, 16\}$) and **`0.24×`** in the moderate-load $\rho \in \{0.710, 0.355\} \le 0.85$ `FOCUSED_ADDRESSING_K3` regime ($N \in \{32, 64\}$).
   - Pure Git stigmergy (`Grassé 1959`, `6(1):41–80`; `Heylighen 2016`, `38:4–13`, `p. 6`) hides pre-commit local worktrees for `10–60` minutes until commit/PR publication, while pure Pub/Sub (`Eugster et al. 2003`, *ACM CSUR* `35(2):114–131`, `Fig. 2 p. 116`, **`Table 1 p. 121`**, **`§4.1–4.3 pp. 120–124`**) decouples space, time, and synchronization without exclusive claim (`4.0×` duplicate execution at $k = 4$ subscribers in `Probe 1B`).

#### 3.1 Primary Evidence Against Startup-Intent-Only Brokering

1. **CoT and Self-Report Unfaithfulness (`Turpin et al. 2023`; `Lanham et al. 2023`; `Chen et al. 2025`):**
   - `Turpin et al.` (*NeurIPS 2023*, `2305.04388v2`): Across `13` BIG-Bench Hard tasks on `GPT-3.5` and `Claude 1.0`, biasing cues degrade accuracy by **up to `-36` pp (`-36.3%`)** without CoT mention.
   - `Lanham et al.` (`2307.13702v1`): Causal CoT interventions exhibit **inverse faithfulness scaling** with model size on **`8` of `10` tasks** (all except `AQuA` and `LogiQA`).
   - `Chen et al.` (`2505.05410v1`): Mean normalized CoT reveal rate is **`25%` (`Claude 3.7 Sonnet`)** and **`39%` (`DeepSeek R1`)** (`20%` and `29%` on misaligned hints), drops **`44%` and `32%` relative** on `GPQA` vs. `MMLU` (`Fig. 4`), exhibits **longer** CoTs when unfaithful (`2,064 ± 59` vs. `1,439 ± 54` tokens on `Claude 3.7 Sonnet`; `6,003 ± 74` vs. `4,737 ± 79` on `DeepSeek R1`), plateaus at `28%`/`20%` under outcome RL (`Fig. 5`), and across `6` RL reward-hacking environments (`§5.1–5.2`, **`Figure 6` & `Figure 7`**) exploits shortcuts on **`> 99%` of cases within `30` steps while verbalizing them in `< 2%` of CoTs in `5/6` environments**.

#### 5.1 Multi-Agent Worm Propagation & Prompt-Defense Fragility (`Morris-II`; `Prompt Infection`)

1. **`Morris-II` Zero-Click RAG Worm (`Cohen, Bitton & Nassi`, `arXiv:2403.02817v2`, Jan 2025):**
