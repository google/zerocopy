# Software Engineering Communication Artifacts, Reader-Grounded Discourse Cohesion, and AI-Authorship Provenance

## Summary

Primary-source inspection of 39 peer-reviewed papers and preprints plus Nygard (2011) (1982–2026), nine pinned public repositories (`WhatMakesAGoodCM/What-Makes-a-Good-Commit-Message`, `maxscha/commitbench`, `DeepSoftwareAnalytics/RACE`, `JetBrains-Research/commit_message_generation`, `sa4s-serc/ArchAI_ADR`, `sa4s-serc/LLM4ADR`, `coinse/libro`, `panthap2/LearningToUpdateNLComments`, and `panthap2/deep-jit-inconsistency-detection`), and a deterministic seven-probe Python 3.10+ standard-library verification suite (`support/verify_se_communication_cohesion_provenance_probes.py`) establishes five operational invariants governing software engineering communication artifacts, technical prose cohesion, and AI-authorship provenance:

1. **Code Diffs Encode *What*, Never Unstated *Why* (`Tao et al. 2012`; `Tian et al. 2022`; `OMG` `Li et al. 2024`; `OMEGA` `Imani et al. 2025`; `DRAFT` `Dhar et al. 2025`; `Karan et al. 2026`):** Among Microsoft engineers (`Tao et al. 2012`, $N = 180$, `~80%` performing change comprehension multiple times per week), change rationale (`I-1`) ranks highest in importance (`> 2.5 / 3.0`) and is straightforward to obtain only when check-in descriptions record it, whereas downstream breakage risk (`I-9`) is the sole need exceeding `2.0` on both importance and acquisition difficulty and homologous-code consistency (`I-10`) is hardest to acquire. Across `1,597` manually labeled atomic commits from five Java OSS repositories (`Tian et al. 2022`, `WhatMakesAGoodCM@2f7c1e33`), `~93%` of surveyed developers expect both *What* and *Why*, yet **`42.45%` (`678 / 1,597`; unweighted 5-repo mean `43.60%`) omit *Why*, *What*, or both**, dominated by **Missing *Why* (`426 / 1,597 = 26.68%`; unweighted mean `27.82%`, `2.28×` Missing *What* at `11.71%`)**. Diff-only LLM commit-message and commit-to-ADR generators narrate syntactic *What* while omitting or fabricating *Why* unless grounded in linked issues, pull requests, deterministic diff narratives with static context (`OMEGA`, 4-bit `Llama 3 8B AWQ`), or human intent (`CMO`, `ISAC`), and automated `BERTScore` (`> 0.81–0.885`) systematically masks rationale omission and brevity deficits (`DRAFT`, `Karan et al. 2026`).
2. **Developer–Reporter Asymmetry in Bug Reports Requires Execution-Driven Post-Filtering and Round-Robin Cluster Interleaving (`Bettenburg et al. 2008`; `LIBRO` `Kang et al. 2023`; `Probe 3A`):** Across `466` Apache, Eclipse, and Mozilla participants (`156 / 809` reached developers, `310 / 1,199` reached reporters; `Bettenburg et al. 2008`), developers rank steps to reproduce, stack traces, and test cases as most helpful while reporters rate those exact artifacts hardest to supply. Translating natural-language reports into executable tests (`LIBRO`, `coinse/libro@645473db`) therefore requires sampling $n = 50$ candidates at $T = 0.7$, retaining only compiling **Fail-In-Buggy (`FIB`)** tests, clustering by normalized failure output, gating on cluster agreement (`clus_size_output_fib > threshold`), and interleaving representatives **round-robin across clusters** (`selection_and_ranking.py:144–147`). Exact replay reproduces `251 / 750` (`33.5%`) Defects4J bugs (`570` FIB bugs, `ROC AUC = 0.8185`, `219 / 350 = 62.57%` precision at `T_aggr > 1`) and `10 / 31` (`32.3%`, printed `32.2%`) post-cutoff GitHub Recent Bugs (`25` FIB bugs, `ROC AUC = 0.7867`, `25 / 25` rank match), where round-robin rescues `jhy_jsoup-1648` (`10 -> 2`) and `jhy_jsoup-1763` (`14 -> 4`), lifting `acc@5` from `6 -> 8` (`T_aggr > 1`).
3. **Member vs. Inline Comment Quality Separates Identifier Restatement from AST-Unrecoverable Invariants, and Diff-Conditioned Co-Evolution Beats Regeneration (`Steidl et al. 2013`; `Panthaplackel et al. 2020, 2021`; `Probe 2A`):** `Steidl et al. (ICPC 2013)` separate member-comment coherence ($c_{\text{coeff}} \in [0, 1]$ via Levenshtein distance `< 2` against camelCase-split method tokens, §VI.A: `> 0.5` flags trivial restatement such as `removeAllMarkers` at `0.750`; `== 0` flags unanchored docs or opaque identifiers such as `calc`) from inline-comment word-count length (§VI.B: 1-word `// Run` is trivial regardless of enclosing method name). Misapplying member-level `c_coeff == 0` to inline invariant comments triggers false alarms (`Probe 2A`). When code changes (`Panthaplackel et al. 2020`, `LearningToUpdateNLComments@060d14bf`), diff-conditioned minimal comment-edit decoding outperforms from-scratch regeneration across `xMatch`, `SARI`, `GLEU`, and developer preference (`30.2%` vs. `12.4%`, $p < 0.05$), though developers still rejected all automated candidates on `55.0%` of cases (`None = 55.0%`).
4. **Surface Readability Formulas (`Flesch-Kincaid`) Backfire as Editing Targets Because Shortening Destroys Propositional Cohesion (`Davison & Kantor 1982`; `Gopen & Swan 1990`; `Britton & Gülgöz 1991`; `McNamara et al. 1996`; `Gibson 1998`; `Probe 1`):** Splitting causal clauses into choppy sentences by deleting connectives (`because`, `therefore`, `although`, `unless`) lowers Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level (`12.61–13.19 -> 6.62–9.41` across four technical passages in `Probe 1`) while collapsing adjacent argument overlap (`1.000 -> 0.000`), connective density (`1.000–1.333 -> 0.000`), and overlap-graph connectivity (`1.000 -> 0.000–0.500`). Repairing Kintsch inference gaps via argument overlap and Given-before-New topic anchoring (`given_in_topic = 1.000` vs. `0.000` on multi-sentence New-before-Given inversions) outperforms original and readability-formula rewrites and matches expert heuristic revision (`Britton & Gülgöz 1991`), bounded by `McNamara et al. (1996)`'s reverse-cohesion interaction on high-knowledge readers performing deep situation-model tasks.
5. **Explicit Provenance Trailers Remain Irreplaceable for Repository Auditability, While Disclosure Penalties Depend on AI Normalization and Observational Trailer Gaps Are Confounded (`Schilke & Reimann 2025`; `Reif et al. 2025`; `Butler et al. 2026`; `Yu et al. 2026`; `Horikawa et al. 2026`; `Probe 3C`):** While AI disclosure triggers trust/competence penalties in general and non-adopter populations (`Schilke & Reimann 2025`, $\theta = 0.81$; `Reif et al. 2025`, $N = 4,439$; `Gai et al. 2025/2026` non-adopters $d = -0.46$), a within-subjects RCT of **`447` AI-normalized enterprise engineers (`1,788` reviews, `Butler et al. 2026`)** finds **no AI-disclosure penalty** on perceived competence ($\beta = -0.034, p = 0.481, d = -0.07$), effectiveness, or comment count, whereas junior seniority strongly penalizes perceived competence and security. Separately, in `Yu et al. (2026)`'s audit of `33,596` **AI coding-agent PRs**, the aggregate `-26.0 pp` merge-rate gap on **human `Co-Authored-By` trailers** (`53.8%` human–agent collaborative vs. `79.8%` pure autonomous agent, Yates $\chi^2 = 2424.04$) is a Simpson's paradox driven by `Codex` (`64.9%` of PRs, `1.2%` human co-authorship) that reverses in `4 / 5` agents and dissolves under repository + commit-count controls, while author/committer collaboration modes retain a monotone `47.6% -> 64.3% -> 82.0%` gradient (`Table 3`). Because post-hoc signature-free agent commit detection reaches only `0.332` precision at corpus scale (`7,837,946` commits, `Horikawa et al. 2026`), explicit provenance trailers cannot be replaced by post-hoc classification.

## Findings

### 1. Commit Messages, Change Comprehension, CMG Benchmarks, and Architectural Decision Records (`ADRs`)

#### 1.1 Industrial Change-Comprehension Information Needs (`Tao et al., FSE 2012`)

`Basis: documentation`

- **Study Design & Frequency (`Tao, Dang, Xie, Zhang, & Kim, FSE 2012`, DOI `10.1145/2393596.2393656`, pp. 1–11, §§3–4):** Online survey (`N = 180` valid responses across Microsoft engineering divisions) plus exploratory and follow-up interviews rating `15` literature-derived information needs on 4-point `0–3` scales for **Importance** (`3 = very important`, `0 = not important`) and **Acquisition Difficulty** (`3 = difficult`, `0 = straightforward`), alongside `24` free-text emergent needs. Over **`36%`** of respondents perform code-change comprehension **several times each day** and an additional **`43.8%`** perform it **once a day or several times per week** (`~80%` multiple times per week or more).
- **Top Scenarios (§4):** The top four change-comprehension scenarios ranked by respondent frequency are **#1 Reviewing others' changes (`121 / 180 = 67.2%`)**, **#2 Fixing a bug (`100`)**, **#3 Developing a new feature (`89`)**, and **#4 Reviewing own changes (`73`)**.
- **Importance vs. Acquisition-Difficulty Asymmetry (§5.1–5.3, Figs. 5–6, Tables 2–4):**
  - Five needs exceeded `2.0` on importance: **`I-1` Rationale** (*"What is the rationale behind this change?"*, #1 importance, `> 2.5 / 3.0`), **`I-2..I-4` Change Quality** (completeness, correctness, design issues), and **`I-9` Downstream Breakage Risk** (*"Does this change break any code elsewhere?"*). Historical defect/change-proneness metrics (`I-14`, `I-15`) ranked lowest.
  - Critically, **`I-1` (Rationale) was rated the *most straightforward* question to answer** by Microsoft engineers (§5.2)—follow-up interviews showed this low acquisition difficulty depended directly on the availability and quality of linked check-in change descriptions. By contrast, **`I-9` (Downstream Breakage Risk)** was the **only** information need exceeding `2.0` on *both* importance and acquisition difficulty, while **`I-10` (Homologous-Code Consistency)** (*"Are there any other places that need similar changes?"*, importance `1.94`) rated hardest to acquire, ahead of `I-9` and `I-6` (Dynamic Behavior Alteration). Emergent needs highlighted **rejected design alternatives**, **atomic change decomposition** (`Table 3`), and **impacted test cases / test owners** (`Table 4`).

#### 1.2 Empirical *What + Why* Commit Taxonomy & Full Dataset Audit (`Tian et al., ICSE 2022`)

`Basis: documentation + source + execution (Probe 2B exact arithmetic verified via live --tian-csv replay against Dataset/sampled messages.csv)`

- **Developer Expectation vs. OSS Reality (`Tian, Zhang, Stol, Jiang, & Liu, ICSE 2022`, DOI `10.1145/3510003.3510205`, `arXiv:2202.02974v1`, Tables 1–3; `WhatMakesAGoodCM/What-Makes-a-Good-Commit-Message@2f7c1e33`, `Dataset/sampled messages.csv`):**
  - In contrast to Microsoft's linked check-in descriptions (`Tao et al. 2012`), where `I-1` Rationale was readily accessible, `~93%` of surveyed developers in `Tian et al.` expect both **What** and **Why**, yet OSS commit logs routinely omit *Why*.

Stratified sampling yielded `1,649` commits (`Dataset/sampled messages.csv`: `1,649` data rows across `339` lowercase-normalized author emails [`341` raw emails, `312` author names]). Excluding `52` multi-change commits (`if_mulit_commit == '1'`) leaves **`1,597` atomic commits** dual-labeled into four classes (`Probe 2B`):

  - **Missing-*Why* Dominance (§4.1–4.2, Table 3):** **`42.45%`** of pooled atomic commits (`678 / 1,597`; **`43.60%`** unweighted 5-repo mean, reported by Tian et al. as `~44%`) omit *Why*, *What*, or both. **`No Why` (`26.68%` pooled / `27.82%` mean) occurs `2.28×` as often as `No What` (`11.71%`)**, peaking at **`34.2%`** on perfective/refactoring commits (`Table 3`, vs. `12.1%` on corrective bug fixes where issue references supply *Why*). The `65` `Neither` commits cluster into five anti-patterns: single-word (`"Polish"`), submit-centered (`"Loader changes"`), redundant/tautological, empty/punctuation-only, and cryptic uncontextualized IDs.
  - **Normalization, Classifier, & Verbatim-Regex Separability (`preprocessor_step1.py:65–68`, `Bi-LSTMClassifier.py:22–65`, `Probe 2B`):**
    - `CommitMessage/Preprocessor/preprocessor_step1.py:65–68` normalizes external references to **`<pr_link>`** and **`<issue_link>`** (alongside `<otherCommit_link>`, `<url>`, `<version>`, `<enter>`, `<tab>`). `CommitMessage/ModelTraining/Bi-LSTMClassifier.py:22–65` defines `ModelConfig` and `bert_lstm.__init__` (`bert-base-uncased` + 2-layer `Bi-LSTM`, `hidden_dim = 384`, `drop_prob = 0.55`; `forward` starts at `L68`), achieving `84.7%` accuracy on `C-Why`, `91.0%` on `C-What`, and `75.9%` combined `C-Good` accuracy (`76.5%` precision / `70.9%` recall on missing *Why*, 10-fold CV, Tables 4–5).

      * **`WHY_CLAUSE_REGEX`** (`because|since|to (prevent|avoid|fix|reduce|ensure|support|allow|mitigate|resolve)|so that|in order to|fixes #?\d+|prevent(s|ing)?|avoid(s|ing)?`): hits `99/919` (`Why+What`), `12/187` (`Why-Only`), `4/426` (`What-Only`), `0/65` (`Neither`) $\implies$ **`96.52%` precision (`111 / 115`)** but only **`10.04%` recall (`111 / 1,106`)**!

#### 1.3 LLM Commit Message Generation (`CMG`) Benchmarks & Why Diff-Only Models Hallucinate *Why*

`Basis: documentation + source + execution (minimal lexical-stem mechanism illustration in Probe 2C) + derived`

- **Benchmark Hygiene & Filtering Artifacts (`Tao et al., IEEE ICSME 2021`, pp. 126–136, DOI `10.1109/ICSME52107.2021.00018`, `arXiv:2107.05373v3`; `CommitBench` `Schall, Czinczoll, & de Melo, SANER 2024`, pp. 728–739, DOI `10.1109/SANER60148.2024.00080`, `arXiv:2403.05188v1`, `maxscha/commitbench@3219f9ce`; `RACE` `Shi et al., EMNLP 2022`, pp. 5520–5530, DOI `10.18653/v1/2022.emnlp-main.372`, `arXiv:2203.02700v3`, `DeepSoftwareAnalytics/RACE@cab39487`; `Eliseeva et al., ASE 2023`, pp. 723–735, DOI `10.1109/ASE56229.2023.00078`, `arXiv:2308.07655v1`, `JetBrains-Research/commit_message_generation@073f8e9d`):**
  - `Tao et al. (ICSME 2021)` introduced the multi-language `MCMD` benchmark and exposed metric sensitivity across BLEU variants in CMG evaluation. Building on `MCMD`, `RACE` (`model.py`, `evaluate.py`) showed diff-only sequence-to-sequence models emit generic templates (`"update X"`) and improved automated metrics across five languages by retrieving similar historical `(diff, message)` exemplars.
  - `CommitBench` (`analyze/{bot_counter,trivial_counter,revert_counter,issue_counter}.py`) audited legacy CMG corpora (`CommitGen`, `NNGen`, `CoDiSum`, `MCMD`) for bots, reverts, trivial messages, cross-split duplicates, and license hygiene (citing `Tian et al. 2022`'s `~44%` OSS quality deficit in Related Work). To fit BERT's `512`-token window, `CommitBench` itself filters out `Diffs > 512` tokens (`Table II` removes `13,296,820` of `23,284,371` raw commits, releasing `commitbench_long` separately), curating **`1,664,590`** (`~1.66M`) permissive-licensed commits across `6` languages (`Java, Python, Go, JavaScript, PHP, Ruby`) while redacting full names via FLAIR NER and email addresses via pattern matching.
  - `Eliseeva et al. (ASE 2023)` (`CommitChronicle`, Zenodo `10.5281/zenodo.8189043`, `10.7M` commits across `20` languages; `appendix/filters/{first_sentence,length,verb_direct_object}.py`) noted prior findings that over `50%` of generated commit messages from earlier CMG approaches were semantically inadequate, showed that aggressive `verb + direct object` (`V-DO`) imperative-only dataset filters skew benchmarks toward trivial *What*-only diffs while hiding failure on realistic multi-sentence *Why*-bearing commits, and demonstrated that conditioning `CodeT5`, `CodeReviewer`, and `GPT-3.5-Turbo` on author/file history (`context ratio` `0%`, `25%`, `50%`) substantially improves completion fidelity.
- **Why Diff-Only LLMs Hallucinate *Why* & Context-Grounded Alternatives (`OMG` `Li, Faragó, Petrov, & Ahmed, PACMSE/FSE 2024`, pp. 745–766, DOI `10.1145/3643760`; `OMEGA` `Imani, Ahmed, & Moshirpour, ICSE 2025`, main research track, pp. 1844–1856, DOI `10.1109/ICSE55347.2025.00048`, `arXiv:2408.02502v2`; `CMO` `Li et al., 2025`, `arXiv:2501.09861v1`; `ISAC` `Ren et al., 2026`, `arXiv:2608.22004v1`):**
  - **`OMG` (`FSE 2024`):** Demonstrates that a raw diff is an operational transition ($\Delta \text{AST}$) that under-determines teleological motivation (*Why*). Equipping `GPT-4` with ReAct tools retrieving PR titles/descriptions, enclosing method/class summaries, caller/callee context, and maintenance-type labels outperforms diff-only baselines in developer evaluations.
  - **`OMEGA` (`Imani et al., ICSE 2025` main track):** While RQ1–RQ2 evaluate quantized `Llama 3 70B AWQ` and `DeepSeek-Coder 33B` without structured diff translation, raw unified diffs still induce `+`/`-` polarity confusion and ungrounded *Why*. The paper's headline system, **`OMEGA` (*lOcal MessagE GenerAtor*)**, translates diffs deterministically via **`Diff Narrator`** (explicit Addition/Removal/Replacement narratives) plus **`FIDEX`** static class/method context, allowing a **4-bit quantized 8B SLM (`Llama 3 8B AWQ`, `<= 8 GB` VRAM)** to surpass `GPT-4` (`OMG`) in practitioner preference.

#### 3.1 Functional Taxonomy, Member `c_coeff`, & Inline Word-Count Separation (`Steidl, Hummel, & Juergens, ICPC 2013`)

`Basis: documentation + execution (Probe 2A exact Figs. 2–5 calculation and inline-misapplication counterfactuals) + derived`

  1. **Member-Comment Coherence Coefficient ($c_{\text{coeff}}$, §VI.A, Figs. 2–4):** Strictly for **member comments**, after stripping delimiters and Javadoc tag names (`@param`, `@return`; parameter-name tokens such as `str` and `A` remain in $C$) and splitting the method identifier by camelCase/acronym boundaries (`HTMLEncode -> ['html', 'encode']`),
     $$c_{\text{coeff}}(C, M) = \frac{|\{w \in C : \exists m \in M,\; d_{\text{Lev}}(w, m) < 2\}|}{|C|} \in [0, 1]$$
     *(Note the literal 1-character Levenshtein `< 2` edge behavior: any 1-character comment token matches any 1-character identifier subword at distance `1`, e.g., `"a"` vs. `"x"`; Figs. 2–4 contain no 1-character method subwords.)*
     - **`c_coeff > 0.5` $\implies$ Trivial Identifier Restatement:** Fig. 2 `/** removes all defined markers */` on `removeAllMarkers` yields **`c_coeff = 0.750` (`3/4`)**.
     - **`c_coeff == 0` $\implies$ Unanchored Doc or Opaque Identifier:** Fig. 3 `/** Thanks to benoit.heinrich on forum.java.sun.com @param str */` on `HTMLEncode` yields **`0.000` (`0/10`)**; Fig. 4 eigenvalue-decomposition doc on `calc` yields **`0.000` (`0/11`)**, rising into the informative target zone **`0.182` (`2/11`, `0 < c_coeff <= 0.5`)** once renamed `calcEigenvalueDecomposition`.
  2. **Inline-Comment Length Criterion (§VI.B, Figs. 5–7):** Strictly for **inline comments**, Steidl et al. evaluate **comment word count**, *not* `c_coeff`: Fig. 5 `// Run` before `shutdownThread.start()` is a **`1`-word trivial short inline comment** (`Probe 2A`), Fig. 6 `// merge` before a loop flags missing method extraction, and Fig. 7 multi-line rationale preserves non-obvious invariants. As `Probe 2A`'s counterfactual tests show, misapplying member-level `c_coeff` to inline comments both makes `// Run` arbitrarily unstable (`1.000` inside `run()` vs. `0.000` inside `shutdownServer()`) and triggers false alarms on high-value inline invariant contracts (`inline_trap_lock_contract`: `misapplied_c_coeff = 0.000`, `15` words, `6` AST-unrecoverable invariant terms; `inline_trap_overflow_contract`: `0.000`, `10` words, `2` invariant terms).

### 4. Psycholinguistic Discourse Cohesion, Readability-Formula Backfire, and AI-Authorship Provenance

#### 4.1 Argument Overlap, Given-Before-New Topic Anchoring, Reverse Cohesion, Locality, & Grounding

`Basis: documentation + derived`

1. **`Britton & Gülgöz (1991)` — Principled Kintsch Inference-Call Repair Matches Expert Heuristics and Beats Formula Revisions (*J. Educational Psychology* 83(3):329–345, DOI `10.1037/0022-0663.83.3.329`):** Applying Kintsch's cyclical working-memory textbase model to a ~`1,000`-word instructional passage to repair missing adjacent propositional argument overlap (*Principled Revision*) across Experiment 1 ($N = 170$ undergraduates) and Experiment 2 ($N = 125$ Air Force recruits) produced large, statistically significant gains in free recall, inference accuracy, and mental-model cognitive structure over both the *Original* text and a *Readability*-formula revision, **systematically matching** an expert *Heuristic* revision via reproducible cohesion rules.
2. **`Gopen & Swan (1990)` — Topic vs. Stress Position (*American Scientist* 78(6):550–558):** Readers expect each clause's opening **Topic Position** (subject / pre-verbal opening) to anchor backward to **Given** context, its clause-final **Stress Position** to carry **New** load-bearing claims, and grammatical subjects to remain close to finite verbs.
3. **`McNamara, Kintsch, Songer, & Kintsch (1996)` — Local/Global Coherence $\times$ Prior Knowledge $\times$ Comprehension Level (*Cognition and Instruction* 14(1):1–43, DOI `10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1`):** Crossing local argument/connective coherence with global macro-structure across `Experiments 1–2` shows **low-knowledge readers require high local and global coherence on both textbase recall and deep situation-model tasks**, and **high-knowledge readers still benefit from explicit structure on textbase recall**, whereas **high-knowledge readers exhibit a reverse-cohesion advantage specifically on deep situation-model tasks** (inference, problem-solving, sorting) when compact text induces active compensatory inference. As a `derived` engineering consequence, code reviewers and future maintainers are typically *high-knowledge* regarding language syntax (*What*—where redundant restatement degrades signal, matching `Steidl` $c_{\text{coeff}} > 0.5$) yet *low-knowledge* regarding local change history (*Why*—which requires explicit causal cohesion).
4. **`Gibson (1998)` Dependency Locality Theory (*Cognition* 68(1):1–76, DOI `10.1016/S0010-0277(98)00034-1`), `Clark & Brennan (1991)` (*Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition*, pp. 127–149, DOI `10.1037/10096-006`), & `Nickerson (1999)` (*Psychological Bulletin* 125(6):737–759, DOI `10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.737`):** `Gibson (1998)` models sentence processing load as head storage cost plus integration cost over intervening discourse referents (explaining why center-embedding and long subject–verb wedges spike error even in modest-length sentences); `Clark & Brennan (1991)` establish that asynchronous, reviewable media without interactive turn-by-turn repair shift grounding costs onto upfront author calibration; and `Nickerson (1999)` demonstrates that authors systematically over-impute their own privileged mental context to readers (explaining why authors omit *Why* in `26.68%` of commits and reproduction steps in bug reports).

#### 4.2 Why Mechanical Surface Readability Formulas (`Flesch-Kincaid`) Backfire

`Basis: documentation + execution (Probe 1 multi-sentence cohesion analyzer with regex syllable heuristic)`

- **Connective-Deletion Backfire (`Davison & Kantor, Reading Research Quarterly 17(2):187–209, 1982`, DOI `10.2307/747483`; `Redish, ACM JCD 24(3):132–137, 2000`, DOI `10.1145/344599.344637`; `Bailin & Grafstein, Language & Communication 21(3):285–301, 2001`, DOI `10.1016/S0271-5309(01)00005-2`):** Because Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level ($\text{FKGL} = 0.39(W/S) + 11.8(\text{Syll}/W) - 15.59$) measures only words/sentence and syllables/word, editing to lower formula scores causes writers to chop subordinate clauses into choppy standalone sentences and strip explicit causal/contrastive connectives (`because`, `although`, `therefore`, `unless`)—increasing real reader inference burden (`Davison & Kantor 1982`)—while penalizing precise technical terminology (`Bailin & Grafstein 2001`) and ignoring headings, layout, and adult domain usability (`Redish 2000`).
- **Deterministic Multi-Sentence Separation (`Probe 1`):** Evaluating four engineering rationale triples (`lease_fencing`, `cache_invalidation`, `retry_idempotency`, `lock_ordering`) where every variant satisfies `n_sents >= 2` (and single-sentence inputs return `None` rather than masking `0/0`) separates all three structural factors cleanly (using a stdlib regex syllable approximation that may vary $\pm 1$ syllable on words like `timeout`/`balances`/`acquire` without altering the `~3.3–6.6` grade-level directional gap):
  - *Chopped readability-targeted prose (`S = 5` sentences):* Achieves the lowest `FKGL` (`7.04 / 6.62 / 9.41 / 7.21`) **while collapsing `adj_arg_overlap` to `0.000`, `connectives/sent` to `0.000`, and `graph_connectivity` to `0.250 / 0.250 / 0.000 / 0.500`** (`given_in_topic`: `0.000 / 0.250 / 0.000 / 0.500`).
  - *New-before-Given topic-inverted prose (`S = 3` sentences):* Preserves causal connectives (`1.000/sent`), `adj_arg_overlap = 1.000`, and `graph_connectivity = 1.000` (`FKGL = 13.46 / 12.17 / 14.41 / 12.92`), yet isolates `Gopen & Swan`'s topic-anchoring failure with **`given_in_topic = 0.000` (`0 / 2` genuine transitions)**.
  - *Principled `Britton–Gülgöz` + `Gopen–Swan` prose (`S = 3` sentences):* Scores higher `FKGL` (`12.66 / 13.19 / 12.71 / 12.61`) while simultaneously achieving **`adj_arg_overlap = 1.000`, `graph_connectivity = 1.000`, `given_in_topic = 1.000`, and `connectives/sent = 1.000–1.333`**.

## Boundaries

- **`unknown` — Effect of AI-Authorship Disclosure on True Seeded-Bug Detection Recall (`TPR`) and False-Positive Nitpick Precision in Human Code Review:** Although `Butler et al. (2026, arXiv:2611.18572v1)` reused `Spadini et al. (2019, 2020)`'s seeded-bug snippets (`4`, `2`, and `2` seeded bugs plus `1` clean `TicTacToe` snippet), they analyzed only perceptual ratings (`Coder Competence`, `Code Effectiveness`, `Coder Laziness`, hiring) and log-transformed raw `Number of comments` (making at least one comment mandatory mid-study)—never scoring true defect-detection recall or nitpick false-positive rates. No 2024–2026 randomized code-review trial directly measuring seeded-bug detection `TPR` under AI-authorship disclosure was identified.
- **`not examined` — News-to-Code-Review Domain Transfer of One-Line vs. Verbose Disclosure Granularity:** Both `Prajod et al. (2026)` ($N = 40$) and `Zheng, Wang, & Sun (2026)` ($N = 412$) tested news articles rather than pull requests, commit trailers, or architectural docs; whether one-line vs. detailed AI disclosure alters reviewer trust or verification behavior in software engineering code review was not directly examined.
- **`not examined` — Live Execution of Upstream Neural Training/Inference Pipelines and Post-2026 Production Commit Corpora:** Upstream repositories (`RACE`, `CommitBench`, `ArchAI_ADR`, `LLM4ADR`, `LearningToUpdateNLComments`, `deep-jit-inconsistency-detection`) were audited at pinned source and committed artifacts rather than executed against live LLM APIs; only `support/verify_se_communication_cohesion_provenance_probes.py` (including live `--tian-csv` replay against `WhatMakesAGoodCM@2f7c1e33`) was executed.
- **`known not to apply` — Treating Observational Human `Co-Authored-By` Merge Gaps in `Yu et al. (2026)` as an AI-Disclosure Penalty:** Every PR in `Yu et al.`'s `33,596`-PR `AIDev` sample is an AI coding-agent PR, and `Co-Authored-By` marks a *human* co-author; inferring that disclosing AI use on human-authored code causes a `-26.0 pp` merge penalty reverses both the causal contrast and the Simpson's paradox.
- **`known not to apply` — Applying `Steidl` Member-Level `c_coeff == 0` to Inline Comments or Optimizing Technical Prose for Lower `Flesch-Kincaid` Grade Level:** `Steidl et al.` restrict `c_coeff` to member comments (§VI.A) and evaluate inline comments by length/non-obvious rationale (§VI.B); applying `c_coeff == 0` to inline invariant contracts creates systematic false alarms (`Probe 2A`), and optimizing prose for lower `FKGL` destroys causal connectives and argument overlap (`Davison & Kantor 1982`, `Probe 1`).
- **`known not to apply` — Treating `Probe 2C` as a Production Hallucination Detector or `BERTScore > 0.81` as Proof of Rationale Fidelity:** `Probe 2C` is a minimal lexical-stem illustration whose own test cases prove false positives on valid semantic paraphrases (`boundary_paraphrase_fp`) and `has_why = False` on bare issue links (`boundary_bare_issue_link`). Similarly, `DRAFT` (`Flan-T5-base` `BERTScore-F1 = 0.885`) and `Karan et al. (2026)` (`BERTScore-F1 > 0.81`, preliminary `n = 30`, single-author Gemini-only manual audit) show high semantic-embedding overlap co-occurs with repetitive brevity or missing architectural *Why*.
- **`unsupported` — Post-Hoc Signature-Free Recovery of AI Commit Provenance at Corpus Scale:** `Horikawa et al. (2026)` demonstrate that signature-free detectors fail (`0.332` precision across `7.84M` commits; `0.524` held-out `Claude Code` AUC) once explicit trailers are stripped.
