# Agent Behavioral Evaluation Statistics, LLM-as-a-Judge Biases, Automated Code Review, and Prose Faithfulness

## Summary

Primary-source inspection of 43 peer-reviewed papers and technical reports (1996–2026), six pinned public repositories (`sambowyer/bayes_evals`, `sambowyer/no_clt_paper`, `i-Eval/FairEval`, `IDEA-FinAI/LLM-as-Evaluator`, `amazon-science/tofueval`, `ShouSawa/AI-Code-Maintainability`), and a deterministic three-probe verification suite establishes five operational invariants for evaluating agent behaviors, deploying automated judges, reviewing agent-authored code, and preserving caveats in technical prose:

1. **Small-Sample Behavioral Evals Break Asymptotic Inference and Suffer Severe Type-M Exaggeration:** Central Limit Theorem (Wald) and nonparametric bootstrap confidence intervals systematically under-cover at $N \lesssim 100\text{–}300$ across IID, clustered, paired, and non-linear metrics (`Bowyer et al. 2025`; exact Binomial Wald coverage collapses to **`63.89%`** at $N = 20, p = 0.05$ and **`87.75%`** at $N = 100, p = 0.05$, vs. **`92.45%` / `96.59%`** for Wilson and `Beta(1,1)` Bayes, `Probe 1a(i)`). Under clustered tasks ($\text{ICC} = 0.40$), naive CLT falls to `61–82%` coverage and clustered CLT still under-covers at small cluster counts (`46.4%` at $2\times 5$, `86.4%` at $4\times 5$), whereas hierarchical Beta-Binomial Bayes recovers nominal coverage (`96.0–100%`) at the cost of prior conservatism when $\text{ICC} = 0$ (`99.0%–100.0%` coverage and **`+24.1%` width** at $N = 300$ under Bowyer's `Gamma(1,1)` prior via unbounded $u = d/(1+d)$ quadrature, `Probe 1a(ii)`). Detecting a `+10 pp` paired lift ($K = 1, r = 0.5$) at $N = 20$ has only **`8.7%`** power with **`3.56×`** Type-M winner's-curse exaggeration (`Probe 1b(ii)`), and lowering decoding temperature to $T = 0$ to eliminate sampling noise can inflate variance **`3×–5.06×`** and inject mean bias (`Miller 2024` §3.3; `Probe 1c`).
2. **LLM-as-a-Judge Protocols Fail Unless Token Order, Position Swapping, and Confounded Length Are Jointly Controlled:** Unswapped judges exhibit severe order conflict (`46.3%` GPT-4, `82.5%` ChatGPT in `FairEval`), multi-bias vulnerability (`CALM` 12 biases; position robustness `< 0.50` across 3–4 candidates), and strong length dominance (`Chatbot Arena Style Control`: standardized length coefficient **`0.249`** is `~8×` larger than markdown lists `0.031`, headers `0.024`, or bold `0.019`). Reconciling `FairEval` (`EC` helps) against `TofuEval` (`EXP` drops weak-model $\kappa$ by up to `-0.38`) and `Gu et al.` (`w/ explanation` crashes GPT-3.5 position consistency `-19.8 pp`) isolates **causal autoregressive token ordering, temperature, and model capacity**: structurally slotted *evidence-before-score* (`FairEval.py:35-49`) conditions the verdict, whereas *verdict-first* (`TofuEval` App. C.3) or simultaneous weak-judge explanations rationalize bias or add `T = 0.7` entropy. Under joint position and verbosity bias (`Probe 2a`, `[toy model / illustrative surrogate]`), swap-and-tie alone *worsens* false promotion (`66.0% → 90.0%` at $N = 100$) by stripping position noise without removing length confounding; adding length-controlled logistic regression restores calibrated Type-I (`~4–5%`) when length is unconfounded, but observational regression cannot separate fluff from substance-correlated detail (`AlpacaEval-LC`, `Arena Style Control`).
3. **Code Verification Asymmetry Favors Execution-Test Filters Over Non-Reasoning LLM Judges:** On `JudgeBench` Coding (`N = 42` borderline GPT-4o pairs), every non-reasoning model is a much worse coding *judge* than *solver* (`14.3–38.1 pp` deficit: Claude-3.5 `88.10%` vs. `64.29%`, GPT-4o `73.81%` vs. `59.52%`, Gemini-1.5-Pro `64.29%` vs. `26.19%`), whereas reasoning judges invert the gap (`o3-mini high 100.0%`, `DeepSeek-R1 92.86%`). By exact Bayes precision algebra (`Probe 3a`, `[exact numerical / derived calculation]`), post-filter positive predictive value $\text{PPV}(\pi)$ is strictly increasing in the likelihood ratio $\text{LR} = \text{TPR}/\text{FPR}$ for all base rates $\pi \in (0,1)$: a `20%`-recall execution test filter (`SWT-Bench` implied `FPR = 4.832%`, `LR = 4.139`, doubling patch precision to **`47.8%`** despite being statistically orthogonal to bug repair at $p = 0.728\text{–}0.804$) dominates any filter with $\text{LR} = 1.80$ whenever $\text{FPR}_{\text{test}} < 11.11\%$, whereas weak tests (`UTBoost` `28.4%` false-pass rate) destroy precision.
4. **Automated Code Review Improves Actionable Hygiene Without Cutting Review Time, While Real-World Coding Benchmarks and Maintenance Studies Expose Persistent Debt:** Google's `AutoCommenter` crossed `>80%` useful ratio and `~40%` code-change resolution only after suppressing `17 + 5` noisy rule URLs, restricting to changed lines, and applying beam search (`1.3% → 3.9%`), yet its 50% randomized A/B test detected **no statistically significant change** in review duration or iterations. Beyond single-file Python `SWE-bench`, agent pass rates collapse across polyglot/systems code (`Multi-SWE-bench`: `52.2%` Python → `2.2%–21.9%`), multi-file refactoring (`RefactorBench`: `12–27%` vs. `87%` 5-minute human; **`0%`** on 3-task compositions), private enterprise repos (`SWE-Bench Pro`: `<18%`), and feature engineering (`FeatureBench`: `74.4% → 0–5.2%` on shared repos). Reconciling `Liu et al. (2026)` (`302,579` AI commits: net **`+7,342`** security debt, **`22.7%`** survival at `HEAD`) against `Sawada et al. (2026)` (`ShouSawa/AI-Code-Maintainability@834794b6`) shows `>52%` of Sawada's AI cohort consists of non-production tests/docs/configs excluded by Liu, aggregate 6-month commit counts are statistically null (`1,286 vs. 1,423`, $p = 0.3758$), and monthly mean AI commits cross *above* human files in Months 4–6 even as M4–M6 median changed lines remain lower.
5. **Generic "Be Accurate" Prompts Double Scientific Overgeneralization; Caveat Preservation Requires Explicit Decomposition and Lightweight NLI Verification:** Across `4,900` scientific summaries (`Peters & Chin-Yee 2025`), LLMs overgeneralize **`4.85×`** more than human *NEJM Journal Watch* writers ($\text{OR} = 4.85, p < 0.001$), and prompting models to *"avoid inaccuracies"* nearly **doubles** overgeneralization ($\text{OR} = 1.90, p = 0.020$; lowering API temperature `0.7 → 0.0` cuts overgeneralization *odds* by `76%` across `400` abstract summaries, directly conflicting with `Miller 2024`'s $T = 0$ evaluation-variance warning). Factuality judges fail on subtle modern errors (`AggreFact` `ChatGPT-CoT 49.7%`, `TofuEval` `AlignScore 69.2% > GPT-4 64.9%`, `FIB` verbatim-copying trap), whereas `Claimify` (`Selection + Disambiguation + Decomposition`: `99.0%` entailment, `83.7%` element macro-F1) paired with compact NLI (`MiniCheck-FT5`: `74.7%` BAcc matching `GPT-4` `75.3%` at `~400×` lower cost) outperforms ad-hoc LLM judging. Meanwhile, `Kobak et al. (2025)` quantify massive lexical homogenization (`454` excess words, `379` style words across `15.1M` PubMed abstracts), and `Morkes & Nielsen`'s `+124%` concision gain explicitly excluded programmers, editors, and domain experts (bounded by `McNamara et al. 1996`'s reverse cohesion effect).

## Findings

### 1. Behavioral Evaluation Statistics, Power, Prompt Sensitivity, and Benchmark Integrity

`Basis: source + derived + execution`

#### 1.1 Variance Decomposition, Resampling Caps, $T = 0$ Pitfalls, and Exact `Miller (2024)` Audit

1. **Super-Population Variance & Resampling Diminishing Returns (`Miller 2024`, `arXiv:2411.00640v1` §§2–3, Table 4):**
   - Modeling per-question score $s_i = x_i + \epsilon_i$ (question-level expectation $x_i$ plus conditional sampling variance $\sigma_i^2$) with $K$ independent resamples per question gives $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\mu) = (\operatorname{Var}(x) + \mathbb{E}[\sigma_i^2]/K) / n$. Pooling all $KN$ draws as independent violates IID and produces inconsistent standard errors.
   - Under binary grading with uniform item difficulty $x \sim U[0, 1]$, $\operatorname{Var}(x) = 1/12$ and $\mathbb{E}[\sigma_i^2] = 1/6$, so $\operatorname{Var}(\hat\mu \mid K) = \operatorname{Var}(\hat\mu \mid K = 1) \cdot (1 + 2/K)/3$. Increasing $K = 1 \to 2$ cuts total variance by `1/3`, $K = 4$ by `1/2`, $K = 6$ by `5/9`, with a hard asymptotic cap of **`2/3`** (equivalent to continuous next-token logprobs, §3.2).
   - Ignoring question clustering underestimates standard errors by up to **`3.05×`** on real evaluations (Table 4): `DROP` (`SE_clust = 1.34` vs. `SE_CLT = 0.44`, `3.05×`), `MGSM` (`1.62%` vs. `0.86%`, `1.88×`), and `RACE-H` (`0.51%` vs. `0.46%`, `1.10×`).
2. **Temperature $T = 0$ Variance Inflation and Verified Arithmetic Errata (`Miller 2024` §3.3 p. 6, §4.2–5 pp. 8–9; `Probe 1c`, `[exact numerical / derived calculation]`):**
   - Lowering temperature to $T = 0$ deterministically rounds $x_{T=1}$ to $\mathbf{1}\{x_{T=1} > 0.5\}$:
     - For $x_{T=1} \sim U[0, 1]$, $x_{T=0} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ has $\operatorname{Var} = 1/4$, **tripling** minimum variance from $1/12$ (`3.0000×`; 25k MC `2.992×`).
     - For $x_{T=1} \sim U[1/3, 1]$, $x_{T=0} \sim \text{Bernoulli}(3/4)$ shifts $\mathbb{E}[x]$ from `2/3 → 3/4` (**`+8.33 pp` upward bias**) and inflates variance **`5.0625×`** (`1/27 → 3/16`; 25k MC `5.079×`). **Primary-source typo audit (p. 6 L281–284):** `Miller` prints `Bernoulli(p = 1/4)` on line 282 before immediately computing the correct $\mathbb{E} = 3/4$ and $\operatorname{Var} = 3/16$.
   - **Primary-source paired-variance erratum (pp. 8–9 L370–375, L450–474):** Setting $\operatorname{Var}(s_A) = \operatorname{Var}(s_B) = 1/12$ and $r = 0.5$ gives $\operatorname{Cov}(x_A, x_B) = 1/24$, so by Miller's own formula (Eq. 9–10) exact paired variance is $\omega^2 = 1/6 - 2(1/24) = \mathbf{1/12}$ (**`50.0%`** relative reduction), **not** Miller's printed `"1/3 in relative terms (that is, from 1/6 to 1/9)"` (which corresponds to $r = 1/3$). This error propagates into p. 9 Example 1 ($\delta = 3\text{ pp}, \sigma^2 = 0$: exact **$n = 726.75 \approx 727$** vs. printed `969.00 ≈ 969`) and Example 2 ($n = 198, K: 1 \to 10$: exact MDE **`12.85% → 6.80%`** vs. printed `13.27% → 7.57%`, rounded in text to `13.2% → 7.5%`).

#### 1.2 Small-Sample Breakdown of CLT & Bootstrap, `bayes_evals` Source Audit, and `Gamma(1,1)` Prior Conservatism

1. **Failure of Wald CLT and Bootstrap at $N \lesssim 100\text{–}300$ (`Bowyer et al. 2025`, `arXiv:2503.01747v3`; `Probe 1a(i)`, `[exact numerical / derived calculation]`):**
   - Across `80,000` simulated LLM eval datasets ($N \in \{3, 10, 30, 100\}$ IID/paired/$F_1$; $N \in \{10, 30, 100, 300\}$ clustered) and real small benchmarks (`AIME 2025` $N = 15$, `LangChain tool-use` $N = 20$, `MLE-Bench` $N = 75$), nominal 95% CLT CIs reach only **`92.5%`** coverage at $N = 100$ IID, collapse to zero width when $\hat\theta \in \{0, 1\}$, and escape $[0, 1]$ near boundaries. Nonparametric bootstrap ($K = 10{,}000$) also systematically under-covers and is marked `✗` in Table 1 for small-$N$ coverage and efficiency. Exact Binomial PMF evaluation (`Probe 1a(i)`) confirms catastrophic Wald boundary collapse vs. Wilson and `Beta(1+S, 1+N-S)` recovery: at $p = 0.05$, exact Wald coverage is **`22.51%`** ($N = 5$), **`40.02%`** ($N = 10$), **`63.89%`** ($N = 20$), **`87.75%`** ($N = 100$), and **`92.57%`** ($N = 300$), whereas Wilson and `Beta(1,1)` Bayes hold **`91.39%–97.74%`** across all six sample sizes. (Clopper–Pearson is conservative/wider; see `reusable-holdout-thresholdout-ladder-leaderboards-and-clopper-pearson-zero-numerators`.)
   - For non-linear metrics ($F_1$, odds ratios; §3.5 Fig. 6), both Delta-method and bootstrap intervals under-cover, whereas conjugate `Dirichlet(1 + N_conf)` credible intervals track nominal coverage.

#### 1.3 Statistical Power, Type-M Exaggeration, and Practical Sample-Size Thresholds

- **Underpowered NLP Benchmarks & Human Likert Studies (`Card et al. 2020`, `arXiv:2010.06595v1` Tables 2, §§4–5):** Across 119 GLUE comparisons (57 SOTA claims), only **`46%`** had $\ge 80\%$ predicted power (`WNLI` $n = 147$ paired McNemar MDE `+5.26 pp` vs. avg claimed `+1.72 pp`; `MRPC` $n = 1{,}725$ MDE `+1.62 pp` vs. `+0.63 pp`; MT $n = 2{,}000$ gives `~75%` power for `1.0` BLEU). Across 41 EMNLP 2019 human-eval papers, **`69%`** tested $\le 100$ items and `57%` used 3 raters/item — underpowered below a `0.20` effect on $[0, 1]$ under high rater variance; detecting `0.05` requires `10+` workers $\times$ `100` items.

#### 1.4 Spurious Format Sensitivity, Skill Ablations, and Agentic Benchmark Integrity

- **Non-Smooth Formatting Landscape (`FormatSpread`, `arXiv:2310.11324v2` §§4.2–4.3, Table 2):** Across 53 tasks ($N = 1{,}000$), 10 meaning-preserving formats produce a **`7.5 pp`** median spread and swings up to **`78.3 pp`** from removing two colons (`task280`, LLaMA-2-7B `0.043 → 0.826`). Crucially, atomic format triples $(p_1, p_2, p_3)$ are monotonic only **`32.4%–33.6%`** of the time (chance = `33.3%`), falsifying smooth hill-climbing assumptions over prompt formatting.

#### 2.1 Core Bias Magnitudes, Agreement Inflation, and Objective Judge Ceilings

1. **Order, Verbosity, Multi-Bias, and S1-vs.-S2 Agreement Traps (`MT-Bench` `2306.05685v4` Tables 2–6; `CALM` `2410.02736v2` Tables 4–5, §4.2; `FairEval` `2305.17926v2`):**

Even with explicit anti-order-bias instructions (`FairEval`), GPT-4 hits **`46.3%`** conflict (`37/80`, Vicuna win rate `51.3% → 23.8%`) and ChatGPT hits **`82.5%`** (`66/80`, `2.5% → 82.5%`).

Under **S1 (all votes including ties and position-inconsistent swaps, random = 33%)**, GPT-4 agreement falls to **`66%`** (MT-Bench) and **`64%`** (Chatbot Arena; `54%` GPT-3.5, `53%` Claude).

#### 2.3 Length/Style Regression Control, the Swap-and-Tie Variance-Reduction Trap, and Skewness Inversion

- **Observational Length & Style Control (`AlpacaEval-LC` `2404.04475v2` Table 1; `Arena Style Control`, LMSYS Aug 2024):**
  - `AlpacaEval-LC` fits a logistic GLM controlling $\tanh((\text{len}(z_m) - \text{len}(z_b))/\text{std})$ and instruction difficulty $\gamma_x$, evaluated at $\Delta\text{len} = 0$: cuts `gpt4_1106_preview` verbosity-prompt win-rate swing **`41.4 pp → 9.7 pp`** (`22.9%–64.3% → 41.9%–51.6%`), reduces concise/default/verbose SD **`26% → 10%`**, and lifts Spearman correlation with Chatbot Arena **`0.94 → 0.98`** ($p = 0.07$ vs. raw AlpacaEval). *Adversarial gaming caveat (Table 1):* truncating weak answers boosts unregularized LC by `+22.2` (`3.7 → 25.9`); L2 regularization caps gaming at `+8.5` (`12.2`), whereas stratified length-matching explodes by `+40.8`.
  - `Chatbot Arena Style Control` fits contextual Bradley-Terry regression over normalized differences in length and markdown formatting: standardized coefficients (`Control Both`) are **Length `0.249`** (Length-only `0.267`), **Markdown List `0.031`**, **Header `0.024`**, and **Bold `0.019`** — length carries `~8×` the weight of markdown. Controlling style drops `grok-2-mini` **`6 → 18`** and `gpt-4o-mini` `6 → 11` while lifting `claude-3-opus` **`16 → 10`** and `claude-3-5-sonnet` `6 → 4` (`2 → 1` tie on Hard Prompts). Both studies emphasize that observational regression cannot separate substance-correlated length (e.g., thorough CoT) from verbose fluff.
- **Swap-and-Tie Variance-Reduction Trap (`Probe 2a`, `[toy model / illustrative surrogate]`):** Under a linear-logit pairwise judge with position bias $\beta_{\text{pos}} = 1.10$, verbosity bias $\beta_{\text{len}} = 0.85$, and null quality lift ($\Delta Q = 0$, candidate verbosity shift $\mu_{\text{len}} = +0.80$, `acc = 0.75`, 300 trials; all `5,400` length-controlled GLM Newton-Raphson fits converged to `max|step| < 1e-10` in $\le 10$ iterations):
  - Single-order fixed explodes to **`83.3%`** (`N = 20`) and **`100.0%`** (`N = 50, 100`) false promotion.
  - Crucially, **`Swap+Tie` makes false promotion WORSE than `1-Ord Rand` (`65.0% vs. 40.7%` at $N = 50$; `90.0% vs. 66.0%` at $N = 100$; `93.3% vs. 64.7%` at `acc = 0.55`)** because cancelling position variance shrinks the denominator SE while leaving the systematic verbosity bias intact!
  - Adding length control (`Swap+LenGLM`, item-clustered sandwich SE) restores calibrated Type-I (**`5.00%`** at $N = 50$, **`3.67%`** at $N = 100$; `4.33%` at `acc = 0.55`) while preserving **`88.67%`** ($N = 50$) and **`99.67%`** ($N = 100$) power under true lift ($\Delta Q = +1.0$). Under position-bias-only ablation ($\beta_{\text{len}} = 0$), `Swap+Tie` is calibrated (`3.67%–4.33%`); under unbiased negative control, all four protocols sit near `~5%`. *Surrogate caveat:* Probe 2a fits the exact unconfounded linear-logit DGP ($\Delta L \perp Q$), validating identifiability under correct specification rather than causal separation under length–quality confounding.
- **Right-Tail Skewness Inversion Mechanism (`Probe 2b`, `[toy model / illustrative skewness mechanism]`):** When 75% of judge draws slightly favor concise $A$ (`+0.45`, SD `0.60`) while 25% suffer right-tail bias spikes boosting verbose $B$ (`+3.20`), $\operatorname{median}(B - A) < 0 < \mathbb{E}[B - A] = +0.35$, mechanically inverting aggregation order (`Single 52.84%`, **`maj@5 55.36%`** [Condorcet `55.31%`], **`mean@5 34.02%`**, **`best-of-5 19.12%`**), whereas under symmetric Gaussian noise (`0%` spikes) `mean@5` wins (`88.72%` vs. `maj@5 84.64%`, `best-of-5 78.98%`). This illustrates the pure skewness mechanism by which extreme outlier draws poison mean and max aggregation; it is **not** a causal reproduction of `Gu et al.` Table 4 (which confounded pairwise `maj@5` with pointwise `mean@5` on $n = 34$ items).

### 3. Automated Code Review, Complex Coding Benchmarks, Verification Asymmetry, and Wild Maintainability

`Basis: source + derived + execution`

#### 3.1 Industrial Automated Code Review: Precision Calibration vs. Review-Time Null

1. **Google `AutoCommenter` (`arXiv:2405.13565v1`, DOI `10.1145/3664646.3665664`):**
   - Deployed across C++, Java, Python, and Go (3k early adopters → 50% A/B → GA): initial global threshold $t = 0.98$ missed `~80%` of recall and per-language thresholds failed due to URL score skew, necessitating **per-URL calibration**. Greedy decoding placed **`80%`** of comments on unchanged lines (ignored by authors); restricting to changed lines dropped file coverage `6% → 1.3%`, which **beam search ($n = 4$)** tripled to **`3.9%`** (2 s median latency — viable for async review, too slow for IDE) while cutting top-10 URL concentration `80% → 41%`.
   - Early usefulness plateaued at **`54%`** (developer clicks) / **`60%`** (15 independent raters on ~370 comments) due to multi-topic docs, subjective rules, stale guidelines, and technically valid low-value nits. Suppressing **`17 + 5`** non-actionable URLs and rewriting summaries lifted historical usefulness to `66%` (devs) / `74%` (raters), crossing **`>80%`** post-deployment.
   - Only `10%` of comments receive explicit clicks, yet AST line-mapping across `6,000` snapshot pairs + manual audit of `40` pairs (`80%` direct resolution of `50%` disappeared comments) establishes **`~40%` actual code-change resolution**. AutoCommenter spans `330` URLs covering **`68%`** of historical human best-practice URLs, and **`33/50` (`66%`)** of top-50 practices lie beyond easy traditional linters (Fig. 6). Critically, the **50% randomized A/B test (§4.4) detected NO statistically significant reduction** in total code review duration, active review time, or comment-response iterations. *(Compare human defect-detection rates in `fagan-inspection-rates-perspective-based-reading-and-formal-notation-comprehension-trials`.)*
2. **Google `DIDACT` (`10.1145/3639477.3639746`, abstract only) & `CodeReviewer` (`2203.09095v2`):**
   - At Google, authors spend ~`60 min` active shepherding per change; moving from async edit generation to interactive reviewer-time suggested edits led authors to resolve **`7.5%` of all reviewer comments** by directly applying ML-suggested edits (`DIDACT`).
   - `CodeReviewer` (9 languages) achieves `78.60%` precision / `65.63%` recall / `71.53` F1 on change-quality estimation (Table 7), `30.32%` Exact Match on code refinement (Table 6), and only **`5.32` BLEU** on review-comment generation despite `3.60/5` informativeness and `3.20/5` relevance in human evaluation (Table 5), demonstrating that n-gram metrics severely mismeasure free-form review text.

#### 3.2 Beyond Single-File Python: Test Generation, Refactoring, Polyglot Systems, and Feature Engineering

- **`SWT-Bench` (`arXiv:2406.12952v3`, `SWT-Bench-Lite`, 276 valid instances; 311/2,294 full SWE-bench instances had broken/flaky golden tests):**
  - Custom function-level diffs (`ZeroShotPlus`) lift patch well-formedness `48.6% → 89.5%` and fail-to-pass test success `3.6% → 9.4%` (Table 2); `SWE-Agent+` (execute-before-submit) reaches **`18.5%`** (`69.4%` change coverage), beating test-specific `LIBRO` (`14.1%`). Providing the target test file nearly doubles success (`8.1% → 15.1%`), beating even providing the golden code patch (`10.5%`, Table 5).
  - **Orthogonality & Precision Doubling (§5.3, Table 6):** Instances solved on `SWT-Bench` vs. `SWE-Bench` are **statistically uncorrelated** (`SWE-Agent`: `44` SWT, `50` SWE, overlap `7`, **`p = 0.728`**; `ZeroShotPlus`: `26` SWT, `16` SWE, overlap `1`, **`p = 0.804`**). Filtering `SWE-Agent` fixes through `SWE-Agent`-generated reproducing tests achieves only `20%` recall but **more than doubles patch precision to `47.8%`** (up from `50/276 = 18.12%`).
- **Exact Likelihood-Ratio Crossover Theorem vs. Illustrative Coding-Filter Surrogate (`Probe 3a` `[exact numerical / derived calculation]` & `Probe 3b` `[toy model / illustrative surrogate mapping]`):**
  - *Exact theorem (`Probe 3a`):* For any binary patch filter with $(\text{TPR}, \text{FPR})$ applied to a stream of base rate $\pi \in (0, 1)$, post-filter precision is $\text{PPV}(\pi) = [1 + \frac{1-\pi}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{\text{LR}}]^{-1}$, strictly increasing in $\text{LR} = \text{TPR}/\text{FPR}$ independently of $\pi$. At `SWT-Bench`'s exact parameters ($\pi = 50/276 = 18.12\%, \text{TPR} = 0.20, \text{PPV} = 0.478$), exact implied false-pass rate is **`FPR = 4.832%` (`LR = 4.139`)**. Consequently, a `20%`-recall test filter beats any competing verifier with $\text{LR} = 27/15 = 1.8000$ at **every** base rate $\pi \in (0, 1)$ iff **`FPR_test < 11.11%`** (exact `1/9`), and beats $\text{LR} = 25/17 = 1.4706$ iff **`FPR_test < 13.60%`**.
  - *Illustrative surrogate plug-in (`Probe 3b`, 100k-patch MC verified within `0.49 pp`):* Mapping non-reasoning `JudgeBench` Coding accuracies (`59.52%` GPT-4o, `64.29%` Claude-3.5) as pointwise `(TPR = acc, FPR = 1 - acc)` yields `PPV = 24.5%` / `28.5%` at $\pi = 18.12\%$ vs. `47.8%` for `SWT-Bench` and `68.9%` for curated high-specificity tests (`FPR = 2%`), whereas `UTBoost`-style weak tests (`FPR = 28.4%`) *degrade* precision below unfiltered baseline at `20%` recall (`13.5% < 18.1%`, `LR = 0.70`) and still trail `SWT-Bench` even at `80%` recall (`38.4%`, `LR = 2.82`). **Mandatory surrogate boundary:** This plug-in is an illustrative mapping only — `JudgeBench` is pairwise 2AFC (scoring swap-inconsistency/ties as `0`, so `1 - acc` overstates pointwise FPR under reject-on-conflict), covers `N = 42` GPT-4o-filtered problems, and excludes reasoning judges (`92.86%–100.0%`).

### 4. Prose Editing Pathologies, Caveat Preservation, Factuality Detection, and Technical Comprehension

`Basis: source + derived`

#### 4.1 Scientific Overgeneralization and the Accuracy-Prompt Backfire (`Peters & Chin-Yee 2025`)

- **Core Effect (`arXiv:2504.00025v1`, *Royal Society Open Science* 12(4), DOI `10.1098/rsos.241776`, 10 LLMs, `4,900` summaries):** Coding three qualifier-stripping transitions (quantified/scoped → bare generics, past → present tense, descriptive → action-guiding; cf. pragmatic exhaustification in `implicature-safe-summaries-conditional-perfection-and-exhaustification-of-formal-claims`):
  - Across `100` full clinical articles benchmarked against human expert *NEJM Journal Watch* summaries (Table 1), human summaries did **not** significantly overgeneralize ($B = 0.297, p = 0.309$), whereas LLM summaries were **`6.72×`** more likely to overgeneralize than source articles ($\text{OR} = 6.72$, `95% CI [4.22, 10.71]`, $p < 0.001$) and **`4.85×`** more likely than human *NEJM JW* summaries ($\text{OR} = 4.85$, `95% CI [3.06, 7.70]`, $p < 0.001$).
  - Across abstract summaries, newer non-Claude models worsened dramatically vs. older checkpoints: `GPT-4 Turbo` / `LLaMA 2 70B` `~2.6×` (`20.5%–21.5%` specific overgeneralization) vs. `ChatGPT-4o` `~9×` (`48%–61%`), `DeepSeek` (`29%–67%`), and `LLaMA 3.3 70B` `~39×` (`57%–73%`).
  - **"Avoid inaccuracies" prompt backfire (Table 1, §2 RQ2):** Explicitly prompting LLMs to avoid inaccuracies **nearly doubled** generalized conclusions overall ($\text{OR} = 1.90$, `95% CI [1.11, 3.26]`, $p = 0.020$; e.g., `DeepSeek 29% → 67%`, `ChatGPT-4o 48% → 61%`, `LLaMA 3.3 71% → 73%`, `GPT-4 Turbo 20.5% → 23.5%`; step-by-step prompt null, $p = 0.587$).
- **Three Mandatory Scope Narrowings Verified in Primary Text:**
  1. **`Claude 3.7 Sonnet` is borderline/underpowered, NOT a proven null:** While `Claude 2` ($p = 0.685$) and `Claude 3.5 Sonnet` ($p = 0.587$) showed no significant overgeneralization, `Claude 3.7 Sonnet` reached $B = +0.824$ (**$\text{OR} = 2.28, p = 0.064$** at $N = 100$) with **`22%`** specific overgeneralization vs. `2%` undergeneralization (`OAO = +20%`, Tables 2 & 5) — borderline/underpowered rather than evidence of zero bias.
  2. **Claude coverage gaps across sub-experiments:** **Zero Claude models** were evaluated in the *NEJM JW* full-article comparison (L579–587: only `GPT-4 Turbo`, `ChatGPT-4o`, `DeepSeek`), and **only `Claude 3.5 Sonnet`** (`14% → 8%`, Table 5) — not Claude 2 or 3.7 — was tested under the Accuracy prompt.
  3. **`-76%` at $T = 0$ is an odds reduction on `400` API abstracts:** Lowering `T = 0.7 → 0.0` yielded $B = -1.432, p < 0.001$, i.e., a **`76%` reduction in odds** ($\exp(-1.432) = 0.239$, not a `-76 pp` rate drop) estimated on **`400` API abstract summaries** (L213–214), excluding web-UI models (`ChatGPT-4o`, `DeepSeek`, `Claude 3.5/3.7`). Operationally, this creates an explicit temperature tension against `Miller (2024)` §3.3: $T = 0$ can preserve caveats at inference time while biasing/inflating variance if substituted into accuracy evals.

#### 4.2 Atomic Factuality, Detector Staleness, Verbatim Traps, and Claim/Qualifier Decomposition

- **Atomic Precision & Zero Citation Reliability (`FActScore`, `2305.14251v2` Tables 1 & 3, Fig. 2):** Human-verified atomic precision across 183 biographies (`26–41` facts/bio) is only `42.5%` (`InstructGPT`), `58.3%` (`ChatGPT`), and `71.5%` (search-augmented `PerplexityAI`, still `11.1%` unsupported + `14.8%` irrelevant), degrading sharply for rarer entities (`-50%` atomic / `-64%` sentence-level) and later in generation. Inline citations carry **zero reliability signal** (`36.0%` of supported vs. `37.6%` of unsupported `PerplexityAI` sentences include citations). Uncalibrated `Retrieve→LM` overestimates `ChatGPT` by `+6.8 pp` and `Inst-LLaMA` by `+17.1 pp`.
- **Detector Staleness vs. Compact NLI (`AggreFact` `2205.12854v2` Tables 3–4; `MiniCheck` `2404.10774v2` Tables 2–3; `TofuEval` `2402.13249v2` Tables 2–3):**
  - Factuality detectors accurate on pre-Transformer errors (`QAFactEval 80.3%`) degrade sharply on modern fine-tuned SOTA (`FTSOTA`: `QAFactEval 61.6%`, `ChatGPT-CoT 49.7%`, `ChatGPT-DA 48.0%` — at/below chance).
  - On `TofuEval` dialogue summaries (`30.4%–46.0%` summary-level hallucinations, introducing tense/modality shifts and stating opinions as facts), non-LLM `AlignScore` (**`69.2%`** BAcc) beats `GPT-4` (`64.9%`) and `GPT-3.5-Turbo` (`61.6%`) on main-topic `MediaSum`, while 13B–33B open judges sit near chance (`49.6%–59.8%`).
  - On the 10-dataset `LLM-AggreFact` benchmark without per-dataset threshold tuning, 770M-parameter **`MiniCheck-FT5` achieves `74.7%` BAcc — matching `GPT-4` (`75.3%`) and beating `Claude-3 Opus` (`74.1%`) and `GPT-3.5` (`69.6%`) at `~400×` lower inference cost**.
- **Context-Conflict Blindness & Verbatim-Copying Trap (`HaluEval` `2305.11747v3` Table 5; `FIB` `2211.08412v2` §§1, 4):** `ChatGPT` detects summarization hallucinations at only **`58.53%`** (`Claude 2 57.75%`, `Llama-2-7B 49.55%`), primarily failing when world-plausible statements contradict the grounding document (`HaluEval`). On `FIB`, LLMs prefer consistent summaries ~`80%` against abstractive hallucinations, **but fail catastrophically when an inconsistent summary copies source text verbatim** (e.g., `BLOOM` picks the consistent summary only **`9.6%`** of the time on extractive CNN/DM distractors).
- **Qualifier-Preserving Decomposition (`Claimify`, `2502.10855v2` Tables 1–4):** Naive atomic decomposition drops caveats or resolves genuinely ambiguous text arbitrarily. Splitting extraction into **Selection** (filtering verifiable content), **Disambiguation** (preserving or abstaining on unresolved ambiguity), and **Decomposition** yields **`99.0%` entailment** (vs. `89.1%` DnD, `96.6%` SAFE), **`91.2%` sentence / `83.7%` element macro-F1** (vs. `41.4%/56.2%` DnD, `45.1%/57.3%` SAFE, `78.9%/62.5%` VeriScore), and `80.6%` desirable verdicts. Ablating Selection collapses element macro-F1 **`83.7% → 54.4%`**; ablating Disambiguation drops it to **`75.9%`**.

#### 4.3 Lexical Homogenization and the Expertise Reversal Boundary on Concise Prose

- **Excess Style Vocabulary at Scale (`Kobak et al. 2025`, `arXiv:2406.07016v5`, *Science Advances* 11(27)):** Across **`15.1 million`** English PubMed abstracts (2010–2024), 2024 exhibits **`454` excess words** (`343` unique lemmas, vs. `190` content-dominated words during peak COVID-19 in 2021). **`379 / 454`** are **style words** (`66%` verbs, `14%` adjectives: `delves` $r = 28.0$, `underscores` $r = 13.8$, `showcasing` $r = 10.7$; frequency gaps `potential` $\delta = 0.052$, `findings` $0.041$, `crucial` $0.037$), yielding a conservative **`≥13.5%`** lower bound on LLM-processed 2024 abstracts (up to **`41%`** in China computation and `34%` in Korea Sensors).
- **Concision Gains vs. Expert Comprehension Reversal (`Morkes & Nielsen` 1997/1998; `McNamara et al. 1996`, DOI `10.1207/s1532690xci1401_1`):**
  - `Morkes & Nielsen` (Study 3 between-subjects $N = 51$, ~10/cell, 7-page "Travel Nebraska" site) reported `79%` scanning and geometric-mean usability gains of `+47%` (scannable), `+58%` (concise, ~50% shorter), `+27%` (objective), and **`+124%`** (combined; `+159%` follow-up). **Primary-source audit caveats:** Study 3 used one-tailed uncorrected $t$-tests at ~$n = 10$/cell, **explicitly excluded programmers, CS professionals, writers, and editors**, screened out anyone with prior domain knowledge, and found **zero statistically significant improvement on objective task time, errors, or memory for the `objective` condition alone**.
  - Complementarily, `McNamara et al. (1996)` *(paywalled; standard directional finding from secondary academic indices)* established the **reverse cohesion effect**: high-coherence explicit text aids **low-knowledge** readers, whereas **high-knowledge** readers construct deeper situation models from denser, lower-coherence prose that demands active inference — directly bounding blanket "always simplify/flatten" prose-editing heuristics for expert engineering documentation.
