# Infix Operator Precedence Misunderstandings, Partial-Order Precedence Lattices, and UAX #31 / UTS #39 Joiner Compatibility

## Summary

Empirical comprehension trials confirm that C-family total-order infix precedence induces substantial human misreading on specific non-arithmetic operator combinations, not uniformly across all expressions. In Derek M. Jones's controlled study of 17 experienced C/C++ developers (mean 14.6 years experience; 2,100 paired judgments), overall accuracy on unparenthesized binary operator pairs was roughly 67% (33% error rate against a 50% random-guess baseline), with catastrophic failure concentrated on bitwise-versus-equality (`|` vs `!=`: 0% correct; `^` vs `!=`: 8%; `&` vs `!=`: 11%; `^` vs `==`: 12%; `|` vs `==`: 22%; `&` vs `==`: 27%), modulo (`%`: 34.88% overall), bitwise XOR (`^`: 47.64% overall due to exponentiation interference), bitwise-versus-shift (`&` vs `<<`: 40%), and logical `||` vs `&&` (50%, indistinguishable from chance), whereas multiplicative-versus-equality and additive-versus-equality reached 100% accuracy. Gopstein et al. (ESEC/FSE 2017, $n=73$) measured a 14 percentage-point accuracy drop ($\phi = 0.33$, $p = 5.90 \times 10^{-5}$) for unparenthesized infix operator precedence (`0 && 1 || 2`) and a 36 percentage-point drop ($\phi = 0.53$, $p = 1.77 \times 10^{-7}$) for macro operator precedence, while MSR 2018 repository mining across 14 C/C++ projects found Operator Precedence to be the second most prevalent atom of confusion overall.

Replacing a total order with a targeted partial order captures the empirically hazardous pairs at low honest-code rejection cost, whereas banning all mixed infix operators incurs an order-of-magnitude higher syntactic tax. Evaluating the exact implemented precedence relations of `carbon-lang` (`534197a3cd35502c1e4e5c159ea2c56fc5818882`, transitively closed 24-level partial order), `rhombus` (`afef550706d41214775c630b86d63eedcda2e90f`, non-transitive pairwise relation with named `order` classes), and `ponyc` (`edace5b6997042571088b9aa2cb0034b1e1384f7`, flat same-token-only infix rule) against 534 non-test modules (278,484 LoC; 5,379 unparenthesized nested operator pairs) of the CPython 3.13.15 standard library yields rejection rates of **7.06%** (`373/5,281`; **3.92%** excluding Python-specific chained comparisons) for Carbon and **6.08%** (`327/5,379`) for Rhombus, versus **56.43%** (`2,980/5,281`, or **73.63%** of distinct-operator pairs) for Pony. Across 15 pure C files (20,366 LoC; 3,179 condition/return expressions; 406 unparenthesized nested operator pairs) from `ponyc` itself—code already written under modern `-Wparentheses` discipline—Carbon and Rhombus reject **0.00%** (`0/406`), whereas Pony's flat rule still rejects **57.14%** (`232/406`), 87.1% of which are routine `a == b || c == d` and `a != b && c != d` expressions.

On Unicode identifier security, **Unicode 15.1–18.0.0** materially changed the default boundary between usability and spoofing. Normative inspection of UAX #31 Revision 45 (Unicode 18.0.0) and execution against `dtolnay/unicode-ident` (`28018f288934fa6be60a6e791e071b7a7195643e`, Unicode 18.0.0 tables, 10,368 B static storage) establish three critical facts: (1) `UAX31-R1a` (*Restricted Format Characters*) was withdrawn in Unicode 15.1, while `UAX31-R1b` governs *Stable Identifiers* (monotonicity across Unicode versions), not joiner rules; (2) `U+200C` (`ZWNJ`) and `U+200D` (`ZWJ`) are now members of `XID_Continue` (`Cf ∩ XID_Continue = {U+200C, U+200D}`), so lexers calling `unicode_ident::is_xid_continue` without an exclusion or security profile silently accept invisible `ZWNJ`/`ZWJ` inside Latin identifiers (`admin<ZWNJ>user`), even though all 12 `Bidi_Control` code points remain disjoint from `XID_Start`/`XID_Continue`; (3) a blanket ban on `General_Category=Cf` breaks mandatory modern orthography in Persian (`نامه‌ای` vs `نامهای`), Malayalam (`ദൃക്‌സാക്ഷി`), Sinhala (`ශ්‍රීලංකා`, `ක්‍ර`), Devanagari, and Kannada, whereas UTS #39 Revision 34 §3.1.1.1 Contexts A1, A2, and B paired with §5.1 Restriction Levels accept all six orthographic specimens while rejecting every invisible-joiner, mixed-script, and Trojan Source bidi attack. However, UTS #39 §4 `internalSkeleton(X)` strips all `Default_Ignorable_Code_Point` characters (including `ZWNJ` and `ZWJ`), creating an irreducible collision between strict skeleton injectivity and Persian/Indic orthographic minimal pairs. `unicode-org/icu4x` (`e1cad3416d4d0b356158942cf76f23ea91cec9ca`) supplies every underlying UCD property map needed for Contexts A1/A2/B and Restriction Levels (`XidStart`, `XidContinue`, `JoinControl`, `JoiningType`, `CanonicalCombiningClass`, `IndicSyllabicCategory`, `ScriptWithExtensions`, `BidiControl`, `DefaultIgnorableCodePoint`), but does not ship UTS #39 `Identifier_Status`, `Identifier_Type`, or `confusables.txt` `skeleton()` APIs.

## Applicability

These findings govern the design, parsing, lexing, diagnostics, and verification of programming-language frontends and static analyzers that balance human intent preservation against syntactic ergonomics and internationalization.

- **Upstream systems and their relationships:**
  - `carbon-language/carbon-lang` (`534197a3cd35502c1e4e5c159ea2c56fc5818882`), `racket/rhombus` (`afef550706d41214775c630b86d63eedcda2e90f`), and `ponylang/ponyc` (`edace5b6997042571088b9aa2cb0034b1e1384f7`) are compared directly as three distinct points on the infix-precedence design spectrum: fixed transitively closed partial order (`Carbon`), extensible non-transitive local pairwise order (`Rhombus`), and flat same-token-only disallowance (`Pony`). Carbon's pattern-matching specification (`docs/design/pattern_matching.md`) and Pony's `casepattern`/`local` grammar (`src/libponyc/ast/parser.c`) are also compared on explicit variable-binding markers.
  - `dtolnay/unicode-ident` (`28018f288934fa6be60a6e791e071b7a7195643e`) and `unicode-org/icu4x` (`e1cad3416d4d0b356158942cf76f23ea91cec9ca`) are interpreted through the normative requirements of Unicode 18.0.0 `UAX #31` (Revision 45) and `UTS #39` (Revision 34), together with official Unicode 18.0.0 UCD (`DerivedCoreProperties.txt`, `DerivedGeneralCategory.txt`, `DerivedCombiningClass.txt`, `DerivedJoiningType.txt`, `IndicSyllabicCategory.txt`, `Scripts.txt`, `ScriptExtensions.txt`) and UTS #39 security data (`IdentifierStatus.txt`, `IdentifierType.txt`, `confusables.txt`).
  - Published human-subject and repository-mining studies (Jones 2006; Gopstein et al. ESEC/FSE 2017 and MSR 2018; Medeiros et al. EMSE 2018; Langhout & Aniche ICPC 2021; Boucher & Anderson USENIX Security 2023) anchor empirical comprehension and security claims.
- **Exact observations vs. generalization boundaries:**
  - Corpus rejection measurements were executed on (a) all 534 non-test modules of CPython 3.13.15 (`/usr/lib/python3.13`, 278,484 LoC) with exact AST and source-slice parenthesization verification, and (b) 15 pure C compiler source files from `ponylang/ponyc` (`edace5b6997042571088b9aa2cb0034b1e1384f7`, 20,366 LoC) for `if`/`while`/`return` expressions. Results generalize directionally to procedural/systems codebases written under similar warning flags, but exact percentages depend on language idioms (e.g., Python's chained comparisons and low bitwise density versus kernel C or HDL code).
  - Unicode 18.0.0 counts and joiner-context outcomes apply strictly to Unicode 15.1–18.0.0 (`U+200C`/`U+200D` in `XID_Continue`); implementations pinned to Unicode $\le 15.0$ exhibit `Cf ∩ XID_Continue = ∅` unless they explicitly opted into the withdrawn `UAX31-R1a` profile.

#### 1.3 Real-World Prevalence, Defect Correlation, and Developer Perception

- **Expression arity distribution (Jones 2006, Table 1; 890,423 binary operators, 309,150 operator pairs in C code):** **92.82%** of full expressions contain `0` binary operators, **5.28%** contain `1`, and only **1.90%** (`0.86% + 0.62% + 0.14% + 0.13% + ...`) contain $\ge 2$ binary operators. Thus, operator-precedence disambiguation rules affect a narrow syntactic slice of total expressions while governing a disproportionate share of silent logic bugs.
- **Wild prevalence & bug correlation (Gopstein et al. MSR 2018; 14 C/C++ projects from 187 KLOC Nginx to 22,641 KLOC Linux):** Atoms of confusion occur across **4.38% of source lines** (1 in 23 lines; 1.34% of AST nodes; >3.6 million instances). **Operator Precedence** is the **second most prevalent atom** (trailing only Omitted Curly Brace), appearing at roughly **0.0041 per AST node** across the corpus. Bug-fix commits in GCC are **1.25×** more likely to remove atoms of confusion than non-bug-fix commits, atoms are **1.13×** more likely to carry explanatory comments, and project-level atom rates correlate with vulnerability density and negatively with project creation year ($r = -0.65$).
- **Developer survey & refactoring friction (Medeiros et al. EMSE 2018; 50 C projects, 97 active-developer survey responses, 35 submitted PRs):** Operator Precedence occurred in **54%** of projects (0.27 instances/KLOC under their strict `&&`/`||` SrcML detector). Over **60%** of surveyed developers agreed Operator Precedence harms comprehension and discouraged its use; yet when researchers submitted 35 cleanup PRs across patterns, maintainers accepted only **8 of 21** reviewed PRs (**38%**), illustrating high social friction against *post-hoc* parenthesization churn once ambiguous syntax is permitted by the language.

### 2. Comparative Architecture of Partial-Order Precedence Engines: Carbon, Rhombus, and Pony

Basis: source (`carbon-language/carbon-lang@534197a3cd35502c1e4e5c159ea2c56fc5818882`, `racket/rhombus@afef550706d41214775c630b86d63eedcda2e90f`, `ponylang/ponyc@edace5b6997042571088b9aa2cb0034b1e1384f7`) + documentation (Flatt et al. OOPSLA 2023).

The three upstream toolchains implement fundamentally different mathematical relations over operators:

| Dimension | Carbon (`534197a3`) | Rhombus (`afef5507`) | Pony (`edace5b6`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mathematical relation** | **Transitively closed strict partial order** over 24 equivalence classes (`Highest`, 22 operator levels, `Lowest`) + diagonal associativity | **Non-transitive local pairwise relation** (`'stronger`, `'weaker`, `'same`, `'same-on-left`, `'same-on-right`, `'default`) over operators and named `order` classes | **Discrete equivalence (antichain)** across all 35 binary infix tokens (`TK_AND`..`TK_ISECTTYPE`); only `op == op` chains |
| **Core source implementation** | `toolchain/parse/precedence.h` (L16–148), `precedence.cpp` (L14–291), `handle_expr.cpp` (L20–65, L356–446) | `enforest-lib/enforest/operator.rkt` (L56–181), `enforest-lib/enforest/main.rkt` (L62–66, L345–390), `rhombus-lib/rhombus/private/amalgam/order-primitive.rkt` (L27–217) | `src/libponyc/ast/parser.c` (L37–58, L1061–1116), `src/libponyc/pass/syntax.c` (L102–146, L721–746) |
| **Compile-time / parse-time lookup** | `static constexpr OperatorPriorityTable Lookup` (`24×24` `int8_t` = **576 B** in `.rodata`); $O(1)$ array index; fixed-point closure computed at compiler build time (~0.5 s comment at `precedence.cpp:69`) | Continuation-based Pratt/Honu enforestation; runtime macro-expansion alist lookup via `free-identifier=?` in `relative-precedence` (`operator.rkt:115–180`); detects `'inconsistent-prec` and `'inconsistent-assoc` | Single-level left-associative `SEQ` in `parser.c:1095`, then post-parse AST pass `syntax_infix_expr` checking `(left_op != op) && is_expr_infix(left_op) && !ast_checkflag(left, AST_FLAG_IN_PARENS)` |
| **Bitwise (`& \| ^ << >>`) vs. Comparison (`== <`)** | **Ordered**: bitwise **>** `Relational` (fixes C's `a & b == c` bug!) | **Ordered**: `bitwise_*` **>** `equivalence`, `order_comparison` (fixes C bug!) | **Rejected** (`"Operator precedence is not supported. Parentheses required."`) |
| **Bitwise vs. Arithmetic (`+ - * /`)** | **Incomparable** (error) | **Incomparable** (error) | **Rejected** |
| **Bitwise among themselves (`&` vs `\|` vs `^` vs `<<`)** | **Mutually incomparable** (error); `<<`/`>>` non-associative | **Ordered chain**: `~` > `<< >>` > `bits.and` > `bits.or`/`bits.xor` > `bitwise_test` | **Rejected** unless identical token |

| **Logical `and` (`&&`) vs. `or` (`\|\|`)** | **Incomparable** (rejects Gopstein's `0 && 1 \|\| 2`) | **Ordered**: `logical_conjunction` **>** `logical_disjunction` (accepts `0 && 1 \|\| 2`) | **Rejected** |
| **Logical `not` (`!`) vs. Comparison (`==`, `<`)** | **Incomparable** (`not a == b` and `(not a) == b` without parens rejected) | **Partial**: `logical_negation` **>** `equivalence` (`!a == b` parses as `(!a) == b`), **incomparable** with `order_comparison` (`!a < b` rejected!) | Prefix strictly **>** all infix (`not a == b` parses as `(not a) == b`) |
| **Chained comparisons (`a < b < c`, `a == b == c`)** | **Rejected** (`Relational` non-associative) | **Rejected** (`equivalence` and `order_comparison` both `'none` assoc and mutually incomparable) | **Syntactically accepted** if identical token (`a == b == c` passes `syntax_infix_expr` because `left_op == op`!) |
| **Cast (`as`) vs. arithmetic/comparison** | `As` **incomparable** with arithmetic, bitwise, relational, and logical (`a + b as T` rejected) | N/A (annotations use `::` / `:#`) | `TK_AS` is **absent** from `is_expr_infix` (`syntax.c:102–146`), parsed left-to-right in `infix` `SEQ` (`parser.c:1095`), so `a + b as I32` parses silently as `(a + b) as I32` |

#### 2.1 Primary-Source Subtleties and Specification-Implementation Discrepancies

1. **Carbon documentation vs. implementation discrepancy (`docs/design/expressions/README.md:209` vs. `toolchain/parse/precedence.cpp:25–33`):** Basis: source + documentation. The prose example in `docs/design/expressions/README.md` line 209 states *"For example, `or --> == --> as` means that `or` is lower precedence than `as`."* However, both the Mermaid Hasse diagram in the same file (lines 176, 183, 191: `as -----> unary`, `logicalExpression --> as & where & and & or`) and the actual C++ table (`precedence.cpp:25–33`: `MarkHigherThan({NumericPrefix, BitwisePrefix, TypePostfix}, {As, ...})` and `MarkHigherThan({As, LogicalAnd, LogicalOr, Where}, {Ref})`) make `As` **incomparable** with both `Relational` (`==`) and `LogicalOr` (`or`). Any downstream formalization must follow `precedence.cpp`, not the stale prose example.
2. **Rhombus non-transitivity avoids global closure fragility (`enforest-lib/enforest/main.rkt:62–66`, `operator.rkt:115–180`):** Basis: source. Because Rhombus macros can introduce domain-specific infix operators independently across modules, requiring a global transitively closed DAG could silently break module composition when two unrelated libraries induce a cycle or unintended transitive chain. Instead, `relative-precedence` inspects only the direct declarations on `left-op` and `op` (and their optional named `order` structs), returning `'inconsistent-prec` if they contradict and `#f` if neither declares an edge. An important consequence visible in `order-primitive.rkt:141–211`: even though `bitwise_conjunction > equivalence` and `equivalence > logical_conjunction`, `bitwise_conjunction` and `logical_conjunction` have **no direct edge**, so `a bits.and b && c` is rejected while `a bits.and b == 0 && c` is accepted (each adjacent reduction step compares an explicitly ordered pair).
3. **Carbon's Pratt-parser soundness theorem for partial orders (`proposals/p000555-operator-precedence.md:179–186`, `handle_expr.cpp:361–389`):** Basis: source + derived. In a transitively closed partial order, encountering `a $ b @ c` with `$` incomparable to `@` is *locally decidable* with zero lookahead: in any valid parse tree, one of `$` or `@` would be an ancestor of the other, and because precedences strictly increase along every root-to-leaf path, transitivity would force `$` and `@` to be comparable—contradiction. Thus `HandleExprLoop` (`handle_expr.cpp:361–389`) detects ambiguity in $O(1)$ time with two table lookups (`GetPriority(ambient, op)` and `GetPriority(lhs, op)`), emitting `"parentheses are required to disambiguate operator precedence"` and setting `state.has_error = true` while recovering cleanly.

#### 3.1 Root-Cause Breakdown of Corpus Rejections

- **Why Carbon rejects 7.06% of Python stdlib pairs (and 0.00% of `-Wall`-clean C conditions):** In Python stdlib, Carbon's 373 rejections concentrate sharply in five buckets: (1) Python-specific chained comparisons (`<=`/`<=`: 99, `<`/`<=`: 41, `==`/`==`: 11, `<`/`<`: 9 — **42.9%** of all Carbon rejections); (2) `or` mixed with `and` (`39` left + `30` right = **69**, **18.5%** — directly targeting Jones's 50%-accuracy pair and Gopstein's `0 && 1 || 2` atom); (3) Python's low-precedence `not` applied to comparisons/bitwise (`not a <= b`: 37, `not a & b`: 14, `not a == b`: 12, `not a < b`: 7, `not a >= b`: 4 — **74**, **19.8%**); (4) `+` mixed with `%` (`28`, **7.5%** — directly targeting Jones's 34.88%-accuracy `%` operator); and (5) `|` mixed with `<<` (`12`, **3.2%**). In `ponyc`'s C condition/return expressions—already authored under GCC/Clang `-Wparentheses`—authors already parenthesized every `&&`/`||` and bitwise/comparison combination, dropping Carbon and Rhombus friction to **0.00%**.
- **Why Rhombus rejects 6.08% on Python stdlib:** Rhombus accepts `or`+`and` (69 pairs) and `+`+`%` (28 pairs), saving 97 rejections relative to Carbon, but rejects `and`+`&` (`32` pairs due to non-transitivity between `bitwise_conjunction` and `logical_conjunction`) and `a ** -b` (`22` pairs because `arithmetic.rkt:99` assigns unary `-` to `multiplication`, weaker than `exponentiation`).
- **Why Pony's flat rule imposes an 8–10× higher tax (56.4–57.1% of pairs):** Pony rejects universal, 94–100%-understood idioms: in Python stdlib, `and`/`or` over `==`/`!=`/`<`/`>` accounts for >1,200 rejections, `+` over `*` accounts for 304, and mixing `+` with `-` accounts for 112 (`a + b - c`); in `ponyc`'s own C code, `||`/`&&` over `==`/`!=` accounts for **202 of 232 (87.1%)** rejections!
- **Explicit Binding Sigils in Patterns (Carbon & Pony vs. Python/Rust/ML):** Basis: source (`carbon/docs/design/pattern_matching.md:64–247`, `ponyc/src/libponyc/ast/parser.c:630–637, 838`) + execution (CPython 3.13.15 stdlib `ast.Match` audit). In ML/Rust/Python (PEP 634), a bare lowercase identifier `case FOO:` or `case x:` in a `match` pattern silently binds a fresh variable rather than comparing against an in-scope constant if a constant name is misspelled or not in scope (Python's PEP 634 prevents constant-vs-binding confusion only by requiring dotted names like `Enum.VAL`, while bare names always bind). Both **Carbon** and **Pony** eliminate this ambiguity syntactically: Carbon requires explicit `: Type` annotations (`case n: i32 =>` or `case n: auto =>`, `pattern_matching.md:134–138`) so bare expressions (`case MY_CONST =>`) are always value comparisons via `==`; Pony requires an explicit `let`/`var` keyword inside `casepattern` (`parser.c:630–637`, `case let x: U32 =>`). In our scan of CPython 3.13.15 stdlib, `match`/`case` remains rare (`10` statements, `39` cases), containing `32` value patterns, `5` `_` wildcards, `3` bare name bindings, and `1` `as` alias—meaning requiring an explicit binding marker (`let x` or `x: T`) affects only **4 of 39 cases (10.3%)** while making value-vs-binding distinction 100% local without name-resolution context.

### 4. UAX #31, UTS #39 Joiner Rules, and Script Compatibility Across `unicode-ident` and `icu4x`

Basis: normative (`UAX #31` Rev. 45 / Unicode 18.0.0; `UTS #39` Rev. 34 / Unicode 18.0.0) + source (`dtolnay/unicode-ident@28018f288934fa6be60a6e791e071b7a7195643e`, `unicode-org/icu4x@e1cad3416d4d0b356158942cf76f23ea91cec9ca`) + execution (official Unicode 18.0.0 UCD & UTS #39 tables).

#### 4.1 Normative Shift in Unicode 15.1–18.0.0: Withdrawal of `UAX31-R1a` and Promotion of `ZWNJ`/`ZWJ`

Direct inspection of `UAX #31` Revision 45 (Unicode 18.0.0, 2026-09-01) and `DerivedCoreProperties.txt` (Unicode 18.0.0) resolves a widespread misconception in language specifications:

1. **Clause semantics (`UAX #31` Rev. 45, lines 683–729 & 2673–2678):**
   - **`UAX31-R1a` (*Restricted Format Characters*) was withdrawn in Unicode 15.1.** Its joiner characters (`U+200C ZWNJ`, `U+200D ZWJ`) were added directly to `Other_ID_Continue` / `XID_Continue` (`DerivedCoreProperties.txt` line 10044: `200C..200D ; XID_Continue # Cf [2]`), while its contextual security rules moved normatively to **UTS #39 §3.1.1 (*General Security Profile for Identifiers — Joining Controls*)**.
   - **`UAX31-R1b` (*Stable Identifiers*)** is *not* a ZWJ rule; it mandates forward monotonicity (once a string is a valid identifier under a given Unicode version, it remains valid under all future Unicode versions).
2. **Exact Unicode 18.0.0 set intersections (measured directly from UCD 18.0.0 + UTS #39 tables):**
   - `XID_Start`: **158,716** code points (`Cf ∩ XID_Start = 0`).
   - `XID_Continue`: **162,081** code points (`Cf ∩ XID_Continue = 2`, specifically `{U+200C, U+200D}`).
   - `General_Category=Cf`: **170** code points total (`138` are `Default_Ignorable_Code_Point`; `32` are visible format characters such as Arabic/Syriac/Kaithi number signs).
   - `Bidi_Control`: **12** code points (`U+061C`, `U+200E..200F`, `U+202A..202E`, `U+2066..2069`), all in `Cf`, **0** in `XID_Start ∪ XID_Continue`. Consequently, Trojan Source bidirectional reordering attacks (`CVE-2021-42574`; Boucher & Anderson, USENIX Security 2023) operate inside **comments and string literals**, never inside `UAX31-R1` identifiers; whereas homoglyph attacks (`CVE-2021-42694`) and invisible-joiner attacks operate inside identifiers.
   - `UTS #39 Identifier_Status=Allowed`: **33,792** code points; `confusables.txt`: **6,712** prototype mappings; `U+200C` and `U+200D` both have `Identifier_Type=Default_Ignorable` (hence *excluded* from `Identifier_Status=Allowed` unless explicitly admitted via §3.1.1.1 Contexts A1/A2/B).
3. **Direct execution against `dtolnay/unicode-ident@28018f288934fa6be60a6e791e071b7a7195643e` (`src/tables.rs`, `UNICODE_VERSION = (18, 0, 0)`):** Executing the exact branchless 2-level trie lookup on `TRIE_START` (511 B), `TRIE_CONTINUE` (1,793 B), and `LEAF` (8,064 B; **10,368 B total**) confirms `is_xid_continue('\u{200C}') == true`, `is_xid_continue('\u{200D}') == true`, and `is_xid_continue('\u{202E}') == false`. Any Rust/systems compiler upgrading `unicode-ident` across Unicode 15.1 without adding either a `Default_Ignorable` filter or UTS #39 §3.1.1.1 checks silently begins accepting `admin\u{200C}user` as a distinct identifier from `adminuser`.

#### 4.2 Exact UTS #39 §3.1.1.1 Contextual Joiner Rules and Empirical Policy Matrix

UTS #39 Revision 34 §3.1.1.1 requires **two global preconditions**—(i) **NFC normalization** and (ii) **single-script string** (ignoring `Common`/`Inherited` via `Script_Extensions`)—plus one of three regular-expression contexts:

- **Context A1 (Cursive-breaking `ZWNJ`, e.g., Persian):** `/$LJ $T* ZWNJ $RJ/` where `$LJ = [Joining_Type=Dual_Joining, Left_Joining]`, `$RJ = [Joining_Type=Dual_Joining, Right_Joining]`, `$T = [Joining_Type=Transparent]`. (Strictly tighter than IDNA2008 CONTEXTJ, which permits `$T*` *after* `ZWNJ`.)
- **Context A2 (Conjunct-breaking `ZWNJ`, e.g., Malayalam, Kannada):** `/$L $M* $V $M₁* ZWNJ $M₁* $L/` where `$L = \p{General_Category=Letter}`, `$V = \p{Canonical_Combining_Class=Virama}` (`CCC=9`, 69 code points in Unicode 18.0.0), `$M = \p{General_Category=Mn}`, `$M₁ = [Mn & CCC≠0]`.
- **Context B (Conjunct-forming `ZWJ`, e.g., Sinhala, Devanagari):** `/$L $M* $V $M₁* ZWJ (?!$D)/` where `$D = \p{Indic_Syllabic_Category=Vowel_Dependent}` (741 code points in Unicode 18.0.0).

*Implementation pitfall discovered during execution:* Because every Virama (`$V`) has `General_Category=Mn` and `CCC=9 ≠ 0`, `$V ⊂ $M₁`. A naive greedy backward scan skipping `$M₁*` before matching `$V` consumes the virama itself and spuriously rejects all valid Malayalam, Sinhala, Devanagari, and Kannada sequences; implementations must either backtrack or exclude `$V` from the rightmost `$M₁*` scan.

#### 4.3 The UTS #39 `internalSkeleton` vs. Contextual Joiner Tension and ICU4X Coverage

Basis: normative (`UTS #39` Rev. 34 §4, lines 1014–1105) + source (`unicode-org/icu4x@e1cad3416d4d0b356158942cf76f23ea91cec9ca`, `components/properties/src/{lib.rs,props.rs,script.rs}`).

- **Why strict `skeleton()` injectivity conflicts with Persian/Indic minimal pairs:** UTS #39 §4 defines `internalSkeleton(X)` in four steps: (1) convert to `NFD`; (2) **remove all `Default_Ignorable_Code_Point` characters**; (3) map via `confusables.txt` prototypes; (4) reapply `NFD`; and defines `skeleton(X) = bidiSkeleton(LTR, X)` (applying UBA L2–L4 before `internalSkeleton`, since `internalSkeleton` can alter `Bidi_Class` and does not commute with bidi reordering, with a fast path `bidiSkeleton(LTR, X) = internalSkeleton(X)` whenever `X` contains no `Bidi_Class` `R` or `AL` characters). Because `U+200C` and `U+200D` are `Default_Ignorable_Code_Point`, Step 2 deletes them unconditionally. Consequently, enforcing strict `skeleton`-uniqueness within a scope makes it **illegal to declare both `نامه‌ای` ("a letter") and `نامهای` ("names of") in the same scope**, even though both are single-script, NFC, Context-A1-valid, and visually distinct in Persian typography. Conversely, omitting Step 2 allows `admin<ZWNJ>user` to evade skeleton collision unless Contexts A1/A2/B already rejected it at lexing time.
- **UTS #39 §5.2 Restriction Levels (1–6):** `Moderately_Restrictive` permits Latin plus any single Recommended script *except* Cyrillic and Greek (blocking Latin+Cyrillic `sсope` while allowing Latin+Arabic or Latin+Malayalam bilingual identifiers—though Contexts A1/A2/B themselves still require single-script around joiners). `Highly_Restrictive` restricts multi-script mixing to `Latn + {Jpan | Hanb | Kore}`. Neither Restriction Level catches *whole-script* confusables (`scope` Latin vs. `ѕсоре` all-Cyrillic) or single-script confusables (`ǉeto` vs. `ljeto`), which require in-scope `bidiSkeleton` collision checking (as recommended in Carbon `p000142` and UTS #55 §5.1).
- **Exact `unicode-org/icu4x` (`e1cad3416d4d0b356158942cf76f23ea91cec9ca`) capabilities vs. gaps:** `components/properties/src/props.rs` provides `#![no_std]` zero-copy `CodePointSetData` / `CodePointMapData` for `XidStart`, `XidContinue`, `IdStart`, `IdContinue`, `JoinControl`, `BidiControl`, `BidiClass`, `DefaultIgnorableCodePoint`, `GeneralCategory` (`GeneralCategoryGroup::Cf`), `JoiningType`, `JoiningGroup`, `CanonicalCombiningClass`, `IndicSyllabicCategory`, `IndicConjunctBreak`, `PatternSyntax`, `PatternWhiteSpace`, `Script`, and `ScriptWithExtensions` (`src/script.rs`). However, at this pinned revision, neither `components/properties` nor `components/experimental` exposes `Identifier_Status`, `Identifier_Type`, or UTS #39 `confusables.txt` / `skeleton()` / `bidiSkeleton()` APIs; downstream toolchains must ingest UTS #39 tables directly or pair `icu_properties` + `icu_normalizer` with a dedicated `confusables` crate/table.

### 6. Human Comprehensibility and Conceptual Simplicity / Model Refinement (C1–C2)

Basis: derived from primary empirical studies + source inspection.

#### 6.1 (C1) Human Comprehensibility & Mental-Model Ergonomics

- **Human comprehension vs. LLM/agent execution:** An LLM or static analyzer can apply a 15-level C precedence table mechanically with high reliability, whereas human working memory reliably retains only **3–4 coarse chunks** (postfix/primary, multiplicative/additive, comparison, logical)—consistent with Jones (2006) finding 94–100% accuracy on `*`/`+`/`==` and 0–50% accuracy on bitwise, modulo, shift, and `&&`/`||`. Designing surface syntax around what machines can disambiguate optimizes for the wrong bottleneck; human code review and specification audit require that syntactic grouping be locally obvious without recalling arbitrary tie-breakers.
- **Working-memory concept count & local vs. nonlocal reasoning:**
  - *Total order (C/Rust):* Requires memorizing $\binom{15}{2} = 105$ cross-level precedence edges; silent miscompilation occurs whenever memory errs.
  - *Targeted partial order (Carbon):* Reduces required human memorization to 4 intuitive chains (postfix > unary > `*` > `+` > comparison > logical; bitwise > comparison) and converts all other combinations into **local compile-time diagnostics** (`handle_expr.cpp:382`) rather than silent semantic shifts.
  - *Flat antichain (Pony):* Minimizes grammar rules to 1 rule (`op == op`), **but degrades visual readability** by forcing parenthesis clutter on universally understood formulas (`((a * b) + (c * d))` and `((x == 1) and (y == 2))`), trading precedence-recall load for delimiter-matching load.
  - *Explicit binding sigils (Carbon `x: T`, Pony `let x`):* Converts pattern matching from **nonlocal** (requiring lookup across imports/outer scopes to determine whether `FOO` is a constant or a fresh binder) to **100% local** syntax inspection.
  - *Unicode joiners & confusables:* Humans cannot visually distinguish `admin<ZWNJ>user` from `adminuser`, nor Latin `scope` from Cyrillic `ѕсоре` in sans-serif review UIs, whereas Persian/Malayalam/Sinhala readers *immediately* see orthographic corruption when `ZWNJ`/`ZWJ` are stripped. Diagnostics must print escaped code points (`U+200C`, `U+0441 CYRILLIC SMALL LETTER ES`) alongside visual snippets so reviewers can audit invisible characters directly.

#### 6.2 (C2) Simplicity, Orthogonality, and Universal Model Refinement

- **Separating core invariants from accidents:** C's notorious `a & b == c` trap is an historical accident from B (which lacked `&&` and overloaded `&` for logical conjunction), not a semantic necessity. Similarly, treating `General_Category=Cf` as a monolithic "security hazard" conflates three orthogonal Unicode mechanisms: (1) stateful bidirectional reordering controls (`Bidi_Control`, 12 CPs, already excluded from `XID_Continue`—relevant to literals/comments), (2) invisible default-ignorable joiners (`Join_Control`, 2 CPs, contextually visible *only* inside cursive/virama clusters), and (3) visual cross-script homoglyphs (`confusables.txt` + `Script_Extensions`, completely untouched by a `Cf` ban).
- **Minimal universal abstraction (Felleisen & Ousterhout tests):**
  - *For expressions:* A **strict partial order + per-group associativity** (implemented via a single table-driven Pratt parser) strictly subsumes both total orders (linear chain) and Pony's flat rule (empty edge set) as special cases of one deep module, adding **zero** new grammar productions or adapter layers (whereas yacc-style precedence climbing must explode nonterminals to avoid primary-expression reduce/reduce conflicts, as Carbon `p000555:219–228` documents). Between transitive (Carbon) and non-transitive (Rhombus) partial orders, **transitivity** is strictly cleaner for closed core languages because it guarantees local ambiguity decidability with zero lookahead and prevents counterintuitive gaps like Rhombus accepting `a bits.and b == 0 && c` while rejecting `a bits.and b && c`; **non-transitive local edges** become necessary only when independent macros define open-ended infix operators without a global precedence DAG.
  - *For Unicode identifiers:* Rather than ad-hoc character blacklists (`ban Cf`), the orthogonal three-stage pipeline—(1) `UAX31-R1` NFC `XID_Start`/`XID_Continue` lexical scan, (2) UTS #39 §3.1.1.1 Context A1/A2/B gate triggered *only* on `U+200C`/`U+200D`, and (3) UTS #39 §5.2 Restriction Level + §4 `bidiSkeleton` scope-collision diagnostic (with explicit contextual-joiner minimal-pair policy)—separates lexical well-formedness, script-shaping validity, and anti-spoofing cleanly without breaking any living script.

### 7. End-to-End Solution and Implementation Validation (V1–V4)

Basis: documentation + source + execution.

- **(V1) Specification Adequacy, Mutation & Non-Vacuity Validation:** Formal grammar/precedence specifications must be cross-checked against prose docs to catch specification drift (such as Carbon's `README.md:209` claiming `or --> == --> as` contrary to `precedence.cpp:25–33`). For Unicode joiner rules, golden orthographic test suites across Persian, Malayalam, Sinhala, Devanagari, and Kannada must pair *positive* linguistic specimens (minimal pairs with semantic distinction) with *negative* mutant specimens (deleting the preceding virama, appending a `Vowel_Dependent` after `ZWJ`, mixing scripts, inserting trailing `ZWNJ`, or denormalizing to NFD) to verify 100% mutant kill rate—directly catching greedy-scan bugs like `$M₁*` consuming `$V` (`CCC=9`).
- **(V2) Compiler, Lowering & Checker Soundness Fuzzing:**
  - *Precedence parsers:* Differential property-based fuzzing generates random expression trees with explicit parentheses, strips parentheses selectively wherever `GetPriority` predicts admissibility, reparses, and asserts exact AST isomorphism (verifying both **unambiguity** and **round-trip faithfulness**). Carbon's `ConsistencyCheck()` (`precedence.cpp:129–142`) and `CARBON_CHECK(table[b][a] != LeftFirst)` (`precedence.cpp:96`) enforce antisymmetry/acyclicity statically; Rhombus tests `'inconsistent-prec` and `'inconsistent-assoc` diagnostics across conflicting macro definitions.
  - *Unicode lexers:* Exhaustive verification over all `1,114,112` Unicode scalar values (`0x0000..0x10FFFF` excluding surrogates) is cheap (< 5 ms in Rust/C) and makes differential testing between compressed tries (`unicode-ident`, `icu_properties`) and raw UCD `DerivedCoreProperties.txt` **exhaustive rather than sampled**. Fuzzing multi-codepoint combining sequences against reference regex engines validates Context A1/A2/B state machines on adversarial long `$M*`/`$T*` runs (preventing catastrophic regex backtracking / ReDoS).
- **(V3) Concurrency, Crash, Silicon & Physical Leakage Validation:** Not directly applicable as a primary failure mode for pure stateless lexer/parser syntax passes, **except** at the human-review / terminal-rendering boundary where terminal emulators, web diff viewers, and IDE shaping engines (HarfBuzz/Uniscribe/CoreText) implement differing versions of the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm (UAX #9) and font fallback tables—meaning a sequence that appears distinct in one shaping engine may collapse visually in a fallback monospace font (UTS #39 §3.1.1.2 Limitation 1). Differential rendering tests across major monospace programming fonts and review UIs are needed to validate actual visual distinctness.
- **(V4) Human Comprehension Validation & Empirical Behavioral Litmus Testing:** Formal soundness of a partial-order grammar does *not* prove that humans understand the surviving edges. Validating *which* edges belong in the partial order requires behavioral litmus testing (analogous to SMoL mental-model studies and Jones 2006 / Gopstein 2017 paired-evaluation trials): every edge retained in a language's precedence DAG (e.g., Rhombus retaining `&& > ||` or `mod > +`) should be backed by empirical human accuracy thresholds (e.g., $\ge 90\%$ agreement across developers) rather than designer intuition, and every diagnostic wording should be tested for first-try comprehension and repair accuracy.

## Boundaries

- **Other source languages and bit-heavy C/Rust corpora (`not examined`):** Our real-corpus partial-order measurement covered CPython 3.13.15 stdlib (534 files, 278,484 LoC) and 15 pure C files from `ponylang/ponyc` (20,366 LoC, condition/return expressions only, excluding assignment RHS and macro bodies). Low-level C/Rust cryptography, networking, and kernel bit-twiddling codebases (e.g., Linux drivers, OpenSSL, codecs) exhibit higher densities of `&`, `|`, `^`, `<<`, `>>`, and `%` in assignment expressions and may show higher Carbon/Rhombus rejection rates than `ponyc`'s `-Wparentheses`-clean conditions.
- **Direct execution of the Carbon compiler and Rhombus Racket runtime (`not examined`):** Precedence tables for Carbon (`precedence.cpp`), Rhombus (`order-primitive.rkt` + `operator.rkt`), and Pony (`syntax.c`) were transcribed verbatim from pinned upstream source and cross-checked with a 14-case unit test suite rather than by invoking the native `carbon`, `racket`, or `ponyc` binaries on transformed corpus files. Subtle interactions with Rhombus user-defined macros, Carbon `where`/`impl`/`as` type-expression boundaries, or Pony type-expression `|`/`&` unions were verified by source inspection rather than native compiler execution.
- **Controlled human-subject evaluation of explicit pattern-binding sigils (`unknown`):** While the shadowing/typo failure mode of bare pattern identifiers in ML/Rust/Python is well documented and syntactically eliminated by Carbon (`x: T`) and Pony (`let x`), we found no controlled randomized human-subject trial directly measuring error rates or comprehension speed of explicit binding sigils versus bare pattern identifiers (analogous to Gopstein 2017 for operator precedence).
- **Unicode versions prior to 15.1 (`known not to apply`):** The finding that `unicode-ident::is_xid_continue` admits `U+200C` and `U+200D` applies to Unicode 15.1–18.0.0 (`dtolnay/unicode-ident@28018f288934fa6be60a6e791e071b7a7195643e`). Older lexers pinned to Unicode $\le 15.0$ exclude `ZWNJ`/`ZWJ` from `XID_Continue` by default.
- **UTS #39 `confusables` / `Identifier_Status` inside `icu4x` (`unsupported`):** Direct inspection of `unicode-org/icu4x@e1cad3416d4d0b356158942cf76f23ea91cec9ca` (`components/properties/src/props.rs` and `components/experimental/src/`) confirms that UTS #39 confusable skeletons (`confusables.txt`) and `Identifier_Status`/`Identifier_Type` are not provided as ICU4X component APIs at this revision.
- **Font-dependent visual indistinguishability inside valid A1/A2/B contexts (`known not to apply` as a complete defense):** As UTS #39 §3.1.1.2 explicitly warns, satisfying Contexts A1, A2, and B does *not* guarantee visible distinction across all fonts or all valid sequences (for example, some Indic consonant pairs lack a ligature in fallback monospace fonts, making `C + V + ZWNJ + C` visually identical to `C + V + C`).
