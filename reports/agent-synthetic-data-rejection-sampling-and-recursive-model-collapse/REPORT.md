# Synthetic Data Collapse, Verifier-Filtered Rejection Sampling, Curriculum Censoring, and Deduplication Forensics

## Summary

Recursive training on synthetic model outputs, verifier-filtered rejection sampling, and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) partition into four distinct mathematical regimes—and exhibit concrete failure modes in production synthetic-data pipelines:

1. **Data-Pool Retention vs. Tail-Truncated Subsampling (`Shumailov et al.`, `Gerstgrasser et al.`, `Kazdan et al.`, `Probe 1`, `Probe 2B`):** Pure `Replace` recursion diverges linearly in fixed-design ridgeless regression ($E_{\text{test}}^{\text{Replace}}(\hat{w}_n) = \frac{\sigma^2 d}{T-d-1} n$) and converges almost surely to absorbing Dirac $\delta$ masses in discrete categorical and deep finite-sample Gaussian loops. Unbounded-compute `Accumulate` caps linear-regression excess risk at $\le \frac{\pi^2}{6} E_{\text{Gen-1}}$ and bounds 1D biased-MLE Gaussian mean drift via the Euler product $1 - \prod_{k=1}^t(1 - \frac{1}{k^2 n}) \to 1 - \frac{\sin(\pi/\sqrt{n})}{\pi/\sqrt{n}}$, yet still diverges logarithmically on fixed-bandwidth nonparametric KDE ($\operatorname{Var}(X_t) = \sigma_0^2 + h^2 H_t \to \infty$) unless bandwidth shrinks as $h_t = c(tn)^{-1/5}$. Under fixed-compute closed-loop `Accumulate-Subsample`, untruncated empirical resampling (`top_p = 1.0`) plateaus at `42.0%` rare-tail recall loss over 20 rounds (`0.02825 -> 0.01638`), whereas **under tail-truncated generation (`top_p = 0.85`)**, uniform $1/t$ subsampling erodes rare-tail recall by **`91.3%`** (`0.02825 -> 0.00247`).
2. **Epistemic Contamination Floors, Heavy-Tail Cutoff, and Categorical Sample Budgets (`Dohmatob et al. ICML 2024 & ICLR 2025`, `Seddik et al.`, `Probe 1C`, `Probe 2A`):** Any non-vanishing fraction $p_2 > 0$ of epistemically shifted synthetic data ($\|w_2^* - w_1^*\|_\Sigma^2 = c^2$) imposes an irreducible linear-scaling error floor $\Omega(p_2^2 c^2)$ (`Strong Model Collapse`). On Zipfian skill distributions $p_i \propto i^{-\beta}$ ($\beta > 1$), $k$-tail-cut synthetic data stalls test risk at $\min(T, k^\beta)^{-(1-1/\beta)}$, while mixing real fraction $\pi \in (0, 1)$ delays recovery until $T_{\text{grok}} \asymp k^\beta / \pi$ (`5.00×` sample inflation per `5×` reduction in $\pi$). On a categorical vocabulary of size $s$ anchored by $N$ real tokens, `Seddik et al.` Theorem 3 bounds expected $\ell_1$ drift uniformly across generations ($C_0 = \frac{e^3}{2\pi} \approx 3.1967$) when per-generation synthetic draw count satisfies $n \le 2\pi e^{-2}\min\!\left[s - 2,\; \log\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}\pi N\epsilon}{6es}\right)\right]$ ($2\pi e^{-2} \approx 0.8503$).
3. **Curation Multiplicity, Verifier Breakdown Likelihood Ratio, and Iterative DPO Judge Shift (`Ferbach et al.`, `Feng et al.`, `Self-Rewarding LMs`, `STaR`, `ReST^EM`, `RFT`, `Probe 3`):** Pure $K$-choice Bradley-Terry self-curation acts as RLHF with vanishing regularization $\beta = 1/t$, collapsing conditional diversity onto maximum-reward modes. Mixing curated outputs at weight $\lambda$ against reference distribution $p_{\text{ref}}$ guarantees **prior-independent** ($q_0$-uniform) density-ratio stability $\sup_x \frac{p_t(x)}{p_{\text{ref}}(x)} \le \frac{1}{1 - \lambda(K-1)}$ when $\lambda < \frac{1}{K-1}$ (`Ferbach` Theorem 2.4); when $\lambda \ge \frac{1}{K-1}$ on finite-support distributions with optimal mass $q_0 > 0$, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t \| p_{\text{ref}}) \le -\log q_0$ stays finite, but rare-mode density ratios blow up as $1/q_0$ (`57,858.5×` at $\lambda = 3.0, q_0 = 10^{-5}$). Under binary $(\phi, \psi)$-verifier pruning (`Feng` Theorem 4.2), asymptotic downstream classification transitions sharply from `100%` to `0%` accuracy at $p_\star = \frac{1}{1 + \psi/\phi}$, governed by the false-positive-to-recall likelihood ratio $\psi/\phi$. Separately, iterative DPO with self-generated LLM-judge preferences (`Self-Rewarding LMs`) improves held-out pairwise preference agreement (`78.7% -> 81.7%`) and `AlpacaEval 2.0` win rates while inflating mean response length `2.34×` (`1,092 -> 2,552` tokens) and degrading objective reasoning benchmarks (`GSM8K`, `ARC-C`).
4. **Shared-Policy RLVR `pass@k` Contraction, Offline `0/8` Censoring, and Compositional KL Resets (`Yue et al.`, `ProRL`, `open-r1`, `Probe 4`):** On multi-path benchmarks where dominant templates already succeed on routine problems, shared-parameter GRPO policy gradients sharpen common heuristic weights across prompts—raising `pass@1` (`22.1% -> 69.5%`) while contracting `pass@256` (`98.6% -> 82.4%`) even without offline prompt filtering (`Yue et al.`). Offline rollout filtering (`open-r1` `N=8`, strict `0.1 < r < 0.6`) permanently deletes `88.61%`–`92.27%` of solvable $p \in [0.010, 0.015]$ frontier problems prior to RLVR, compounding `pass@256` contraction (`78.1%`). Conversely, on low-baseline $L$-step conjunctive tasks ($R = \prod_{l=1}^L \mathbb{1}_{a_l = a_l^*}$), marginal step credit $q^{L-1} \ll \beta$ traps static-reference KL optimization at a low stationary point ($q = 0.2734 \implies \text{pass@1} = 0.56\%$ for $L=4, q_0=0.20, \beta=0.05$), whereas periodic reference-policy resets ($\pi_{\text{ref}} \leftarrow \pi_\theta$, `ProRL`) form a proximal mirror-descent ratchet that crosses the barrier (`pass@1 -> 100.0%`, `pass@256 -> 100.0%`).
5. **Production Pipeline Forensics (`self-instruct`, `WizardLM`, `datatrove`, `open-r1`, `limit-of-RLVR`, `Probe 5`):** Direct source inspection uncovers live data-loss and contamination defects across widely used synthetic-data repositories: `self-instruct` combines `top_p = 0.5` bootstrap truncation, unstemmed `[a-z0-9]+` `ROUGE-L > 0.7` morphological blind spots, over-broad regex blacklists, and whole-task deletion (`return []`) on valid multi-output inputs; `nlpxucan/WizardLM` implements only a single `M = 1` pass over 5 of 6 paper operators with zero *Elimination Evolving* filters; `datatrove` MinHash skips `<5`-token documents (`100%` duplicate leak), sets default `TextNormConfig.norm_numbers = True` (collapsing `24/25 = 96%` of distinct parameterized math problems to `"0"`), and merges LSH buckets via unverified Union-Find transitive chains (`19 -> 1` survivor across `J = 0.0` endpoints); and `open-r1` `decontaminate.py` splits 8-grams on whitespace without stripping punctuation, missing punctuation- or LaTeX-reformatted benchmark copies.

### 2. Recursive Data Retention, Heavy-Tail Truncation, and Categorical Absorption

`Basis: documentation + source + derived + execution`

1. **Parametric vs. Nonparametric Accumulation (`Gerstgrasser et al.`, `Shumailov et al.`, `Kazdan et al.`, `Probe 1A–1B`):**
   - In fixed-design isotropic linear regression ($d = 5, T = 26, n = 25, \sigma = 1$), exact analytical risk gives `Replace = 6.2500` vs. `Accumulate = 0.4014 <= 0.4112` ($\frac{\sigma^2 d}{T-d-1}\frac{\pi^2}{6}$). Reproducing the three `KoyejoLab` workflows in pure stdlib (`120` Monte Carlo trials solving exact normal equations and measuring population excess risk $\|\hat{w} - w^*\|_2^2$) yields `Replace = 5.8502`, `Accumulate-Subsample = 0.8977`, and `Accumulate = 0.4279` (`late_ratio_15_to_25`: `1.215` for `Accumulate-Subsample` vs. `1.674` for `Replace`).
   - In 1D Gaussian `Replace` recursion (`Shumailov` Eq. 4 vs. Eq. 15), the $\frac{3}{2}\sigma^2\sum_{i=0}^n \frac{1}{M_i}$ Wasserstein-2 law holds in the asymptotic $M \gtrsim 100, n/M \ll 1$ regime (combining $1\cdot\sigma^2/M_i$ mean drift with $\frac{1}{2}\sigma^2/M_i$ standard-deviation drift: at $M = 200, n = 6$, theoretical `0.0450` matches Monte Carlo `0.0472`, refuting a naive unit-prefactor `0.0300`), whereas for deep recursion $n \gtrsim M$, $\mathbb{E}[\log S^i] < 0$ drives variance almost surely to `0` (`late_median_var = 0.2997` at $M = 25, n = 30$). Under biased MLE `Accumulate` (`fit_gaussians.py:L102`, `bias=True`), exact Euler-product mean-squared drift at $M = 25, t = 30$ is `0.06328` (Monte Carlo `0.06235`, $t \to \infty$ limit $1 - \frac{\sin(\pi/5)}{\pi/5} = 0.06451$).
   - By contrast, nonparametric Gaussian KDE (`Kazdan` App. C, Theorems 5–7) with fixed bandwidth $h = 0.5$ diverges under **both** `Replace` ($\operatorname{Var}(X_{200}) = 51.00$) and `Accumulate` ($\operatorname{Var}(X_{200}) = 1 + h^2\sum_{i=1}^{200}\frac{1}{i} = 2.470 \to \infty$), converging only when bandwidth shrinks adaptively as $h_t = c(tn)^{-1/5}$ (`1.1935 < 1.2142`; note `Kazdan` p. 17 prose prints a sign typo $c(tn)^{1/5}$ while the proof sum uses $(in)^{-2/5}$).
2. **Closed-Loop `Accumulate-Subsample` Under Untruncated vs. Tail-Truncated Generation (`Dohmatob et al. ICML 2024`, `Probe 2A–2B`):**
   - On an infinite Zipfian skill distribution $p_i = i^{-\beta}/\zeta(\beta)$ ($\beta = 1.5, \zeta(1.5) = 2.612375$), `top_p = 0.5` nucleus truncation (`self-instruct` `bootstrap_instructions.py:L178`) retains only the top `k = 2` skills and imposes an irreducible error floor of `0.4819`. At $k = 25$ ($k^\beta = 125$), pure tail-cut synthetic training stalls at `0.1516` vs. clean Hutter scaling `0.0347` at $T = 20,000$ (empirical log-log slope `-0.3331` matching $-(1 - 1/\beta) = -1/3$). ### 3. Curated $K$-Choice Mode Collapse, Verifier Specificity, and Iterative Judge Drift

`Basis: documentation + derived + execution`

1. **One-Way $q_0$-Uniform Density-Ratio Control vs. Finite-Support KL (`Ferbach et al.`, `arXiv:2407.09499v1` Thms. 2.1–2.4, `Probe 3A`):**
   - Pure $K = 4$ Bradley-Terry curation ($p_{t+1} = p_t H_{p_t}^K$) collapses distribution entropy (`1.612 -> 0.673` nats) onto the reward-maximizing support ($D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_* \| p_{55}) = 7.90 \times 10^{-12}$) and matches exact RLHF with $\beta = 1/t$ as $K \to \infty$ (verified to `< 10^{-15}` at $t = 5$).
   - Under reference-anchored mixing $p_{t+1} = \frac{1}{1+\lambda}p_{\text{ref}} + \frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}p_t H_{p_t}^K$, `Ferbach et al.` Theorem 2.4 proves a **one-way sufficient condition**: if $\lambda < \frac{1}{K-1}$, then $\sup_x \frac{p_t(x)}{p_{\text{ref}}(x)} \le \frac{1}{1 - \lambda(K-1)}$ and $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t \| p_{\text{ref}}) \le -\log(1 - \lambda(K-1))$ for all $t \ge 1$, **uniformly over arbitrarily small initial optimal mass $q_0 = \mathbb{P}_{\text{ref}}(r(x)=r_*)$**. On any fixed distribution with $q_0 > 0$, $\frac{p_t(x)}{p_{\text{ref}}(x)} \le \frac{1}{q_0}$ holds trivially for all $\lambda \ge 0$, so $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t \| p_{\text{ref}}) \le -\log q_0 < \infty$ never diverges to $+\infty$; what breaks when $\lambda \ge \frac{1}{K-1}$ is prior-independent density-ratio stability (`Probe 3A` at $K = 4, q_0 = 10^{-5}$: safe $\lambda = 0.25 < 1/3$ caps $\max_t D_{\mathrm{KL}} = 0.0046 \le \max \log \frac{p_t}{p_{\text{ref}}} = 0.3570 \le 1.3863$, whereas $\lambda = 3.0$ inflates $\max_x \frac{p_t(x)}{p_{\text{ref}}(x)}$ to **`57,858.5×`** while $D_{\mathrm{KL}} = 6.001 < -\log(10^{-5}) = 11.513$).
2. **Verifier Breakdown Likelihood Ratio vs. Iterative DPO Judge Drift (`Feng et al.`, `Self-Rewarding LMs`, `Probe 3B`):**
   - For binary $(\phi, \psi)$-verifier SFT pruning (`Feng et al.` Theorem 4.2), the asymptotic threshold $p_\star = \frac{1}{1 + \psi/\phi}$ depends on the **likelihood ratio $\psi/\phi$** between false-positive acceptance $\psi$ and true-positive recall $\phi$—not on $\psi$ in isolation. A 4-way multiple-choice answer check ($\phi = 1.0, \psi = 0.25 \implies p_\star = 0.800$) flips downstream accuracy from `1.00` at $p = 0.70$ to `0.00` (complete label inversion) at $p = 0.88$, whereas a high-specificity test suite ($\phi = 0.90, \psi = 0.02 \implies \psi/\phi = 0.0222, p_\star = 0.9783$) preserves `1.00` accuracy at $p = 0.88$. Likewise, if an ungrounded binary filter's false-positive rate drifts upward across iterations ($\psi_t: 0.15 \to 0.72$ at fixed $\phi = 0.80, p = 0.62$), $p_{\star, t}$ drops across $p$ (`0.842 -> 0.526`) and collapses accuracy (`1.00 -> 0.00`).
   - **Mechanism distinction (`Self-Rewarding LMs` vs. binary SFT pruning):** `Yuan et al.` (`arXiv:2401.10020v3`) do **not** run binary SFT pruning (which failed in their Appendix A.2); they run **Iterative DPO** on self-judged preference pairs. Their judge's held-out human-agreement accuracy on Open Assistant actually *increases* across iterations (`78.7% -> 80.4% -> 81.7%`, Table 4) alongside `AlpacaEval 2.0` (`9.94% -> 20.44%`), yet DPO preference optimization exploits length and stylistic correlates—inflating mean output length `2.34×` (`1,092 -> 1,552 -> 2,552` tokens) while regressing objective reasoning (`GSM8K` `60.27 -> 57.70`, `ARC-C` `57.51 -> 53.13`).

### 4. RLVR Reasoning Boundaries, Offline Pass-Rate Censoring, and Reference-Reset Horizon

`Basis: documentation + source + derived + execution`

The apparent tension between `Yue et al.` (`arXiv:2504.13837v5`, where RLVR contracts `pass@256` on `Omni-MATH-Test` from `69.1% -> 63.9%`) and `Liu et al.` (`ProRL`, `arXiv:2505.24864v1`, where prolonged RLVR expands `pass@128` on `Reasoning Gym` and `boxnet`) is governed by three distinct mechanisms (`Probe 4A–4B`):

1. **Shared-Parameter Heuristic Sharpening on High-Baseline Multi-Path Tasks (`Yue et al.` Sec. 4.3, `ProRL` *Diminish* Regime, `Probe 4B` Regime I):**
   - `ProRL` explicitly observes the same *Diminished Reasoning Boundary* (`pass@128` contraction) on high-baseline math benchmarks where the starting model already solves routine problems via standard templates.
   - Modeling 40 problems (`28` medium items solvable by both a rare structural invariant `a=0` and a high-prior algebraic/casework template `a=1` or `a=2`; `12` frontier items solvable solely by `a=0` at initial prior $p_0 = 0.0120$, sharing algebraic/casework surface cues) under a **24-parameter shared log-linear policy** $\pi_\theta(a \mid x) \propto \exp(\theta^\top \phi(x, a))$ updated strictly by group-normalized `N = 8` GRPO gradients (zero offline filtering) reproduces the crossover without side channels: reinforcing `a=1, 2` on medium problems sharpens shared cue weights, lifting `pass@1` from **`22.1% -> 69.5%`** while contracting `pass@256` from **`98.6% -> 82.4%`** (`N = 1024` unbiased estimator `98.7% -> 80.2%`).
2. **Offline `0/8` Pass-Rate Censoring vs. Online Re-Sampling (`open-r1` vs. `ProRL`, `Probe 4A–4B`):**
   - `huggingface/open-r1@5b6ff22b` (`compute_pass_rate.py:L192`, `filter_dapo.yaml`, `filter_python.yaml`) samples `N = 8` rollouts *once offline* and enforces strict `0.1 < mean_reward < 0.6`, retaining only pass counts `{1, 2, 3, 4}/8`. A solvable frontier problem with true pass rate $p = 0.015$ (`true pass@256 = 97.91%`) has an **`88.61%`** probability of permanently vanishing before training starts (`92.27%` at $p = 0.010$). Applying this offline gate to the shared-policy GRPO loop deletes `11/12` frontier prompts upfront (receiving zero gradient updates) and contracts `pass@256` further from `82.4%` down to **`78.1%`** (`N = 1024` estimator `77.1%`). By contrast, online dynamic sampling at $N = 16$ (`ProRL`) visits `91.1%` of $p = 0.015$ items at least once across 10 epochs.
3. **Conjunctive Credit Attenuation vs. Reference-Policy Resets (`ProRL` Eq. 4, `Probe 4B` Regime II):**
   - On an $L$-step conjunctive compositional task ($R = \prod_{l=1}^L \mathbb{1}_{a_l = 0}$, base per-step prior $q_0 = 0.20 \implies \text{pass@1} = 0.16\%, \text{pass@256} = 33.6\%$), the exact KL-regularized step stationarity condition $\ln\frac{q}{1-q} = \ln\frac{q_{\text{ref}}}{1-q_{\text{ref}}} + \frac{q^{L-1}}{\beta}$ has marginal reward signal $q^{L-1} = 0.008 \ll \beta = 0.05$ at $L = 4$. With a frozen reference policy $\pi_{\text{ref}} = \pi_0$, a genuine negative-gradient barrier on $q \in (0.2734, 0.2990)$ traps optimization at **`q = 0.2734`** (`pass@1 = 0.56%`, `pass@256 = 76.1%`). Periodically resetting $\pi_{\text{ref}} \leftarrow \pi_\theta$ converts static KL control into a proximal mirror-descent ratchet ($\operatorname{logit}_{m+1} = \operatorname{logit}_m + q^{L-1}/\beta$), crossing the barrier to **`pass@1 = 100.0%, pass@256 = 100.0%`**.

### 6. Engineering Trade-Offs Across Pipeline Regimes

`Basis: derived`

1. **Real-Data Mixing Floor vs. Convergence Horizon:** Along `Dohmatob et al.`'s grokking scaling law $T_{\text{grok}} \asymp k^\beta / \pi$, reducing the clean real-data batch fraction $\pi$ trades real-token budget linearly against training-step horizon (`5.00×` step inflation from $\pi = 0.25 \to 0.05$ and again from $0.05 \to 0.01$ in `Probe 2A`). Maintaining a stratified real-data floor (such as $\pi \ge 0.20$) under fixed-compute `Accumulate-Subsample` prevents $1/t$ dilution of tail examples when synthetic decoding is nucleus- or temperature-truncated (`Probe 2B`). On clean SFT datasets exceeding roughly `1,024` examples—the empirical crossover observed specifically on `Gemma-2-2B` / `HelpSteer2` (`Kazdan et al.` Sec. 4)—unverified synthetic supplements risk introducing an $\Omega(p_2^2 c^2)$ epistemic bias floor (`Dohmatob et al. ICLR 2025`).
2. **Rejection-Sampling Multiplicity vs. Reference Anchor Ratio:** Increasing best-of-$K$ selection multiplicity accelerates per-step expected reward gain proportionally to $\frac{K-1}{K}$, at the cost of tightening `Ferbach et al.`'s prior-independent density-ratio stability region $\lambda < \frac{1}{K-1}$ and accelerating conditional entropy collapse (`Kirk et al.` `11.3×` EAD drop; `Yuan RFT` `LLaMA-33B` `2.78`-path saturation). Re-initializing outer `STaR`/`ReST^EM` M-steps from the base checkpoint (`Zelikman et al.`, `Singh et al.`) and deduplicating rollouts by execution/equation structure rather than surface strings counteracts cumulative mode narrowing.
3. **Verifier Likelihood Ratio ($\psi/\phi$) vs. Generator Competence ($p$):** Under binary SFT pruning (`Feng et al.` Theorem 4.2), label noise contracts only when generator error $p$ lies below $p_\star = \frac{1}{1 + \psi/\phi}$. Achieving $p_\star \ge 0.97$ requires a false-positive-to-recall ratio $\psi/\phi \le 0.03$ (for example, $\psi \le 0.02$ paired with recall $\phi \ge 0.65$, such as executable compiler/kernel checks or deterministic test suites; if recall drops to $\phi = 0.20$ at $\psi = 0.02$, $p_\star$ falls to `0.909`). In iterative DPO with learned or self-rewarding judges (`Self-Rewarding LMs`), monitoring response length and held-out objective benchmarks separately from pairwise preference win rates detects reward-model length/style drift.
4. **Online Frontier Retention vs. Compositional KL Horizon:** Retaining `0/N` prompts in an online re-sampled buffer (`ProRL` $N = 16, T = 1.2$) avoids the `88.6%`–`92.3%` permanent frontier deletion of single-pass offline `0/8` filtering (`open-r1`), while pairing explicit KL regularization with validation-gated $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ resets overcomes the $q_0^{L-1} \ll \beta$ conjunctive credit barrier without sacrificing per-stage KL stability (`Probe 4B`).
5. **Domain-Aware Normalization and Verified Clustering in Deduplication:** Disabling digit normalization (`norm_numbers = False`) on mathematical and code corpora preserves parameterized problem diversity (`25/25` vs. `1/25`), hashing exact normalized strings for `< n_grams`-token prompts closes the short-document bypass (`minhash.py:L251`), verifying exact shingle Jaccard similarity on candidate LSH pairs prevents transitive Union-Find over-merging, and stripping punctuation/LaTeX delimiters prior to word $n$-gram matching closes trivial decontamination leaks.

## Boundaries

- **Known not to apply:**
  - `Gerstgrasser et al.`'s $\frac{\pi^2}{6}$ linear-regression `Accumulate` bound **does not apply** to fixed-bandwidth nonparametric KDE (`Kazdan` Theorem 6: variance grows as $\sigma_0^2 + h^2 H_t \to \infty$ unless $h_t \propto (tn)^{-1/5}$) or to fixed-compute closed-loop `Accumulate-Subsample` under tail-truncated decoding (`Probe 2B`: `91.3%` rare-tail recall loss at `top_p = 0.85`).
  - `Ferbach et al.`'s $\lambda < \frac{1}{K-1}$ theorem **does not imply** $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_t \| p_{\text{ref}}) = +\infty$ when $\lambda \ge \frac{1}{K-1}$ on finite-support distributions with $q_0 > 0$ (where $D_{\mathrm{KL}} \le -\log q_0 < \infty$ always holds); rather, $\lambda \ge \frac{1}{K-1}$ forfeits *prior-independent* ($q_0$-uniform) density-ratio control.
  - `ProRL` (`arXiv:2505.24864v1`) **does not universally expand `pass@128/256`** across all task families: on high-baseline math benchmarks where base templates already cover solutions, `ProRL` itself exhibits *Diminished* `pass@128` boundaries (Sec. 4.2, p. 7).
  - `Kazdan et al.`'s `~1,024` clean-example threshold (`arXiv:2410.16713v4` Sec. 4) is an empirical measurement on `Gemma-2-2B` / `HelpSteer2` SFT with `55k` filtered synthetic completions, **not** a task- or scale-invariant universal constant.
- **Unknown:**
  - Whether prolonged RLVR with explicit KL control and periodic $\pi_{\text{ref}}$ resets achieves sustained `pass@256` expansion on multi-step compositional tasks when initialized from a **raw pretrained base model** rather than an already CoT-distilled checkpoint (`DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`) is unresolved across `Yue et al.` and `Liu et al.`.
- **Not examined:**
  - Live execution of upstream distributed LLM training, vLLM rollout generation, or C-extension `xxhash`/`SpaCy` pipelines; multimodal image/diffusion collapse dynamics beyond their comparison roles in `Shumailov et al.`, `Gerstgrasser et al.`, and `Kazdan et al.`; inference-only decoding diversity schedules; continual skill-library admission/eviction; and within-step GRPO advantage-normalization variants.
- **Unsupported:**
  - `nlpxucan/WizardLM@cd4b4cc0` does **not** support multi-epoch (`M = 4`) evolution, the 6th *Complicating Input* operator, or *Elimination Evolving* rejection filters in its published code despite `arXiv:2304.12244v3` Sec. 3.2; `huggingface/datatrove@5484e67a` `MinhashDedupSignature` does **not** emit signatures for normalized documents shorter than `n_grams` tokens (`default < 5` words).
