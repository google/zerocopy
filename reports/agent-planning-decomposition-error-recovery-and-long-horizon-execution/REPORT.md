# Agent Planning, Task Decomposition, Verification-Guided Search, Error Recovery, and Long-Horizon Execution

## Summary

Primary-source inspection of 35 papers and benchmark reports (2022–2026), two live horizon/coherence datasets (`METR TH1.1` YAML and `Vending-Bench 2`), seven pinned public repositories (`OpenHands/software-agent-sdk` and `OpenHands/OpenHands`, `langchain-ai/langgraph`, `google/adk-python`, `SWE-agent/SWE-agent`, `SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent`, `pydantic/pydantic-ai`, and `karthikv792/LLMs-Planning`), and a deterministic four-probe verification suite establishes five operational invariants for designing planning, search, rollback, and loop-control architectures:

1. **Upfront Planning Pays Off Only Under Environment Grounding, Parallel Fan-Out ($W \ge 2$), and Anomaly-Aware Conditional Replanning:** Decoupling planning from tool execution (`ReWOO`, `LLMCompiler`) cuts latency `1.80×–3.74×` and token cost `3.37×–6.73×` on low-surprise parallel DAGs, whereas attaching an ungrounded zero-shot planner to a finetuned executor halves WebArena-Lite success (`36.36% → 17.16%`, `Plan-and-Act` Table 1) and static Plan-and-Execute collapses on post-observation environments (`17.0%` vs. `32.0%` `ReAct` vs. `44.0%` as-needed `ADaPT` on WebShop). Equal-accuracy modeling with billed layer-verification calls and imperfect anomaly detection (`Probe 2`, `[toy model / mechanistic surrogate]`) confirms two hard boundaries: (a) on purely sequential chains ($W = 1$), event-triggered DAG replanning (`22.94 s/succ`) is **slower** than `ReAct` (`21.18 s/succ`, `0.92×` effective speedup, matching `LLMCompiler` WebShop `26.73 s` vs. `19.90 s`), and (b) under implicit semantic tool corruptions without explicit error codes (`ToolMaze` P3/P4: `5.75%–16.75%` unhinted TSR; fault-recovery scaling `+4.88 pp` vs. `+17.85 pp` per $10\times$ parameters), conditional replanning collapses (`67.2% → 20.8%` at $q_{\text{det}} = 0.15$) unless paired with domain-specific semantic validation.
2. **Reasoning Models Do Not Eliminate Verification Asymmetry, Obfuscation Cliffs, or the Soundness Necessity of External Critics:** On `PlanBench` (`karthikv792/LLMs-Planning@fc638a1aff`), `o1-preview` (`97.8%`) and `DeepSeek-R1` (`99.1%`) saturate 3–5 block NL Blocksworld yet drop to `37.3%` and `25.8%` under isomorphic predicate randomization (`Rand. Mystery BW`), miss `73%–84%` of unsatisfiable goals (`2409.13373v1` Table 3), and cost `$42.12/100` instances at `40.4–111.1 s` vs. `Fast Downward`'s `100%` in `0.265 s`. Sound external verifiers (`LLM-Modulo` / `VAL`) guarantee zero invalid plans accepted and drive large scheduling gains (`Robust LLM-Modulo` `GPT-4o` Trip Planning `3.43% → 40.00%`, Calendar `56.10% → 83.30%`), yet remain bounded by generator priors (`10%` recovery on Mystery BW) and action-space pruning (`+15.0 pp` invalid-option filtering on `GPT-4o`).
3. **Live-Environment Tree Search Shrinks Under Compute Matching and Breaks Under Irreversibility ($\gamma > 0$), Where Pre-Execution World-Model Screening Wins:** `SWE-Search` lifts `GPT-4o` SWE-bench Lite `25.7% → 31.0%` at **`14.1×` API cost** (`$40.86 → $576.00`), while on open models compute-matched `Moatless-Adapted Pass@5` beats `SWE-Search Pass@1` on 2/4 models and trails `SWE-Search Pass@5` by only `0.6–3.4 pp` (`5.3 pp` on `GPT-4o-mini`). Real-environment web tree search (`Koh et al.`) incurs `~3×` live actions and `~10×` wall-clock latency (`749–972 s` vs. `68–88 s`, `WebDreamer` Table 2). Under a strictly enforced 16-call budget with identical clean step accuracy (`p_step = 0.72`) across all policies (`Probe 3B`, `[toy model / mechanistic surrogate]`), synchronized checkpoint rewind (`99.9%`) and live tree search (`84.0%` noisy value $q_{\text{val}} = 0.88$; `92.0%` oracle) lead at $\gamma = 0.0$, whereas at $\gamma = 1.0$ mental world-model screening (`Lookahead == Hybrid = 50.9%`) beats reactive/rewind/restart (`13.5%`) and noisy live `TreeSearch` (`10.5%`, which falls *below* single-pass `Continue` because false-negative value rejections trigger extra live irreversible branches).
4. **Long-Horizon Execution Faces a `4×–10.3×` `p50/p80` Reliability Gap (With Wide Overlapping CIs), Belief-State Coherence Meltdowns, Non-Monotonic Continuation Harm, and Scoped Failure-Memory Trade-Offs:** Across `METR TH1.1` (`n = 23` `v1.1` models, post-2023 `p50` doubling `128.7 days`), `p80` horizons lag `p50` by `3.99×–10.29×` ($\beta_m \in [0.595, 1.001]$) and produce `9/253` `p50 vs. p80` point-estimate rank inversions (`Probe 1A`), **but `0/9` inversions are separated at marginal 95% CI** (`Opus 4.6` `p80` `[27.0, 170.4] min` encloses `Gemini 3.1 Pro` `[52.0, 158.6] min`) and slopes are confounded by scaffold (`triframe` `0.873` vs. `react` `0.814`). On `Vending-Bench`, `GPT-4o mini` performs *worse* at `60k` than `10k` memory (Fig. 12) and `7/9` models stagnate *after* filling their 30k-token cap (`Sonnet +51d`), though Table 9's headline `r = 0.167` is sensitive to one outlier (`Gemini 2.0 Pro` removal shifts $r \to +0.810$, `Probe 1B`). In iterative code repair (`Looping Is Not Reliability`, `Probe 3A`), forced revision is non-monotonic (`Qwen2.5-7B` `0.820 → 0.673 → 0.693`, `ever = 0.853`), stale traces resurrect fixed bugs on `Qwen2.5-14B` (`34/135` vs. `4/135` current, `+22.2 pp`, Holm $p = 0.0337$), correlated verifiers ($\phi = 0.641$) inflate `AND`-gate false acceptance `4.64×`, and strict freshness gating cuts sound completion to `0.384`. Failure-memory gains (`AgentRewind` `+15.8` to `+25.6 pp` under unbudgeted oracle validation; `Sentry` `+37%` avg vs. `-0.068` persistent-playbook drop on `Qwen3.5-9B`) depend on failure-triggered gating, state-hash binding, and option pruning.
5. **Production Agent Harnesses Exhibit Concrete Gaps in Loop Bounding, Stuck Detection, and Context–Environment Rollback Synchronization:** Pinned source audits verify: (a) `OpenHands/software-agent-sdk@3a893a1b26` requires `event1.thought == event2.thought` on valid `ActionEvent`s (`L507`, bypassing Scenarios 1, 2-valid, and 4 on 1-token reasoning edits), checks period-2 cycles only (`L461–468`), and leaves Scenario 5 as `return False` (`L485`, `#282`); (b) `langchain-ai/langgraph@c6335a4255` defaults `DEFAULT_RECURSION_LIMIT` to **`10007`** (`_config.py:L42–44`, via an accidental `"1000"` $\to$ `"10000"` $\to$ `"10007"` 3-commit chain), replays pre-`interrupt()` side effects (`types.py:L928`), and checkpoints graph channels without external environment state—an amplification hazard **distinct** from `IAL-Scan`'s static loop findings (`2607.01641v1`, which marks `framework_default_bound` as `Covered` and flags intra-node `while` loops); (c) `google/adk-python@2b90353cd2` sets `LoopAgent.max_iterations = None` (`L80`) and `ReflectAndRetryToolPlugin.extract_error_from_result() -> None` (`L252–277`); and (d) `SWE-agent@3ea751c087` and `mini-swe-agent@04d809ceab` lack semantic loop detectors (`cost_limit = $3.00`, `max_consecutive_execution_timeouts = 3`), whereas `pydantic/pydantic-ai@4ba5d7d1a2` enforces `request_limit = 50` and rewinds failed `FallbackModel` continuations.

#### 1.2 Equal-Accuracy DAG vs. ReAct vs. Replanning Phase Boundary (`Probe 2`, `[toy model / mechanistic surrogate]`)

Holding baseline per-step error strictly equal across all four paradigms ($\varepsilon_{\text{step}} = 0.015$, **zero unearned planner accuracy discount**) over DAG width $W$, sequential depth $D$ ($N = W \cdot D$), per-layer surprise probability $p_{\text{surp}}$, billed layer-monitor LLM cost $c_{\text{chk}} = 1.0$, imperfect surprise recall $q_{\text{det}}$, false-positive trigger rate $q_{\text{fp}} = 0.03$, and finite single-replan recovery $r_{\text{rep}} = 0.88$ establishes four structural results (`Probe 2`):

| Scenario ($W, D, p_{\text{surp}}$) | `ReAct` (`eff_lat` / SR) | `StaticDAG` (`eff_lat` / SR) | `CondReplan_DAG` (`eff_lat` / SR) | `EffSpdUp` (`Static` / `CondDAG` vs. `ReAct`) |
| :--- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `HotpotQA-like` ($2, 1, 0.00$) | `3.9 s` / `97.0%` | `2.4 s` / `97.0%` | `3.3 s` / `97.0%` | **`1.63×`** / `1.17×` |
| `MovieRec-like` ($8, 1, 0.00$) | `14.1 s` / `88.6%` | `2.6 s` / `88.6%` | `3.6 s` / `88.6%` | **`5.41×`** / **`3.90×`** |
| `ParallelQA-like` ($3, 2, 0.05$) | `10.8 s` / `89.4%` | `3.5 s` / `82.4%` | `5.4 s` / `89.4%` | **`3.08×`** / **`2.01×`** |
| `Mixed DAG` ($2, 4, 0.25$) | `19.2 s` / `71.6%` | `14.6 s` / `28.0%` | `12.5 s` / `71.6%` | `1.31×` / **`1.54×`** |
| `WebShop Seq` ($1, 6, 0.35$) | **`21.2 s`** / `58.0%` | **`76.9 s`** / **`6.9%`** | `22.9 s` / `58.0%` | **`0.28×`** / **`0.92×`** |

1. **Effective Latency-per-Success Exposes Open-Loop Collapse:** Reporting raw wall-clock latency hides task failure. At $W = 1, D = 6, p_{\text{surp}} = 0.35$, `StaticDAG` finishes raw execution fast (`5.3 s`) but succeeds only `6.9%` of the time, yielding **`76.9 s` per successful completion** (`0.28×` effective speedup = **`3.6×` worse** than `ReAct`'s `21.2 s/succ`). Across $(W, D)$, `StaticDAG` loses effective latency-per-success to `ReAct` above $p_{\text{surp}}^* = 13.4\%$ ($W=1, D=6$), $31.3\%$ ($W=2, D=4$), and $52.3\%$ ($W=8, D=4$).
2. **Sequential ($W = 1$) Negative Control (`NEG-CTRL 2A`):** Without parallel tool fan-out ($W = 1, D = 6$), `CondReplan_DAG` with semantic LLM layer verification ($c_{\text{chk}} = 1.0$) is **strictly slower** than `ReAct` (`22.94 s` vs. `21.18 s`, while per-step `Plan-and-Act` takes `32.74 s`), reproducing why `LLMCompiler` ran slower than `ReAct` on sequential `WebShop` (`26.73 s` vs. `19.90 s`). Event-triggered DAG compilation wins on latency only when $W \ge 2$ (`1.54×–3.90×` effective speedup) or when surprises are syntactically detectable without extra LLM calls ($c_{\text{chk}} \approx 0$).
3. **Implicit-Failure Blindness & Grounding Controls (`NEG-CTRL 2B`):** Dropping anomaly detection recall from explicit errors ($q_{\text{det}} = 0.92$) to implicit semantic corruptions ($q_{\text{det}} = 0.15$, `ToolMaze` P3/P4) collapses `CondReplan_DAG` success at $W = 2, D = 4, p_{\text{surp}} = 0.35$ from **`67.2%` to `20.8%`**. Adding an ungrounded-planner step penalty ($\Delta\varepsilon_{\text{plan}} = +0.10$) drops `CondReplan_DAG` below `ReAct` even at low surprise (`45.1%` vs. `85.7%`).
