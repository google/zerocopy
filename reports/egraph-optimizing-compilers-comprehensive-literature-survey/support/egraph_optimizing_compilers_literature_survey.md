# E-Graph-Based Optimizing Compilers: A Comprehensive Literature Survey

*Authored by an AI agent acting on joshlf's behalf.*

## Abstract

Optimizing compilers face a foundational tension between **phase-ordering freedom** and **compilation cost, semantic soundness, and target-level fidelity**. Sequential destructive rewriting commits early to local canonicalizations that can obscure downstream optimization opportunities, whereas **equality graphs (e-graphs)** and **equality saturation (EqSat)** maintain an equivalence-closed space of program variants simultaneously, deferring final selection to a global extraction phase. Over the past two decades—and accelerating sharply since 2021—e-graphs have evolved from theorem-prover congruence structures into engines powering production JIT/AOT mid-ends, tensor and sparse-linear-algebra compilers, hardware RTL/logic synthesis pipelines, DSP vectorizers, and formal verification bridges.

Yet primary-source evidence from 2021–2026 demonstrates that classical whole-program equality saturation is no panacea. Production deployment exposes four structural bottlenecks: (1) **control flow and imperative memory effects** (forcing difficult trade-offs between cyclic Program Expression Graphs, reducible Regionalized Value State Dependence Graphs, and CFG skeletons); (2) **extraction complexity and schedule blindness** (optimal DAG extraction with shared subexpressions is NP-hard and inapproximable, ILP solvers routinely time out, and existing fast extractors ignore register interference and pipeline hazards); (3) **combinatorial blowup** under associative-commutative (AC) rules, variable binders, and unbounded substitutions; and (4) **verification and soundness seams** (deferred undefined behavior such as `poison`/`undef` invalidates symmetric equivalence classes, and built-in proof checkers often leave the matching, rebuilding, or extraction engine in the Trusted Computing Base).

This survey synthesizes the foundational theory, production and research compiler architectures, empirical ablations, formal assurance designs, and open engineering frontiers across the e-graph compiler literature, drawing on primary academic works alongside direct source audits and experimental baselines published in the [`google/zerocopy` reference corpus](https://github.com/google/zerocopy/tree/reference) (commit `8fcbe48eb10bfac0016dec083c05f19caf265e90`).

## 1. Foundations: From Congruence Closure to Modern Relational E-Graphs

### 1.1 Congruence Closure and Automated Deduction

An **e-graph** compactly represents a finite congruence relation over a set of ground terms. Formally, an e-graph consists of a set of **e-classes** (equivalence classes of term IDs) and **e-nodes** (operator symbols $f$ applied to child e-class IDs $c_1, \dots, c_k$), maintaining two invariants:
1. **Hash-consing / uniqueness:** no two distinct e-nodes with identical operator and canonicalized child e-classes inhabit different e-classes;
2. **Congruence:** whenever $c_i \equiv c'_i$ for all $1 \le i \le k$, then $f(c_1, \dots, c_k) \equiv f(c'_1, \dots, c'_k)$.

The algorithmic roots trace to two 1980 *J. ACM* papers: **Nelson and Oppen** (*Fast Decision Procedures Based on Congruence Closure*, DOI `10.1145/322186.322198`) and **Downey, Sethi, and Tarjan** (*Variations on the Common Subexpression Problem*, DOI `10.1145/322217.322228`). Nelson and Oppen integrated congruence closure into cooperating decision procedures for program verification, using parent-pointer lists to propagate equivalences upward whenever two subterms are merged. Downey, Sethi, and Tarjan analyzed congruence closure on directed acyclic graphs as a generalization of value numbering and common subexpression detection, establishing near-linear $O(m \log m)$ / $O(m \alpha(m))$ bounds depending on edge-merging and signature-hashing strategies.

Two decades later, **Detlefs, Nelson, and Saxe** (*Simplify: A Theorem Prover for Program Checking*, *J. ACM* 2005, DOI `10.1145/1066100.1066102`) scaled e-graphs to practical static checkers (`ESC/Modula-3`, `ESC/Java`) by introducing **e-matching**: querying universally quantified trigger patterns against an e-graph *modulo known equalities*. Rather than matching syntactically on a rigid term DAG, e-matching allows pattern variables to bind across any representative inside an e-class, laying the operational pattern-matching primitive later adopted by SMT solvers (Z3, CVC5) and equality-saturation engines.

### 1.2 Ordered Completion vs. Non-Destructive Congruence

Equational reasoning systems split sharply on how they handle symmetric or permutative axioms such as commutativity ($x + y \approx y + x$) and associativity ($(x + y) + z \approx x + (y + z)$):

- **Destructive / Unfailing Knuth–Bendix Completion:** Classical Knuth–Bendix completion turns equations into a terminating, confluent term-rewriting system (TRS) under a reduction order $\succ$ (such as Knuth–Bendix Order `KBO` or Lexicographic Path Order `LPO`), aborting on unorientable axioms. **Unfailing completion** (Bachmair, Dershowitz, and Plaisted, 1989)—exemplified in high-performance modern provers such as Smallbone's **Twee** (CADE 2021, DOI `10.1007/978-3-030-79876-5_35`)—retains unorientable equations for ordered paramodulation whenever an instantiated redex satisfies $\sigma(l) \succ \sigma(r)$, discarding critical pairs that join strictly below the overlap peak `cp_top` via model-relativized `KBO`/`LPO` and ground-joinability checks. This yields linear normal-form reduction at the cost of exponential critical-pair search up front.
- **Non-Destructive Equality Saturation:** Instead of orienting rules into a single normal form, equality saturation freezes the entire congruence closure in an e-graph and applies rewrite rules **additively** (creating new e-nodes and calling `union` without deleting prior representations), deferring orientation and term selection to a post-saturation **extraction** phase.

### 1.3 Early E-Graph Superoptimizers

**Massalin** (ASPLOS 1987, DOI `10.1145/36177.36194`) coined *superoptimization* as exhaustive enumeration of short machine instruction sequences against a reference specification—finding counterintuitive bit-twiddling sequences unreachable by peephole compilers, but scaling poorly beyond a handful of instructions.

**Denali** (Joshi, Nelson, and Randall, PLDI 2002, DOI `10.1145/512529.512566`) introduced **goal-directed e-graph superoptimization**: starting from a target straight-line expression, Denali applies algebraic and machine-instruction axioms to grow an e-graph of equivalent computations and translates the saturated e-graph into a SAT instance to extract an optimal (minimum-cycle) instruction sequence for the DEC Alpha EV6. Soon after, **Bansal and Aiken** (ASPLOS 2006, DOI `10.1145/1168857.1168906`) demonstrated automatic peephole-rule discovery by indexing short machine-code patterns and checking equivalence via SAT/SMT, establishing the paradigm of offline superoptimization harvesting reusable compiler peepholes.

### 1.4 Equality Saturation and Program Expression Graphs (PEGs)

While Denali targeted straight-line arithmetic blocks, **Tate, Stepp, Tatlock, and Lerner** (**Peggy**, POPL 2009 / *LMCS* 2011) generalized e-graph rewriting to whole procedures with branches and loops, coining the term **Equality Saturation**. Classical optimizing compilers suffer from the **phase-ordering problem**: choosing an arbitrary pass order forfeits profitable interactions, and optimal pass ordering is undecidable in general (Touati and Barthou, CF 2006, DOI `10.1145/1128022.1128042`), even though combined optimistic analyses strictly dominate finite sequential passes (Click and Cooper, TOPLAS 1995, DOI `10.1145/201059.201061`).

Peggy replaces destructive CFG passes with **Program Expression Graphs (PEGs)**—a referentially transparent, value-centric IR where loop-carried recurrences become cyclic $\theta$-nodes paired with $\text{eval}$ (loop-exit sampling) and $\text{pass}$ (trip-count predicates) operators. Equality saturation repeatedly applies domain-independent and loop-level axioms over the PEG e-graph, and a global Pseudo-Boolean / **Integer Linear Programming (ILP)** solver extracts an optimized PEG converted back to LLVM/Java bytecode (`1.2–6.0 s` per method in Peggy's original evaluation). Peggy established the template for compiler EqSat while exposing its two enduring hurdles: **saturation overhead** from eager per-union invariant rebuilding and **cyclic PEG extraction/re-sequentialization latency**.

### 1.5 `egg`: Amortized Rebuilding and E-Class Analyses

For over a decade, equality saturation remained too slow and rigid for widespread adoption. **Willsey, Nandi, Wang, Flatt, Tatlock, and Panchekha** (**`egg`**, POPL 2021, DOI `10.1145/3434304`) catalyzed the modern e-graph renaissance through two algorithmic breakthroughs implemented in a reusable Rust library:

1. **Amortized Invariant Restoration (`rebuild`):** Classical congruence closure restores hash-consing and congruence invariants eagerly after every single `union`. In equality saturation, rules run in batched read-only e-matching phases before mutating the graph. `egg` defers invariant repair across an entire batch of `union`s and performs a single amortized `rebuild` pass per iteration—yielding orders-of-magnitude speedups (accelerating the floating-point accuracy tool **Herbie**'s simplifier by over **3,000$\times$**).
2. **E-Class Analyses:** A domain-specific semilattice (e.g., constant folding, interval bounds, known-bits, or non-zero facts) attached to each e-class that automatically updates on node insertion and joins monotonically on `union`, unifying abstract interpretation with equational rewriting.

### 1.6 Relational E-Matching and `egglog`

Classic syntactic e-matching traverses the e-graph top-down via backtracking abstract machines (as in `Simplify` or `egg`), which performs redundant work on multi-pattern rules and cyclic graphs. **Zhang, Wang, Willsey, and Tatlock** (*Relational E-Matching*, POPL 2022, DOI `10.1145/3498696`) observed that e-graphs are relational databases—each $k$-ary operator $f$ corresponds to a $(k+1)$-ary table $(c_1, \dots, c_k, c_{\text{out}})$ with functional dependency $(c_1, \dots, c_k) \to c_{\text{out}}$—reducing e-matching to **conjunctive database queries**. Compiling multi-pattern rules with **Worst-Case Optimal Join (WCOJ / Generic Join)** achieves asymptotic speedups on cyclic patterns and eliminates backtracking redundancy.

**Zhang, Wang, Flatt, Cao, Zucker, Rosenthal, Tatlock, and Willsey** (**`egglog`**, PLDI 2023, DOI `10.1145/3591239`) unified Datalog and equality saturation into a single fixpoint engine. In `egglog`, constructors, equivalence classes, and user analyses are all relational tables reconciled monotonically via union-find and custom `:merge` functions. Modern `egglog` (`core-relations`) compiles multi-atom rules via **hypertree decomposition** and **Free Join** (combining WCOJ and binary hash joins over packed tries in a `SharedArena`) and pairs them with adaptive incremental vs. parallel full-scan rebuilding (`do_rebuild` switching at `STEP_SIZE = 2048`). Crucially for compilers, `egglog` natively represents **contextual relations** (dominance, control assumptions, typing relations, and directed refinement edges `Refines(a, b)`) alongside symmetric congruence.

### 1.7 Structural Extensions: ECTA/VSAs, Colored, Slotted, and Guided E-Graphs

Four recent lines of foundational work generalize e-graphs beyond quantifier-free, unconditional, unguided first-order terms:

- **Version-Space Algebras (VSAs) & Equality-Constrained Tree Automata (`ECTA`):** VSAs (Lau et al., 2003; Polozov and Gulwani, OOPSLA 2015) and finite tree automata represent large sets of syntax trees under *independent* subterm choices, breaking down when choices across distinct subterms are coupled (entangled). **Koppel, Guo, de Vries, Solar-Lezama, and Polikarpova** (ICFP 2022) introduced **ECTAs**, augmenting tree automata with path-equality constraints—subsuming VSAs, finite tree automata, and e-graphs in a unified formalism for entangled program spaces (`ecta` / `Hectare`).
- **Colored E-Graphs:** Reasoning under path conditions or case splits ($P \implies a = b$, $\neg P \implies a = c$) traditionally requires cloning the entire e-graph per branch. **Singher and Itzhaky** (`arXiv:2305.19203`, 2023 / FMCAD 2024) introduced **Colored E-Graphs**, annotating equivalence edges with color/assumption sets over a shared e-node core. Across hundreds of case-split assumptions, Colored E-Graphs cut relative e-node overhead by **$\sim 10\times$** compared to cloned e-graphs while sharing common congruences.
- **Slotted E-Graphs:** Languages with variable binders ($\lambda$-abstractions, `let`, quantifiers, array comprehensions) cause severe blowup in standard e-graphs because $\alpha$-equivalent terms with distinct bound names or shifted de Bruijn indices fail to share structure. **Schneider, Rossel, Shaikhha, Goens, Kœhler, and Steuwer** (PLDI 2025, DOI `10.1145/3729326`) introduced **Slotted E-Graphs**, parameterizing e-classes with first-class variable slots (`AppliedId`) and permutation symmetries inside union-find. On `lean-egg` theorem proving (`mathlib4`), slotted e-graphs solve binder-heavy theorems (`not_supIrred`) in `<1 s` where unslotted `egg` exhausts memory (`>4000` steps), cutting average explanation steps on binder benchmarks from `12.7` to `6.77` (`297` vs. `296` solved) while unifying structural sharing in `SDQL` and `RISE`.
- **Guided Equality Saturation:** **Kœhler, Goens, Bhat, Grosser, Trinder, and Steuwer** (POPL 2024, DOI `10.1145/3632900`) address intractable e-graph blowup by staging saturation around a small sequence of human- or solver-supplied intermediate term sketches (**guides**). In the `RISE`/`Elevate` array compiler, unguided `egg` times out after an hour while exhausting **`>60 GB`** of RAM; guided EqSat with **$\le 3$ guides** discovers the target program in seconds using **`<1 GB`** of memory.

## 2. Production and Research Optimizing Compilers

### 2.1 Production JIT/AOT Mid-Ends: Cranelift `aegraphs`

**Cranelift** (the Wasmtime compiler backend, `bytecodealliance/wasmtime`) provides the clearest primary empirical evidence of e-graphs deployed in a fast production JIT/AOT compiler. Chris Fallin's initial prototype using standalone `egg` suffered **`23%` compile-time overhead with zero rewrites** (down from `>2x` initially) solely from IR serialization/deserialization—exceeding the `~10%` total compile-time budget of Cranelift's mid-end (Fallin, EGRAPHS 2023; April 2026 blog ablation).

To meet strict JIT compilation budgets, Cranelift designed **Acyclic E-Graphs (`aegraphs`)** embedded directly inside its SSA `DataFlowGraph`:
- **In-Place Binary `Union` Spines + CFG Skeleton:** Pure operations (`is_pure_for_egraph`: single-result, non-trapping, and either memory-free or `readonly && notrap && can_move`) are detached from basic blocks into a transient sea-of-nodes while side-effecting instructions remain pinned in a CFG skeleton (optimized separately via `SkeletonInstSimplification` + disjoint `AliasRegion` analysis). Equivalent representations share the SSA `Value` index space via immutable binary `ValueDef::Union(x, y)` nodes—incurring zero overhead for singleton e-classes.
- **Single-Pass Domtree-Preorder Rewriting & Hard Caps:** Instead of fixpoint saturation and global `rebuild()`, `EgraphBlockIter` traverses the dominator tree once in preorder, querying a scoped `ScopedHashMap` GVN table and applying prioritized **ISLE** rules (`cranelift/codegen/src/opts/*.isle`) under hard caps (`ECLASS_ENODE_LIMIT = 5`, `MATCHES_LIMIT = 5`, `REWRITE_LIMIT = 5`, `EXTRACTOR_FUEL = 500`). General commutativity and associativity rules are explicitly prohibited (`opts/README.md` Rule 3) in favor of directional canonicalization.
- **SSA Dominance Invariant (`subsume`):** Any rewrite dropping a non-constant SSA `Value` operand from LHS to RHS (e.g., `(band x 0) => 0` or `(select 1 x y) => x`) **must** wrap its result in `(subsume ...)` (`opts/README.md` Rule 2) to replace the e-class rather than unioning `orig` and `rewritten`; otherwise, GVN can unify the unioned e-class with a dominating block where `x` is out of SSA scope (formally verified in `aegraph_unsubsumed_union_violates_dominance`).
- **Scoped Elaboration:** `compute_best_values` runs single-pass bottom-up tree-sum DP over `Cost(u32)` (`Iconst=1`, simple ALU `=3`, `Imul=10`, default `=4`, trap/effect `+10`, load `+20`, store `+50`), and `Elaborator` materializes pure nodes on demand using `available_block`, `active_loop_depth` preheader LICM (`loop_hoist_level`, strictly requiring `!can_trap()` because preheaders execute on zero-trip loops), and 14 `remat.isle` rematerialization rules.

**Fallin's April 2026 Empirical Ablation:** On Sightglass WebAssembly benchmarks comparing `aegraph` against an identical-ruleset classical GVN+LICM pipeline, `aegraph` achieves **`~2%` execution speedup** at **`+7–8%` compile-time cost** (grown from near parity in 2023 as hundreds of ISLE rules accumulated). Crucially, ablating `ValueDef::Union` (destructively retaining only the single lowest-cost rewrite at each step) reveals that **the transient sea-of-nodes-with-CFG-skeleton drives virtually the entire `~2%` gain**: multi-representation `Union` nodes + DP extraction add only **`~0.1%` runtime speedup** (at `~0.005%` compile time), averaging **`1.13` e-nodes per e-class** with only **2** missed eager sub-optimalities across `~4M` value nodes (both from one non-cost-monotone `ireduce`-of-`iadd` rule in `spidermonkey.wasm`). Open Wasmtime issues (`#6159`, `#6260`, `#8529`, `#8959`) further document SIMD inner-loop pipeline stalls, `imul` duplication across `brif` arms, and the ongoing difficulty of unifying mid-end `aegraphs` with target `lower.isle` instruction selection (`#7321` / `#8529`). Furthermore, inspecting `rustc_codegen_cranelift` (`vtable.rs:8-12`) exposed a subtle frontend-backend seam bug: `vtable_memflags()` sets `MemFlags::trusted()` and `set_readonly()` without `set_can_move()`, causing `is_pure_for_egraph` to reject all Rust vtable loads and forfeit `aegraph` CSE/hoisting across stack stores.

### 2.2 Whole-Function Effectful EqSat: `eggcc` and `Statewalk DP`

While Cranelift avoids cyclic saturation and CFG restructuring, **`eggcc`** (Flatt, Pal, Zhang, Tjoa, Graham, Fischman, Nandi, Rosenthal, Tatlock, and Ni, OOPSLA 2026, `arXiv:2609.31887v2`, DOI `10.1145/3839530`) tackles whole-function imperative optimization in `egglog` by lowering reducible CFGs into a **Regionalized Value State Dependence Graph (RVSDG)** (`If`, `Switch`, `DoWhile` regions threaded with explicit linear state edges `StateT`) and encoding path-sensitive facts via `Assumption` placeholder contexts (`add_context.rs`):

- **Effect Extraction via `Statewalk DP`:** Selecting an extracted DAG in an RVSDG requires finding a **valid total ordering of effectful nodes** consistent with `StateT` dependencies—a problem that causes COIN-OR `CBC` to fail across PolyBench and `Gurobi` to hit 5-minute timeouts or infeasibility reports. `eggcc` solves this via **`Statewalk DP`**, a dynamic program that is fixed-parameter tractable (`O(k n^2)`) in the **statewalk width $k$** (number of live independent state tokens crossing a cut). Across benchmarks, `94.7%` of regions have $k=1$ (`98.4% <= 5`), though two `heat-3d` stencil regions with massive unaliased load/store parallelism spike to $k = 106,259$ (requiring live-state bitmask and `SATELLITE_BAR=6` pruning). `Statewalk DP` achieves a **`520x` geometric-mean extraction speedup over Gurobi**.
- **Memory & Substitution Boundaries:** Primary-source inspection of `eggcc` reveals sharp practical limits on imperative EqSat:
  1. The rich points-to `memory.egg` ruleset is commented out in `schedule.rs` in favor of `mem_simple.egg`, which itself comments out load-past-store commutation and pure-load state bypass because they break the `WeaklyLinear` state-edge invariant when stored values depend on loaded values (with `(union (Get load 1) state)` moved to `non_weakly_linear.egg` under `Statewalk DP`).
  2. Context substitution is hard-capped at `(repeat 5 ...)` (`schedule.rs:57`) because dead terms diverge under substitution.
  3. Evaluated on PolyBench and Bril (using LLVM 18 for `sroa`, `-regalloc=greedy`, ISel, and scheduling), `eggcc` yields **`0.571` vs. `0.326`** (PolyBench) and **`0.876` vs. `0.695`** (Bril) normalized runtime against `LLVM-O3-O0` (`0.312`/`0.542` with LLVM target-specific opt)—trailing overall due to missing scalar promotion, generalized loop unrolling, and stack coloring—while achieving dramatic wins where domain rules eliminate loops outright (`6.52x` speedup over `LLVM-O3-O3` on Fenwick `lowbit`, collapsing the loop via `hackers_delight.egg` to `n & (-n)`).

### 2.3 MLIR Integrations: `DialEgg`, `Tamagoyaki`, and `SEER`

Integrating equality saturation into extensible multi-level compiler infrastructure (**MLIR**, Lattner et al., CGO 2021) exhibits three distinct architectural points:

- **`DialEgg`** (Zayed and Dubach, CGO 2025, DOI `10.1145/3696443.3708957`): A dialect-agnostic MLIR `egg-opt` prototype that text-serializes MLIR regions/blocks into `(Reg (vec-of (Blk (vec-of ...))))`, writes temporary files, and shells out per block via `std::system("egglog ...")`. Source inspection reveals both IPC serialization overhead and a strict single-result requirement (`README.md:65`; `Egglog.cpp:864-868` logs multi-result ops without skipping, falling through to opaque wrappers).
- **`Tamagoyaki`** (Merckx, Lopoukhine, Coward, De Sutter, Cheng, and Grosser, `arXiv:2602.16707v2`, 2026): Avoids external serialization by embedding e-graphs natively inside MLIR via `equivalence.{graph,class,yield}` + `ematch` + multi-pattern `pdl_interp` bytecode (`2.38x` multi-pattern e-matching speedup; `1.18x` geomean vs. Herbie excluding bytecode load, `0.78x` including load; `-11%` delay and `-6%` area on CIRCT `ShiftedFma`). However, Tamagoyaki explicitly scopes its representation to **pure, straight-line code fragments** (`Section 4.1`), leaving arbitrary CFG/memory saturation out of scope.
- **`SEER`** (Cheng, Coward, Chelini, Barbalho, and Drane, ASPLOS 2024): Applies MLIR + multi-root equality saturation to **High-Level Synthesis (HLS)**, orchestrating software compiler passes and datapath transformations concurrently to achieve up to **`38x` performance** within `~1.4x` hardware area (surpassing human-expert RTL on an industrial case study).

### 2.4 Tensor, Sparse, Array, and Floating-Point Compilers

Equality saturation thrives in pure, side-effect-free algebraic/tensor domains where phase ordering dominates:
- **`TENSAT`** (Yang, Phothilimthana, Wang, Willsey, Roy, and Pienaar, MLSys 2021): Replaces TASO's sequential backtracking search with `egg` equality saturation + multi-pattern matching + cycle-filtered ILP extraction, yielding up to **`16%` faster runtime** on deep learning computation graphs while reducing optimization time by **`48x` on average**.
- **`Glenside`** (Smith et al., MAPS 2021): Introduces a pure *access-pattern* tensor IR without explicit loop nests, enabling `egg` to discover im2col lowering, data-layout transformations, and systolic/GEMM kernel mappings purely via algebraic rewrites.
- **`SpEQ`** (Laird, Liu, Bjørner, and Dehnavi, PLDI 2024, DOI `10.1145/3656445`): Applies equality saturation to sparse tensor algebra, lifting low-level sparse loops/codes to high-performance vendor primitives (`cuSPARSE`, `MKL`).
- **`RISE` & `Elevate`** (Hagedorn et al., ICFP 2020): A functional array IR paired with a compositional rewrite-strategy DSL, later coupled with Guided Equality Saturation (POPL 2024) to overcome binder/distributive blowup.
- **`Herbie`** (Panchekha, Sanchez-Stern, Wilcox, and Tatlock, PLDI 2015): Floating-point accuracy synthesis, whose algebraic expression simplification phase became **`>3,000x` faster** upon adopting `egg` (POPL 2021).

### 2.5 Hardware RTL, Logic Synthesis, and CIRCT

Hardware datapaths and combinational logic networks lack heap aliasing and register-allocation interference, making DAG-cost equality saturation especially potent:
- **`ROVER`** (Coward, Drane, and Constantinides, *IEEE TCAD* 2024, DOI `10.1109/TCAD.2024.3410154`): Formulates mixed-precision RTL datapath optimization via `egg` rewriting + theoretical hardware cost models + ILP extraction, achieving up to **`63%` area reduction** while emitting back-end equivalence-checking verification scripts.
- **`E-Syn`** (Chen, Hu, Zuo, Yu, Ma, and Zhang, DAC 2024, DOI `10.1145/3649329.3656246`): Brings equality saturation to multi-level Boolean logic synthesis with technology-aware cost functions, reporting **`~15.29%` delay reduction** (delay mode) and **`~6.42%` area savings** (area mode) over conventional greedy And-Inverter Graph (AIG) flows.
- **CIRCT:** The LLVM-incubator Circuit IR Compilers and Tools project (`synth` AIG/MIG, `comb`, `hw` dialects) increasingly serves as a substrate for hardware e-graph integrations such as `Tamagoyaki` (`ShiftedFma`) and `SEER`.

### 2.6 Instruction Selection, Rule Synthesis, and SMT Superoptimization Contrast

- **DSP Vectorizing ISel (`Diospyros`, `Isaria`, `Ruler`):** **Diospyros** (VanHattum et al., ASPLOS 2021, DOI `10.1145/3445814.3446707`) pairs symbolic evaluation with `egg` over hand-written rules to vectorize irregular DSP linear-algebra kernels (**`3.1x` average speedup**). **Ruler** (Nandi et al., OOPSLA 2021) turns equality saturation onto *rule synthesis itself*, automatically inferring minimal, sound algebraic/ISA rulesets from an interpreter specification. **Isaria** (Thomas and Bornholt, ASPLOS 2024) couples `Ruler` with automated rule phasing and pruning to synthesize vectorizing compilers from ISA specifications, outperforming hand-tuned DSP libraries by up to **`6.9x`**.
- **Compiler Theorem Proving & Inverse Decompilation (`Caviar`, `Szalinski`):** **Caviar** (Kourta et al., CC 2022, DOI `10.1145/3497776.3517781`) replaces greedy precondition proving in the **Halide** image-processing compiler with an e-graph TRS. **Szalinski** (Nandi et al., PLDI 2020, DOI `10.1145/3385412.3386012`) pairs `egg` with *inverse transformations* to decompile flat CSG meshes into structured `map`/`fold` CAD programs.
- **SMT Superoptimizers (`Souper`, `Hydra`, and the `Hydride` Distinction):** Not all synthesizing instruction selectors or peephole optimizers use e-graphs. **Souper** (Sasnauskas et al., 2017, `arXiv:1711.04422`) and **Hydra** (Mukherjee and Regehr, OOPSLA 2024, DOI `10.1145/3649837`) synthesize and width-generalize LLVM IR peephole rewrites via SMT bitvector solvers (`Z3`/`Alive2`-style semantics) rather than congruence closure. Crucially, **Hydride** (Kothari et al., ASPLOS 2024)—often cited alongside `Diospyros` and `Isaria` in SIMD vectorization—uses **Rosette + Z3 SMT CEGIS synthesis** over AutoLLVM IR (`synthesis/`), *not* equality saturation or e-graphs.

## 3. Core Technical Challenges and Architectural Taxonomy

### 3.1 Control Flow, Loops, and Stateful Memory Effects

Representing control flow and imperative side effects inside an e-graph creates a fundamental architectural divergence across compiler IRs:

| Encoding Strategy | Representative Systems | How Control & Memory Are Encoded | Structural Strength | Observed Failure Mode / Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cyclic PEG** | `Peggy` (POPL 2009) | Cyclic $\theta/\text{eval}/\text{pass}$ loop nodes + heap tokens | Captures loop-level algebraic equivalences uniformly | NP-hard cyclic ILP extraction (`1.2–6.0 s`/method); cross-loop bisimulation reconstruction |
| **Reducible RVSDG + Linear `StateT`** | `eggcc` (OOPSLA 2026), `jlm` (TECS 2020) | Nested acyclic `If`/`Switch`/`DoWhile` ($\gamma/\theta$) + linear state tokens + `Assumption` contexts | Eliminates dominator/loop-finder churn (`21%` of LLVM 7.0.1 `-O3` passes in Reissmann et al.); local `DNE`/`CNE`/`PSH` | Irreducible-CFG restructuring injects synthetic predicates (`2.463x` dynamic branches in 2-entry probe); coarse state edges serialize independent loads (`WeaklyLinear` fragility in `eggcc`); `heat-3d` $k=106,259$ spike |
| **Pure Sea-of-Nodes + CFG Skeleton** | Cranelift `aegraph` (2023–2026) | Side-effecting ops remain pinned in CFG blocks; pure ops float in `ValueDef::Union` | $O(N)$ single-pass domtree walk; zero CFG restructuring; avoids V8 `TurboFan` pathology (`3–7x` L1 dcache misses, `190x` slower LoadElim vs `Turboshaft` CFG) | Requires `subsume` on operand-dropping rules; misses cross-block control-flow transformations without separate CFG passes |
| **Straight-Line / Per-Block MLIR** | `Tamagoyaki` (2026), `DialEgg` (CGO 2025) | `equivalence` region inside pure straight-line ops (`Tamagoyaki`) or external block dump (`DialEgg`) | Interleaves cleanly with MLIR traits & `pdl_interp` (`Tamagoyaki`) | `Tamagoyaki` excludes control flow/memory (`§4.1`); `DialEgg` pays per-block IPC and mishandles multi-result ops |

Three invariant hazards recur across imperative e-graphs:
1. **SSA Dominance Violations (`subsume`):** Unioning `(band x 0)` with `0` without `subsume` lets GVN hoist a `Union` reference into a dominating block where `x` is undefined (`aegraph_unsubsumed_union_violates_dominance`).
2. **Speculation / Zero-Trip Loop Traps:** Hoisting an instruction into a loop preheader (`idom` in Cranelift `elaborate.rs`) executes it unconditionally—even when trip count $k=0$. Thus `is_pure_for_egraph` must enforce `!can_trap()`, or hoisting `udiv` introduces spurious division-by-zero traps (`canary_hoist_trapping_div_above_zero_iteration_loop_unsound`).
3. **Aliasing vs. State Linearization:** In `eggcc`, allowing a load to commute past a store or bypass a state token breaks `WeaklyLinear` when stored values depend on loaded results, forcing `schedule.rs` to disable `memory.egg` in favor of conservative `mem_simple.egg` + guarded `non_weakly_linear.egg`.

### 3.2 Extraction, Cost Modeling, and Register/Schedule Blindness

Once saturated, an e-graph encodes exponentially many equivalent programs; choosing an optimal concrete program exposes a stark algorithmic chasm:

- **Tree Cost ($O(|E| \log |V|)$ / Bellman–Ford) vs. DAG Cost (NP-Hard):** Built-in extractors in `egg` (`AstSize`, `AstDepth`), `egglog` (`TreeExtractor`), `slotted-egraphs`, and Cranelift (`compute_best_values`) compute **tree costs**, summing child costs on every use. This runs in milliseconds (or sub-ms in Cranelift), but overcounts shared common subexpressions—and in Cranelift (`elaborate.rs:428-455`) can saturate `u32` cost to `Cost::infinity()` on shared DAG chains. When shared sub-DAGs are charged once globally (**DAG cost**), extraction is **NP-hard and inapproximable within any constant factor** via reduction from Minimum Set Cover (Sun, Zhang, and Ni, `arXiv:2408.17042`; Fallin 2026).
- **The Zero-Grounded Cycle Paradox in ILP:** Formulating DAG extraction as a binary ILP (`class.active`, `node.variable`) without acyclicity constraints permits detached self-supporting cycles ($c_a \leftrightarrow c_b$) disconnected from ground leaves to win with artificially tiny costs (e.g., `2.0` cyclic vs. `16.5` true acyclic DAG vs. `31.5` tree in a 4-class benchmark). `extraction-gym`'s `faster_ilp_cbc.rs` combats this via preprocessing plus iterative cycle-banning cuts ($\sum_{e \in C} x_e \le |C| - 1$); because COIN-OR CBC lacks incremental warm-start cut callbacks, hard cyclic e-graphs trigger repeated solver restarts running past **`>36,000 s` (10 hours)**.
- **Exact FPT and Differentiable Extractors:**
  - **Cyclic Monotone Boolean Circuit Treewidth DP (`glenn-sun/egg-extraction-gym`, `arXiv:2408.17042`):** Equates e-graph extraction to finding a minimum-weight well-founded satisfying assignment of a cyclic `Or`/`And`/`Variable` monotone circuit, solved exactly in **$2^{O(w^2)} \mathrm{poly}(w, n)$** time via nice tree decomposition of width $w$ (`KnownTag` + `TransitiveGraph` cycle rejection)—scaling in ms–seconds for low treewidth ($w \le 8$) while hitting a $2^{\Theta(w^2)}$ wall on dense arithmetic meshes ($w \gtrsim 12$). *(Primary source audit of `circuit.rs:69-72` also exposed a subtle bug in `contract_edge_remove_out`, where an unconditional `self.add_edge(u, w)` immediately follows `if u != w { self.add_edge(u, w); }`, injecting spurious self-loops on 2-cycles.)*
  - **`Statewalk DP` (`eggcc`, OOPSLA 2026):** Achieves $O(k n^2)$ FPT exactness over effectful RVSDGs (`520x` faster than Gurobi), pricing extracted DAGs via static operator bags (`SCost`).
  - **Differentiable GPU & Hybrid Solvers (`SmoothE`, `e-boost`):** **SmoothE** (Cai et al., ASPLOS 2025, DOI `10.1145/3669940.3707262`) relaxes discrete e-node selection to GPU gradient descent + acyclicity penalties on A100 GPUs (`4.6±0.0 s` on `impress` to `24.4±2.5 s` avg on `tensat`, `42.4±0.7 s` on `NASRNN`). **e-boost** (Yin et al., `arXiv:2508.13020v2`) combines adaptive heuristics with CP-SAT/Gurobi, accelerating logic/FV extraction `558x` over vanilla ILP and `138x` over ASPLOS'25 `SmoothE` (`19.04%` lower cost, though updated `SmoothE` beats `e-boost+CPLEX` by `7.8%`/`1.7%` under 60 s caps on `boole`/`emorphic`), yet still reaches `3,599.09 s` (`mul128` CP-SAT) and `3,600.83 s` (`mul48` Gurobi) on `73k–1.2M`-node circuits.
- **Register-Pressure and Schedule Blindness:** **No existing fast extractor models finite register pressure, interference graphs, or CPU pipeline hazards.** A deterministic probe (`probe_egraph_backend.py`) over symmetric $W$-wide dual-reduction DAGs shows DAG sharing cuts operations **`1.68x`** at $W=8$ (`47` DAG ops vs. `79` rematerialized-tree ops), yet naive postorder DFS hits **`peak_regs = 12`** (spilling on an 8-register target) whereas schedule-aware interleaving holds **`peak_regs = 7`**, and exact subset-DP cuts $W=4$ peak live registers `8 -> 7 -> 6`. Production Cranelift bugs (`#6159` SIMD loop stalls, `#6260` scheduling, `#8959` `imul` duplication across `brif`) corroborate that cost-only extraction systematically misprices live ranges and rematerialization.

### 3.3 E-Graph Blowup, AC Combinatorics, and Compile-Time Budgets

Unguided equality saturation runs past general-purpose compiler latency and memory limits along three axes:

1. **Associativity-Commutativity (AC) Combinatorics:** Saturating an $n$-term sum $a_1 + \dots + a_n$ under $(x+y)=(y+x)$ and $((x+y)+z)=(x+(y+z))$ generates $2^n - 1$ e-classes and super-exponentially many e-nodes (`egg 0.11.0`: `15` e-classes / `63` e-nodes / `123 µs` at $n=4$ $\to$ **`202` e-classes / `2,627` e-nodes / `3,593 µs`** at $n=7$; deterministic Python probe `Probe A`: `127` e-classes / `1,939` e-nodes / `181.08 ms` uncapped vs. **`49` e-classes / `154` e-nodes / `1.29 ms`** under Cranelift-style `5/5` caps). General compilers therefore ban unconstrained AC axioms during saturation (`Cranelift opts/README.md`) or normalize associative chains into sorted $n$-ary canonical forms.
2. **Variable Binders and $\alpha$-Renaming:** Unslotted e-graphs blow up when de Bruijn shifts or distinct binder names fracture sharing (`RISE` `>60 GB` / 1h timeout without POPL 2024 guides; `lean-egg` OOM on `not_supIrred`), motivating Slotted E-Graphs or Guided EqSat.
3. **Unbounded Substitution / Dead Code:** Even bounded contexts diverge under unrestricted substitution, forcing `eggcc` to hard-cap substitution at `(repeat 5 ...)` (`schedule.rs:57`).

### 3.4 Verification, Proof Production, and Translation Validation

How can an optimizing compiler leverage e-graph search without absorbing hundreds of thousands of lines of Rust matchers, hash-consers, ILP solvers, or SMT engines into its **Trusted Computing Base (TCB)**?

#### Proof Forests and DAG-Shared `TreeExplanation` Certificates
Naive path-compressing union-find destroys merge provenance. **Nieuwenhuis and Oliveras** (RTA 2005 / *J. ACM* 2007, DOI `10.1145/1284320.1284324`) recover equational proofs via an uncompressed **directed proof forest** (`make_leader` edge reversal on union), nearest-common-ancestor (`NCA`) traversal, and an auxiliary `HighestNode` union-find avoiding exponential re-traversal of overlapping congruence paths. **`egg`** (`src/explain.rs`) adapts this with memoized subtree caching (`ExplainCache`), `let`-shared `TreeExplanation`, and **Flatt, Coward, Willsey, Tatlock, and Panchekha**'s (FMCAD 2022) greedy proof-size shortcutting (`CONGRUENCE_LIMIT = 2`, `GREEDY_NUM_ITERS = 2`, cutting congruence-chain DAG steps `4.0x` from `8 -> 2`).

Quantitative benchmarking on `egg 0.11.0` shows why **DAG-shared certificates are mandatory**: when a rewritten subterm sits beneath a $d$-deep shared binary DAG (`dup^d`), unshared `FlatExplanation` chains blow up as $2^{d+3}-1$, reaching **`319` steps (`289,986 B`)** at $d=6$ versus **`10` steps (`2,537 B`, `114.3x` smaller)** for `let`-shared `TreeExplanation` (`256` flat steps vs. `9` DAG steps at depth $K=8$ on doubling congruence).

#### Fail-Open Pitfalls in Built-In Checkers
Direct source inspection of upstream engines shows their built-in proof checkers cannot act as high-assurance TCB oracles:
- **`egg` (`src/explain.rs:368-442, 780-856`):** `Explanation::check_proof` **fails open (`true`)** whenever a rule is missing from the table (`// give up when the rule is not provided`), whenever a dynamic/constant-folding `Applier` returns `None` from `get_pattern_ast()`, or on `union_trusted` labels, while `FlatTerm::rewrite` panics on unbound RHS variables.
- **`egglog` (`src/proofs/{proof_format,proof_checker,proof_encoding_helpers}.rs`):** Hash-consed `TermDag`/`ProofStore` checking rejects `:unsafe-seminaive` rules and derives reflexivity only on action-touched terms, yet executes inside unverified Rust rather than a formal proof assistant kernel.

#### Soundness Under Deferred Undefined Behavior (`PoisonOr` and `freeze`)
In compiler IRs with deferred undefined behavior (`LLVM IR`, `MiniSSA`), rules that drop a potentially poisoned operand—such as $x \times 0 \sqsubseteq 0$, $x \ \& \ 0 \sqsubseteq 0$, $x - x \sqsubseteq 0$, or dropping `nuw`/`nsw` flags—are **strictly directional refinements (`⊑`), not symmetric equalities**. Mechanization in Lean `v4.34.1` (`EGraphRefinementProofs.lean`, theorem `symmetric_eclass_poison_unsound`) proves that placing `mulZero x` and `const 0` in a symmetric e-class allows backward rewriting `0 -> poison * 0`, turning defined `0` into `.poison`. Sound proof-producing EqSat therefore requires a **two-stratum decomposition**:
1. **`BiEqRule` (Bidirectional Poison-Exact Equalities):** $\llbracket \text{lhs} \rrbracket = \llbracket \text{rhs} \rrbracket$ ($x + 0 \leftrightarrow x$, `sh2add`, `andn`, commutativity), admissible inside symmetric e-classes;
2. **`DirRefineRule` (Forward-Only Refinements):** $\llbracket \text{lhs} \rrbracket \sqsubseteq \llbracket \text{rhs} \rrbracket$ ($x \times 0 \to 0$), represented as directed relations in `egglog` or forward post-extraction steps.

Furthermore, because `freeze` is **non-monotonic** in `⊑` (`poison ⊑ val 5`, yet `freeze 0 poison = val 0` does not refine `freeze 0 (val 5) = val 5`), lifting directed refinements across a shared `let`-DAG requires guarding against `freeze` ancestors (`isFreezeFree` in `SideCondCert/AEGraph.lean`).

#### Rule Checkers vs. Post-Extraction Translation Validation
Existing compiler verification tools split on what sits inside the TCB:

- **Local Rule Verifiers (`VeriISLE`/`Crocus`, `Arrival`, `Lean-MLIR`, `eggcc/proofs`):**
  - **Crocus / VeriISLE** (VanHattum et al., ASPLOS 2024, DOI `10.1145/3617232.3624862`) and **Arrival** (McLoughlin et al., OOPSLA 2025, DOI `10.1145/3764383`) verify Cranelift ISLE rules against Sail/ARM ASL specs via `cvc5`/`z3`: `1,054` in-scope / `1,331` total `opt` expansions (`5,134` instantiations) pass in `~4 m`. Yet `verify.sh` excludes `vector`, `atomics`, `spectre`, `narrowfloat`, `amode_const`, `i128`, `wasm_category_stack`, and `simplify_skeleton`, checks only `x64-iadd-base-case` on x64 in CI, relies on global `relax_nan` flags (unsound if float ops feed integers), trusts unverified SMT solvers, and verifies **zero lines** of `egraph/mod.rs`, `elaborate.rs`, or `alias_analysis.rs`.
  - **`eggcc/proofs/proofs.v`** comprises 240 lines of Coq 8.19.2 proving 4 `Ctx` theorems on a toy AST lacking `Load`/`Store`, RVSDG lowering, `mem_simple.egg`, and `Statewalk DP`.
  - **`Lean-MLIR`** (Bhat et al., `arXiv:2407.03685`) kernel-checks SSA peephole rewrites (`denote_rewritePeepholeAt`, `EffectKind`, `SLLVM`) in Lean 4 without verifying e-graph saturation or DAG extraction.
- **Pure-Kernel Reflection Checkers (`CongruenceDag`):** Under strict kernel axiom boundaries permitting only `{propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}` (banning `grind`, `omega`, `bv_decide`, and `native_decide` per-invocation native axioms), serializing `egg` explanations into a closed, `fvar`-free 5-constructor arena checker (`refl`, `symm`, `trans`, `cong`, `rule` with `SortTag` and `sideProofs`) activates Lean 4's C++ `!has_fvar` GMP/WHNF fast path (`type_checker.cpp:1093, 1181`), checking multi-sorted `Bool`/`Nat`/`BitVec`/byte-codec congruences in `<0.5 ms` by `Eq.refl true`—paralleling how **IsaFoR / CeTA** (Thiemann and Sternagel, TPHOLs 2009 / UITP 2014) extracts an Isabelle/HOL-verified Haskell checker for XML `CPF` termination/confluence certificates emitted by untrusted provers.
- **Post-Extraction Translation Validation (`Alive2`, `Chamois-CompCert` `BTL`):** **Alive2** (Lopes et al., PLDI 2021, DOI `10.1145/3453483.3454030`) validates bounded LLVM IR refinement across `poison`/`undef`/`memory`, while **Chamois-CompCert** (`BTL`, Gourdin et al., OOPSLA 2023, DOI `10.1145/3622799`) validates arbitrary untrusted superblock CSE/reordering/LICM/memory forwarding via verified hash-consed symbolic execution in Coq—keeping the entire optimizer (whether `aegraph`, `egglog`, or scheduler) **completely outside the TCB**.

### 4.2 Three Non-Dominated Architecture Regimes

Synthesizing empirical measurements across Cranelift, `eggcc`, MLIR, and formal verification yields three non-dominated backend designs—while refuting monolithic all-in-one whole-function `egglog` + GPU extraction + unified `MachInst`+regalloc e-graphs for general-purpose compilation:

1. **Regime A — General-Purpose Fast JIT/AOT (`aegraph` + Decoupled `ISLE` + Post-Pass TV):** Embed a bounded acyclic e-graph (`ECLASS_ENODE_LIMIT`, no general AC) directly on a persistent CFG side-effect skeleton (`+7–8%` compile time), keep target lowering (`lower.isle`) and register allocation (`regalloc2`) decoupled, and place **0% of the e-graph matcher or `Elaborator` in the TCB** via post-pass CFG/symbolic Translation Validation (`BTL`/`Alive2`/`Lean-MLIR`-style) plus kernel-checked rule/regalloc checkers (`regalloc2::checker`, `CongruenceDag`).
2. **Regime B — Domain & Pure-Kernel Superoptimization (`RVSDG`/`Slotted` + `Statewalk DP`/Guided EqSat):** Deploy bounded `RVSDG` + `Statewalk DP` ($k \le 5$ on `98.4%` of regions), `Slotted E-Graphs`, or `Guided EqSat` selectively on pure computational kernels, DSP/SIMD blocks (`Diospyros`/`Isaria`), bit-twiddling idioms (`eggcc` `hackers_delight`), tensor graphs (`TENSAT`), RTL datapaths (`ROVER`), or binder-heavy IRs, discharging soundness via per-kernel `CongruenceDag`/refinement certificates before lowering.
3. **Regime C — Hybrid Verified Systems Compiler:** Retain CFG/SSA lowering, pressure-aware post-egraph scheduling, and verified register allocation as the compiler spine; run fast bounded `aegraph` canonicalization globally, invoke bounded superoptimization selectively on hot pure/SIMD slices, and verify end-to-end via decoupled Translation Validation + pure-kernel certificate replay.

### 4.3 Open Research and Engineering Problems

1. **Sub-Millisecond Register-Pressure- and Schedule-Aware DAG Extraction:** No existing extractor bridges tree DP (`O(n log n)`), `Statewalk DP` (`O(k n^2)`), and NP-hard DAG sharing with interference coloring, spill cost, and pipeline latency (`Probe B` `12` vs `7` live regs; Cranelift `#6159`/`#6260`/`#8959`). Developing bounded-treewidth or heuristic DAG extractors that co-optimize rematerialization, shared-value liveness, and instruction latency under `<1 ms` budgets remains open.
2. **Scalable Alias-Partitioned Memory Encodings for Effectful EqSat:** `eggcc`'s `WeaklyLinear` fragility (`memory.egg` disabled) and `heat-3d`'s $k=106,259$ statewalk explosion highlight the need for fine-grained alias-sliced `MemorySSA`/separation-logic tokens inside relational e-graphs that neither serialize independent memory regions nor violate state linearity.
3. **Unifying Mid-End `aegraphs` with Target Instruction Selection:** Solving the forward-GVN vs. backward-lowering direction conflict (`wasmtime#8529` / `#7321`) cleanly—potentially via structured atomic lowering regions (`InstColor`) or unified `MiniMach`-style target IRs—without blowing up e-class sizes or breaking `subsume` dominance.
4. **Native Poison-Monotonic E-Graphs (`egglog` + `0`-TCB `CongruenceDag`):** Building compilers that emit closed, `fvar`-free `CongruenceDag` + `SideCondCert` certificates directly from bounded `aegraph` or directed-refinement `egglog` runs—closing the TCB gap left by `VeriISLE` (unverified `egraph/elaborate` + SMT solver) and `egg`'s fail-open `check_proof`.
5. **Compositional Inductive Loop Superoptimization:** Extending intra-block and bounded-unroll EqSat to loop-carrying recurrences and vectorization (`LoopVectorize`/VPlan) with compiler-emitted inductive invariants rather than brittle bounded unrolling.
