# R563/R564 rustc MIR behavior on two installed nightlies

## Scope and provenance

This supplements the published [R563 MIR-stage report](../rustc-mir-before-charon-nightly-2026-05-31/REPORT.md) (SHA-256 `00affbc2b248156168c560f67d38a8c1e5c5f0e19ffb9f22532a276d126f09b0`) and [R564 safety-operation report](../rustc-mir-unsafe-operations-nightly-2026-05-31/REPORT.md) (SHA-256 `1a782fc0b4bfe301d124288122490369d93d5acdaa61470d24d6adf394fe4315`). Neither source report retained executable `.rs` fixture bytes or ran the proposed probe. We **reconstructed** [R563's six-function specimen](fixture/r563.rs) from its Revalidation prose and made a small new [R564 specimen](fixture/r564.rs); these are new inputs, not a replay of original fixture bytes.

The source reports analyze `rust-lang/rust@14210df0e27ccd7d9e6a05b8085cbd438e4bbc65`. The installed May 31 nightly binary is instead `rustc 1.98.0-nightly` at commit `f8a08b688cbe60acc386ed1fbd1b7cbaaf5576b1`, SHA-256 `2ab7af1ea2ec5c69195fd8dfb0e1f91afdb7cc1e53127bba416616ce43a18dbc`; the September 30 nightly is `rustc 1.101.0-nightly` at `5c543b0b8c73c7b72bc8284ced4fb22ead15734d`, SHA-256 `29f8ccc9aa7b0d8798eda854fa7f0e4ba3867c8c336b87d52dbb3b24b3f0878d`. Both target the local `aarch64-apple-darwin` host. This is a **toolchain-era runtime comparison**, not execution of the exact source pin. [results.json](results.json) retains both full `rustc -Vv` outputs and the binary paths.

Direct `rustc` ran 12 serial cells: each specimen under each version with `--emit=mir` and `-Zmir-opt-level=0` or `2`, plus an opt-level-0 `-Zdump-mir=SimplifyCfg` or `-Zdump-mir=LowerIntrinsics` run. Each command and its exit, streams, emitted MIR, dump manifest, resource admission, sampled RSS, and elapsed time are preserved. All 12 exited 0. The six emitted MIR pairs are **byte-identical across the two compilers**. The 48 R563 stage dumps per version and eight R564 stage dumps per version also have paired identical bytes after matching function, pass, and before/after phase; R563's numeric pass IDs in the dump filenames differ between compilers. This equality is specific to the reconstructed inputs and recorded flags, not a general compiler equivalence claim.

## R563: stage and reachability observations

The `SimplifyCfg-initial.before` dumps for `after_return` and `after_diverge` contain a `marker()` call in blocks disconnected from entry. The corresponding `SimplifyCfg-initial.after` dumps omit the call. Neither emitted opt-level-0 nor opt-level-2 MIR contains those calls. This directly exhibits early reachability cleanup for these two source tails.

At opt-level 0, `constant_branch` still has a `switchInt` on a `false` temporary and a `marker()` call in the other arm. The selected `SimplifyCfg` dumps retain that switch/call through the recorded pre-optimization pass. At opt-level 2, `constant_branch` has neither `switchInt` nor `marker()`. The `dynamic_branch` control retains `switchInt` and `marker()` at opt-level 2. This demonstrates a difference between unreachable source after a terminator and a syntactically false branch at the recorded output points.

These are rustc text-dump observations. They do not directly query Charon's `tcx.mir_promoted`, prove what Charon extracts, or establish behavior for dependency bodies or other optimization flags. The stage names come from `-Zdump-mir`, whose format is unstable.

## R564: safety-operation representation observations

In `operations` at opt-level 0, the text MIR shows `&raw const (*_2)` for the raw borrow, `copy (*_1)` for the raw-pointer load, a `Bits { word: ... }` construction followed by `copy (_6.0: u32)` for the union field, an ordinary `unsafe_add(...)` call terminator, a static-origin allocation for `COUNTER`, and `asm!("nop", options())` as a terminator. These output shapes agree across both compilers. At opt-level 2, some intermediate shapes, including the unused raw borrow and union construction, are optimized away; inline asm and the unsafe function call remain. Thus this fixture shows why output stage matters to an operation inventory.

The `copy_one` specimen calls `std::ptr::copy_nonoverlapping::<u8>`; in both `LowerIntrinsics.before` and `.after` dumps that **wrapper call remains an ordinary call**. This probe therefore does **not** demonstrate a dedicated `CopyNonOverlapping` MIR statement, although the source report describes compiler-intrinsic lowering for selected direct intrinsics. A separate direct-intrinsic specimen would be needed to test that lowering. The dump also cannot prove that safety authorization context is absent from all compiler-internal MIR metadata: the original report's HIR/THIR and MIR-structure claims rest on source review. Conversely, textual absence of `unsafe { ... }` is not evidence that the operation is safe or authorized without source context.

## Reproduction and limits

All commands were direct local rustc invocations, offline, without Cargo, Charon, Aeneas, Anneal, foreign targets, downloads, installs, or a server. The runner [support/probe.py](support/probe.py) checks before each child for >20% estimated reclaimable RAM (`vm_stat` free + inactive + speculative pages divided by physical RAM), >1 GiB free disk, and <100 MB owned package bytes; it kills a child after 30 seconds or sampled RSS >1 GiB. Recorded minimum admission was **26.0366%** RAM and **4,078,661,632 bytes** free disk; maximum package size at admission was **128,512 bytes**, sampled child RSS **97,680 KiB**, and child duration **0.247 s**. Very short children may finish before RSS is sampled, yielding a recorded zero; zero does not mean no memory was used.

The full [raw outputs](raw/), [results.json](results.json), and [analysis.json](analysis.json) are retained. `python3 -B support/check.py` verifies exact fixture and recorded tool identities, all 12 command structures and raw hashes, gate arithmetic, relevant MIR/dump facts, and cross-version byte comparisons without invoking rustc; it is designed to run after package relocation. `python3 -B support/probe.py` reruns the guarded local experiment against the installed binaries. MIR text and `-Zdump-mir` names are unstable debug output. These two small reconstructed fixtures cannot establish equivalence of the full compiler, rustc-private query behavior, or Charon/Aeneas translations.
