# Multi-Language 2-Safety and Hyperproperty Composition Across Heterogeneous Boundaries

## Summary

Preserving 2-safety hyperproperties (such as observational determinism, non-interference, and constant-time) across **heterogeneous multi-language boundaries**—compiler IR lowerings that change observation alphabets, FFI membranes between managed and native runtimes, garbage-collected heaps, effect-handler/coroutine schedulers, and hardware-speculation boundaries—fails whenever the boundary expands the adversary's observation alphabet or context power without aligning low-equivalence classes across the trace relation.

1. **Vertical vs. Horizontal Composition Asymmetry (`LeakageBisim` vs. `UniformRefinement` vs. Robust Hyperproperty Preservation):**
   - **Leakage bisimulation (`LeakageBisim`)** composes vertically across arbitrary heterogeneous public leakage transformers $\phi_2 \circ \phi_1$ (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]`: `Toy.HyperComposition.leakageBisim_compose`), yet rejects honest secret-independent determinizing lowerings such as concrete stack-slot layout or evaluation-order fixing (`leakageBisim_incompatible_with_determinizer`, `leakageBisim_rejects_uniformDetTarget`).
   - **Uniform refinement (`UniformRefinement`, defined in this package's toy model)** resolves this vertical obstruction—admitting secret-independent determinizers (`uniformRefinement_admits_uniformDetTarget`), rejecting secret-dependent determinizers (`uniformRefinement_rejects_leakyDetTarget`), and preserving closed-module 2-safety and set-valued non-interference (`uniformRefinement_preserves_twoSafety`, `uniformRefinement_preserves_setNI`)—yet does not protect against a richer foreign observation alphabet: `uniformRefinement_breaks_under_richer_context` pairs a valid coarse `UniformRefinement` of the `SetNI` (non-`TwoSafety`) specification `nondetLogicalSource` by `managedSourceSem secretAllocModule` with the failure of `TwoSafety` under the richer `membraneGCSem 5` observation of the same `ManagedModule` record (disclosed: no separate linking operator is formalized in that theorem).
   - **Robust relational/hyperproperty preservation (`R2HSP`, `RSCHP`, `RHC`, `R2rSP`, `RRHP`)** (Abate et al., CSF 2019; ESOP 2020 / TOPLAS 2021) restores horizontal security against arbitrary target contexts within the modeled target alphabet by quantifying over linked contexts, and composes vertically across heterogeneous compiler passes into class intersection $C_1 \cap C_2$ under relational trace composition ${\sim_1} \bullet {\sim_2}$ **if** pass 1 robustly preserves $\tilde{\sigma}_{\sim_2}(C_1)$ under $\sim_1$, pass 2 robustly preserves $C_2$ under $\sim_2$, and $\vdash_{\mathrm{wf}} {\sim_2} : C_1$ (Kruse, Backes, and Patrignani, `arXiv:2307.08681v3`, Theorem III.1).
2. **Sufficient Galois-Connection Conditions Across Alphabet-Changing Membranes (`Abate et al.`, ESOP 2020 / TOPLAS 2021):**
   - Building on the trace-relation / Galois-connection bijection $\tilde{\tau} \dashv \tilde{\sigma}$ (documented in adjacent corpus packages cited under **Applicability** and constructively mechanized here in `tau_sigma_galois`, `ccRel_iff_tpTau`, `ccRel_iff_tpSigma`), transferring a 2-safety hyperproperty (`TwoSafetyRel`, or Abstract Non-Interference $\mathrm{ANI}^{\rho}_{\phi}$) from source to target under $\mathrm{CC}_\sim$ has two cleanly separable **sufficient** boundary conditions: **input low-equivalence reflection** (`ReflectsLowIn`: low-equivalent target inputs relate only to low-equivalent source inputs) and **output low-equivalence preservation** (`PreservesLowOut`: low-equivalent source outputs never split into target outputs distinguishable by the target observer) (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]`: `galois_two_safety_composition`). Both conditions compose vertically along heterogeneous chains (`relComp_reflectsLowIn`, `relComp_preservesLowOut`, `heterogeneous_chain_two_safety`).
   - For arbitrary (non-subset-closed) hyperproperties, transferring $\tilde{\sigma}$-lifted source obligations from exact trace image ($\mathrm{HC}_\sim$ / $\mathrm{RHC}_\sim$) holds under a **Galois insertion** $\tilde{\tau} \circ \tilde{\sigma} = \mathrm{id}$ (Abate et al., ESOP 2020 Theorem 5.3 / B.5; `galois_insertion_transfers_sigma_hyperprop`), whereas the converse implication holds under a **Galois reflection** $\tilde{\sigma} \circ \tilde{\tau} = \mathrm{id}$.
3. **Concrete Cross-Boundary Side-Channel Mechanisms Breaking Horizontal 2-Safety:**
   - **Managed-heap allocation and GC pauses across FFI** (Pedersen and Askarov, S&P 2017): secret-dependent allocation volume or object lifetime triggers GC mark/sweep/compaction pauses observable to native callers via wall-clock timing, violating `PreservesLowOut` and breaking horizontal 2-safety even while 1-safety and $\mathrm{CC}_\sim$ hold (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]`: `membrane_breaks_two_safety`, `membrane_preserves_one_safety`, `gc_membrane_satisfies_ccRel`, `gc_membrane_violates_preservesLowOut`).
   - **Coroutine/effect-handler stack-copying and scheduler interleaving across FFI** (Russo and Sabelfeld, JLAP 2009; Stefan et al., ICFP 2012; Xie et al., ICFP 2020; Sivaramakrishnan et al., PLDI 2021): secret-dependent handler nesting depth, segmented-stack growth/copying, or cooperative yield counts expose timing and scheduling interleavings across native FFI and concurrent thread boundaries.
   - **Speculative and microarchitectural observation mismatches across language/compartment membranes** (Guarnieri et al., S&P 2020, S&P 2021; Patrignani and Guarnieri, CCS 2021; Narayan et al., USENIX Security 2021): non-speculative source semantics trivially satisfy `RSNI(L)` (`Theorem 3.4` in Patrignani and Guarnieri, CCS 2021), yet compiled targets linked with an attacker context violate Spectre-v1 (PHT) `RSNI(T)` unless hardened by strong countermeasures (ICC `lfence` or strong SLH; plain SLH gives only weak `RSNI(T-)`, and MSVC-style selective fences are proved insecure). Across broader multi-tenant Wasm-to-native boundaries where BTB and RSB poisoning also apply, sandbox isolation requires dedicated predictor and control-flow hardening (e.g., *Swivel*'s linear blocks, BTB flushing, and CET/MPK enforcement).

**Model–system relation: none proved.** All Lean `v4.34.1` results in `lean/` are `[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]` statements about abstract relational trace semantics (`Toy.HyperComposition`); they do not prove or refute security of any concrete compiler, garbage collector, effect runtime, or CPU microarchitecture.

## Findings

### 1. Vertical vs. Horizontal Composition Across Heterogeneous Pass and Language Chains

Basis: documentation (primary papers: `10.1109/CSF.2018.00031`, `10.1145/3371075`, `10.1145/3460120.3484761`, `10.1109/CSF.2019.00025`, `10.1145/3460860`, `10.48550/arXiv.2307.08681`, `10.1145/3341689`, `10.1145/3519939.3523703`, `10.1145/3571220`) + execution (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]` in `lean/Toy/HyperComposition.lean`) + derived synthesis.

Multi-language systems compose along two orthogonal axes (Patterson and Ahmed, ICFP 2019, `10.1145/3341689`; Sammler et al., POPL 2023, `10.1145/3571220`):

- **Vertical composition** chains compiler passes or language lowerings $L_1 \xrightarrow{\downarrow_1} L_2 \xrightarrow{\downarrow_2} L_3$, typically changing intermediate observation alphabets ($\mathcal{O}_1 \to \mathcal{O}_2 \to \mathcal{O}_3$) and resolving source nondeterminism (evaluation order, stack frame layout, register assignment, memory allocator choices).
- **Horizontal composition** links independently compiled or foreign-language components $C_T[P\downarrow]$ (or $P_1 \bowtie P_2$ across a Matthews–Findler boundary / FFI membrane / compartmentalized ABI), where each side may have been verified under a different source semantics and observation alphabet.

Comparing the four principal hyperproperty-preserving relations across both axes reveals a sharp structural matrix:

| Relation / Criterion | Vertical Composition ($L_1 \to L_2 \to L_3$) | Admits Uniform Determinizing Lowering? | Horizontal Composition Under Richer Foreign / Target Context? | Exact Failure Mode or Side Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Leakage Bisimulation (`LeakageBisim`)** | **Yes** unconditionally across public transformers $\phi_2 \circ \phi_1$ (`leakageBisim_compose`) | **No** (`leakageBisim_incompatible_with_determinizer`, `leakageBisim_rejects_uniformDetTarget`) | Only if linked context's observations are already in $\mathcal{O}_2$ and context respects language abstraction | Two-sided trace-set equality forbids eliminating any secret-independent nondeterminism whose $\phi$-images differ |
| **Uniform Refinement (`UniformRefinement`, toy model)** / Structured-Leakage Simulation (Barthe et al., CSF 2018 / POPL 2020 / CCS 2021) | **Yes** when each pass supplies a public transformer $\Phi_i$ and preserves secret-independent choice resolution (`uniformRefinement_preserves_twoSafety`, `uniformRefinement_preserves_setNI`) | **Yes** (`uniformRefinement_admits_uniformDetTarget`); rejects secret-dependent determinizers (`uniformRefinement_rejects_leakyDetTarget`) | **No** (illustrated by conjunction witness `uniformRefinement_breaks_under_richer_context` without a formal linking operator): closed-module refinement ignores observations only visible in a richer foreign/target alphabet | Fails when a foreign/target observer sees unmodeled effects (GC pause, stack reallocation, shared cache/predictor state) or breaks source memory isolation |
| **Robust 2-Hypersafety / Subset-Closed Hyperproperty Preservation (`R2HSP` / `RSCHP` / `R2SCHP`)** (Abate et al., CSF 2019; ESOP 2020 / TOPLAS 2021) | **Sufficient condition** (Kruse, Backes, and Patrignani, `arXiv:2307.08681v3`, Thm. III.1): yields $C_1 \cap C_2$ under ${\sim_1} \bullet {\sim_2}$ **if** pass 1 preserves $\tilde{\sigma}_{\sim_2}(C_1)$ under $\sim_1$, pass 2 preserves $C_2$ under $\sim_2$, and $\vdash_{\mathrm{wf}} {\sim_2} : C_1$ | **Yes** for subset-closed criteria (`R2HSP`, `R2SCHP`, `RSCHP`), since target contexts produce subsets of back-translated source traces | **Yes** against arbitrary target contexts $C_T$ within the modeled target observation alphabet $\Sigma_T$ | Requires context-based back-translation for `RSCHP` (quantifier $\forall P\,C_T\,\exists C_S\,\forall t$); still breaks if real hardware/runtime exposes observations outside $\Sigma_T$ |
| **Robust Relational Hyperproperty Preservation (`R2rSP` / `R2rSCHP` / `RRHP`)** (Abate et al., CSF 2019; ESOP 2020 / TOPLAS 2021) | **Conditional** on relational trace-relation composition and well-formedness across passes | **Yes** for subset-closed `R2rSCHP` / `R2rSP`; **No** for exact `RHC` / `RRHP` (requires two-sided trace-set equality) | **Yes**, including multi-program confidentiality and cryptographic/behavioral indistinguishability across linked contexts | Highest proof and runtime isolation cost; requires shared source context $C_S$ back-translating target interactions simultaneously across $P_1, P_2$ |

#### 1.1 Why `LeakageBisim` and `UniformRefinement` Separate Vertically and Under Enriched Observations

In `lean/Toy/HyperComposition.lean` (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]`):

- `LeakageBisim φ S T` asserts $\forall l\,h\,o_2.\; T(l, h, o_2) \iff \exists o_1.\; S(l, h, o_1) \land o_2 = \phi(l, o_1)$. Vertical composition (`leakageBisim_compose`) holds directly by substituting existential witnesses across $\phi_2 \circ \phi_1$, with zero side conditions on determinism or alphabet injectivity.
- However, `leakageBisim_incompatible_with_determinizer` proves generally that whenever a source $S$ admits two observations $o_1, o_2$ at $(l, h)$ with $\phi(l, o_1) \neq \phi(l, o_2)$, **no deterministic target $T$** can satisfy `LeakageBisim φ S T`. Concretely, `nondetAlignSource` (modeling a secret-independent choice of stack alignment) satisfies `SetNI`, and `uniformDetTarget` deterministically selects alignment `false` uniformly across all secrets, yet `leakageBisim_rejects_uniformDetTarget` proves $\neg\mathrm{LeakageBisim}\;\mathrm{id}\;\mathrm{nondetAlignSource}\;\mathrm{uniformDetTarget}$.
- Weakening two-sided equality to `UniformRefinement φ S T`—requiring (i) sound $\phi$-refinement ($T(l,h,o_2) \implies \exists o_1.\; S(l,h,o_1) \land o_2 = \phi(l,o_1)$) and (ii) secret-independent retention ($T(l,h_1,o_2) \land (\exists o_1.\; S(l,h_2,o_1) \land o_2 = \phi(l,o_1)) \implies T(l,h_2,o_2)$)—strictly generalizes `LeakageBisim` (`leakageBisim_implies_uniformRefinement`), admits `uniformDetTarget` (`uniformRefinement_admits_uniformDetTarget`), rejects secret-dependent determinization `leakyDetTarget` (`uniformRefinement_rejects_leakyDetTarget`), and preserves both `SetNI` and `TwoSafety` for closed modules (`uniformRefinement_preserves_setNI`, `uniformRefinement_preserves_twoSafety`).
- **Scope disclosure on `uniformRefinement_breaks_under_richer_context`:** In the toy model, `uniformRefinement_breaks_under_richer_context` is the conjunction of two facts about the same `ManagedModule` record `secretAllocModule`: (1) `managedSourceSem secretAllocModule` satisfies `UniformRefinement id` with respect to the coarse logical specification `nondetLogicalSource` (which satisfies `SetNI`, not `TwoSafety`), and (2) the richer GC-observing semantics `membraneGCSem 5 secretAllocModule` fails `TwoSafety` (`membrane_breaks_two_safety`). No separate contextual linking operator is formalized in Lean; `membraneGCSem 5` is a fixed enriched observation of the same module record illustrating why closed-alphabet refinement does not bound observations outside that alphabet.

#### 1.2 Vertical Composition of Heterogeneous Secure Compilers (`Kruse, Backes & Patrignani`, 2024)

When a multi-pass or multi-language compilation pipeline combines distinct passes—for example, a memory-safety enforcement pass (`SecurePtrs`, preserving safety class $C_1$ under trace relation $\sim_1$) followed by a constant-time pass (`FaCT`, preserving `CCT` under trace relation $\sim_2$)—vertical composition does **not** automatically preserve $C_1 \cup C_2$ or even $C_1 \cap C_2$ without checking trace-relation compatibility.

Kruse, Backes, and Patrignani (`arXiv:2307.08681v3`, Section III, Theorem III.1) establish a **sufficient** sequential composition law for trace-relating robust compilers:

- Define trace-relation well-formedness $\vdash_{\mathrm{wf}} {\sim} : C \iff \forall \pi \in C.\; \tilde{\sigma}_\sim(\pi) \in \tilde{\sigma}_\sim(C)$ (ensuring the upper adjoint preserves the property class structure).
- **Theorem III.1 (Composition of Secure Compilers w.r.t. $\tilde{\sigma}$):** **If** (1) pass 1 robustly preserves $\tilde{\sigma}_{\sim_2}(C_1)$ under $\sim_1$, (2) pass 2 robustly preserves $C_2$ under $\sim_2$, and (3) $\vdash_{\mathrm{wf}} {\sim_2} : C_1$, **then** the composed pipeline $\gamma_2 \circ \gamma_1$ robustly preserves the **intersection** $C_1 \cap C_2$ under relational composition ${\sim_1} \bullet {\sim_2}$. The theorem also holds for classes of hyperproperties—for instance, composing `SecurePtrs` (safety properties) with `FaCT` (the `CCT` hyperproperty) yields a compiler robustly preserving the intersection of safety properties and `CCT` (`arXiv:2307.08681v3`, §III, p. 4). (Separately, in their Section IV–V multi-pass instantiation, Kruse et al. verify optimizing passes Dead Code Elimination `DCE` and Constant Folding `CF` alongside `TMS`, `SMS`, speculative safety `SS`, and Strict Cryptographic Constant-Time `sCCT`—where `sCCT` in Definition IV.4 is a stricter *trace property* over-approximating the hyperproperty `CCT`.)
- **Order sensitivity (Corollary III.1):** Reordering passes ($\gamma_1 \circ \gamma_2$ vs. $\gamma_2 \circ \gamma_1$) has a **sufficient** soundness condition when both trace relations are well-formed ($\vdash_{\mathrm{wf}} {\sim_1} : C_2$ and $\vdash_{\mathrm{wf}} {\sim_2} : C_1$) and class-indifferent ($\tilde{\sigma}_{\sim_2}(C_1) = C_1$ and $\tilde{\sigma}_{\sim_1}(C_2) = C_2$). Kruse et al. remark cautiously that Corollary III.1 is likely not applicable to `SecurePtrs` and `FaCT`: *"While first running `SecurePtrs` and then `FaCT` should be fine, the other direction has potential security hazards, since the `SecurePtrs` compiler does not account for cryptographic-constant time primitives, such as `ctselect`."*

#### 1.3 Horizontal Linking and Compartmentalization Across Multi-Language Boundaries

Horizontal multi-language composition introduces two distinct failure axes beyond vertical pass composition (cross-referencing `semantic-multi-language-interop-dimsum-melocoton-and-linking-types` and `secureptrs-turn-taking-simulation-capableptrs-...` for full proof details):

1. **Semantic interop vs. hyperproperty linking:** Matthews and Findler (POPL 2007, `10.1145/1190216.1190220`; TOPLAS 2009, `10.1145/1498926.1498930`), Patterson et al. (PLDI 2022, `10.1145/3519939.3523703`), and Sammler et al. (`DimSum`, POPL 2023, `10.1145/3571220`) achieve horizontal and vertical composition of semantic type soundness and single-trace refinement (`Beh(Impl) ⊆ Beh(Spec)`) across heterogeneous languages via syntactic converters, realizability convertibility, and decentralized event wrappers. **Derived boundary limitation:** Single-trace refinement does not preserve non-subset-closed hyperproperties (`GNI`) and preserves 2-safety across a foreign boundary only if every side-channel observation available to the foreign language is explicitly reified into the shared event alphabet.
2. **Compartmentalized secure compilation against dynamic compromise and pointer forgery:** Abate et al. (CCS 2018, `10.1145/3243734.3243745`, `RSCDC`), El-Korashy et al. (`CapablePtrs`, CSF 2021, `10.1109/CSF51468.2021.00036`; `SecurePtrs`, CSF 2022, `10.1109/CSF54842.2022.9919680`), and Thibault et al. (`SECOMP`, CCS 2024, `10.1145/3658644.3670288`) enforce memory-safe horizontal linking of partial or compartmentalized programs against untrusted target contexts. However, these criteria target **robust safety (`RSC` / `RSCDC`)** (1-safety integrity against pointer forging and out-of-bounds corruption), **not** timing or microarchitectural 2-safety (`R2HSP` / `RSCHP`): an untrusted compartment barred from dereferencing private capabilities can still observe timing, GC, or speculative side channels unless the compartment membrane also bounds observation leakage.

### 2. Sufficient Galois-Connection $(\alpha, \gamma)$ Conditions for Transferring 2-Safety Across Alphabet-Changing Membranes

Basis: documentation (primary papers: `10.1007/978-3-030-44914-8_1`, `10.1145/3460860`, `10.1145/964001.964017`, `10.1109/CSF.2017.13`, `10.1145/3436809`) + execution (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]` in `lean/Toy/HyperComposition.lean`).

#### 2.1 Delta Beyond Adjacent Galois-Connection Reports: Input/Output Low-Equivalence Split and Chain Composition

The baseline Abate et al. (ESOP 2020 `arXiv:1907.05320v4`; TOPLAS 2021 `10.1145/3460860`) machinery—the bijection between trace relations ${\sim} \subseteq \mathrm{Trace}_S \times \mathrm{Trace}_T$ and Galois connections $\tilde{\tau} \dashv \tilde{\sigma}$ (Lemma 2.7), the Trinitarian View $\mathrm{TP}_{\tilde{\tau}} \iff \mathrm{CC}_\sim \iff \mathrm{TP}_{\tilde{\sigma}}$ (Theorems 2.4, 2.6, 2.8), the subset-closure wrapper $\mathrm{Cl}_\subseteq \circ \tilde{\tau}$ (Theorem 2.11), and the output low-equivalence functionality criterion—is already documented in `institution-comorphisms-heterogeneous-logics-and-trace-relating-compilation` (§5–6) and `coalition-presheaf-information-flow-and-relational-hyperproperty-compilation` (§1). For self-containment inside our Lean toy model, `tau_sigma_galois`, `ccRel_iff_tpTau`, and `ccRel_iff_tpSigma` constructively kernel-check the adjunction and trinity with 0 axioms.

The distinct multi-language composition delta addressed here is **separating input-reflection from output-preservation across split membranes, composing both along multi-hop heterogeneous language chains, and pinpointing the exact failed premise on a GC membrane**:

1. **Forward Degradation vs. Backward Sufficiency for Abstract Non-Interference (Abate et al., ESOP 2020, §4; Giacobazzi and Mastroeni, POPL 2004, `10.1145/964001.964017`):**
   - For split trace relations ${\sim} = \langle {\sim^\circ}, {\sim^\bullet} \rangle$ separating inputs and outputs and $\mathrm{ANI}^{\rho}_{\phi} = \{\pi \mid \forall t_1, t_2 \in \pi.\; \phi(t_1^\circ) = \phi(t_2^\circ) \implies \rho(t_1^\bullet) = \rho(t_2^\bullet)\}$:
   - **Forward compilation (`Theorem 4.1` & `Theorem 4.2`):** When $\sim^\circ, \sim^\bullet$ are total maps from target to source traces with $\sim^\circ$ surjective (e.g., $\mathrm{Trace}_T = \mathrm{Trace}_S \times \mathbb{N}_\omega$ with $s \sim (s, n)$), a $\mathrm{CC}_\sim$ compiler transfers source $\mathrm{ANI}^{\rho_S}_{\phi_S}$ to target $\mathrm{ANI}^{\rho^\#_T}_{\phi^\#_T}$ with $\rho^\#_T = g^\bullet \circ \rho_S \circ f^\bullet$, where $\rho^\#_T(\{(s, n_1)\}) = \rho^\#_T(\{(s, n_2)\})$. Thus, the target guarantee derived from $\mathrm{CC}_\sim$ alone collapses execution times to **timing-insensitive non-interference** and does not by itself rule out timing leaks to a timing-observing target attacker. (When $\sim^\bullet$ is non-functional due to source undefined behavior, `Theorem 4.2` characterizes a family of target attackers satisfying $\rho^\#_T(t^\bullet) = \rho^\#_T(\tilde{\tau}^\bullet(\rho_S(s^\bullet)))$.)
   - **Backward obligation sufficiency (`Theorem 4.3`):** To derive a strong target 2-safety property $\mathrm{ANI}^{\rho_T}_{\phi_T}$ from source $\mathrm{ANI}^{\rho^\#_S}_{\phi^\#_S}$ ($\phi^\#_S = \tilde{\sigma}^\circ \circ \phi_T \circ \tilde{\tau}^\circ$, $\rho^\#_S = \tilde{\sigma}^\bullet \circ \rho_T \circ \tilde{\tau}^\bullet$) via $\mathrm{CC}_\sim$, Abate et al. Theorem 4.3 establishes two **sufficient** hypotheses: (i) $\sim^\bullet$ is a total surjective map from source outputs to target outputs, and (ii) $\forall s, t.\; s^\circ \sim^\circ t^\circ \implies \phi_T(t^\circ) = \phi_T(\tilde{\tau}^\circ(s^\circ))$ (target input equivalence is representable in the source).
2. **Mechanized Input/Output Split and Multi-Hop Chain Composition (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]`):**
   - For arbitrary trace spaces $S, T$ and abstract 2-safety `TwoSafetyRel lowIn lowOut Beh := ∀ t₁ t₂, Beh t₁ → Beh t₂ → lowIn t₁ t₂ → lowOut t₁ t₂`, `galois_two_safety_composition` proves that three hypotheses are **sufficient** to transfer `TwoSafetyRel` from $S$ to $T$:
     - `CCRel R BehS BehT` ($\mathrm{Beh}_T \subseteq \tilde{\tau}_R(\mathrm{Beh}_S)$),
     - **`ReflectsLowIn R lowInS lowInT`:** $\forall s_1\,s_2\,t_1\,t_2.\; R(s_1,t_1) \land R(s_2,t_2) \land \mathrm{lowIn}_T(t_1,t_2) \implies \mathrm{lowIn}_S(s_1,s_2)$ (low-equivalent target inputs relate only to low-equivalent source inputs), and
     - **`PreservesLowOut R lowOutS lowOutT`:** $\forall s_1\,s_2\,t_1\,t_2.\; R(s_1,t_1) \land R(s_2,t_2) \land \mathrm{lowOut}_S(s_1,s_2) \implies \mathrm{lowOut}_T(t_1,t_2)$ (low-equivalent source outputs never split into target outputs distinguishable by the target observer).
   - **Vertical chain composition across heterogeneous alphabets (`S → M → T`):** Under relational composition `RelComp R₁ R₂`, `tau` distributes covariantly (`tau_relComp`), `sigma` distributes contravariantly (`sigma_relComp`), and both `ReflectsLowIn` and `PreservesLowOut` compose transitively (`relComp_reflectsLowIn`, `relComp_preservesLowOut`), yielding end-to-end 2-safety across multi-hop heterogeneous language chains (`heterogeneous_chain_two_safety`).
   - **Sharpness on the GC membrane:** `gc_membrane_satisfies_ccRel` and `gc_membrane_reflectsLowIn` hold for `gcTraceRel`, while `gc_membrane_violates_preservesLowOut` isolates `PreservesLowOut` as the exact failed premise.
   - **Reactive hypersafety instance (`Patrignani & Garg`, CSF 2017, `10.1109/CSF.2017.13`; `Abate et al.`, ESOP 2020 §5.3):** In reactive settings with $\alpha?/\alpha!$ actions, Trace-Preserving Compilation (`TPC`) requires a cross-language action relation that is total on source actions and injective, padding invalid target inputs with a fixed uniform response $\checkmark$; Abate et al. (§5.3) classify `TPC` as an instance of **$\mathrm{RSCHP}^{\mathrm{HSafe}\circ\tilde{\tau}}$** (not plain $\mathrm{RSCHP}$), mapping source hypersafety to target hypersafety.

#### 2.2 Constructive Mechanization of the Weak Trinity for General Hyperproperties (`Abate et al.`, Thm. 5.3 / B.5)

For arbitrary (including non-subset-closed $\forall\exists$) hyperproperties, Abate et al. (ESOP 2020, Theorem 5.3 & Theorem B.5) show that exact trace image $\mathrm{HC}_\sim$ / $\mathrm{RHC}_\sim$ is unconditionally equivalent to $\mathrm{HP}_{\tilde{\tau}}$ / $\mathrm{RHP}_{\tilde{\tau}}$, while implying $\mathrm{HP}_{\tilde{\sigma}}$ / $\mathrm{RHP}_{\tilde{\sigma}}$ under a **Galois insertion** ($\tilde{\tau} \circ \tilde{\sigma} = \mathrm{id}$) and following from it under a **Galois reflection** ($\tilde{\sigma} \circ \tilde{\tau} = \mathrm{id}$). In `lean/Toy/HyperComposition.lean`, `galois_insertion_transfers_sigma_hyperprop` constructively kernel-checks the Galois-insertion transfer for extensionally invariant hyperproperties (0 axioms), and `gcTraceRel_not_galoisInsertion` proves that the GC projection membrane fails `GaloisInsertion` because collapsing `(0, false)` and `(0, true)` onto source output `0` prevents $\tilde{\tau} \circ \tilde{\sigma}$ from isolating a single `gcPause` value.

### 3. Concrete Cross-Boundary Side-Channel Leakage Mechanisms

Basis: documentation (primary papers: `10.1109/SP.2017.64`, `10.1016/j.jlap.2008.09.003`, `10.1145/2364527.2364557`, `10.1145/3453483.3454039`, `10.1145/3408981`, `10.1109/SP40000.2020.00011`, `10.1109/SP40001.2021.00036`, `10.1145/3460120.3484534`, USENIX Security 2021 Swivel) + execution (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]` in `lean/Toy/HyperComposition.lean`) + derived synthesis.

Three recurring runtime and microarchitectural boundary mechanisms break horizontal 2-safety even when each language's standalone verification guarantees functional non-interference:

#### 3.1 Managed-Heap Allocation and Garbage Collection Across FFI Boundaries

- **Mechanism (`Pedersen & Askarov`, IEEE S&P 2017, *From Trash to Treasure*, `10.1109/SP.2017.64`):** Managed languages (OCaml, Java, Haskell, Go, JS/Wasm-GC) omit allocation counts, free-list state, and garbage-collector invocations from their high-level operational semantics. However, secret-dependent branches or data-dependent temporary allocations ("trash") alter two observable heap quantities:
  1. **GC trigger timing:** Reaching the nursery/heap threshold earlier on one secret branch triggers a stop-the-world or incremental GC pause during a subsequent public operation or before returning across an FFI boundary.
  2. **Mark/sweep/compaction duration:** Even when GC triggers at a fixed point, mark-and-sweep or copying-compaction work scales with the number and graph topology of *live* reachable objects, leaking secret heap structure through pause duration and post-compaction cache locality.
- **Cross-boundary breakdown:** When a managed component $M$ is linked across an FFI membrane with a native caller (C/Rust/assembly) that reads a monotonic clock (`rdtsc`/`clock_gettime`), shares CPU cache lines, or runs a concurrent native thread whose latency jitters during stop-the-world pauses, the native context inhabits a strictly richer observation alphabet $\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{native}} = \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{managed}} \times \mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{GC\text{-}time}}$.
- **Toy-model witness (`[kernel-checked proof]` `[toy model]`):** `secretAllocModule` returns constant `0` logically (`secretAllocModule_source_twoSafety`), yet allocates `1` vs. `10` objects depending on secret `h`. Across `membraneGCSem 5`, 1-safety (`membrane_preserves_one_safety`) and $\mathrm{CC}_{\mathrm{gcTraceRel}}$ (`gc_membrane_satisfies_ccRel`) both hold, and `ReflectsLowIn` holds (`gc_membrane_reflectsLowIn`), yet **`PreservesLowOut` fails** (`gc_membrane_violates_preservesLowOut`) and **horizontal 2-safety breaks** (`membrane_breaks_two_safety`).
- **Mitigation boundary (`Pedersen & Askarov`, S&P 2017):** Restoring 2-safety requires making GC timing part of the cross-boundary contract—e.g., per-security-level partitioned heaps / per-principal nurseries where low-observer allocations and collections never depend on high-heap occupancy or high-root traversal, plus deterministic or padded GC scheduling.

#### 3.2 Coroutine Stack-Copying, Algebraic Effect Handlers, and Scheduler Interleaving Across FFI

- **Mechanism — Effect-handler stack management (`Sivaramakrishnan et al.`, PLDI 2021, `10.1145/3453483.3454039`; `Xie et al.`, ICFP 2020, `10.1145/3408981`):**
  - Runtime implementations of algebraic effects and coroutines rely on dynamic stack/continuation machinery invisible to pure equational source semantics: OCaml 5 allocates small heap-resident **segmented fiber stacks** that grow on overflow via stack reallocation/copying and switch between fiber stacks and the system C stack on FFI calls (`caml_c_call`, Sivaramakrishnan et al., PLDI 2021); evidence-passing / multi-prompt implementations (Xie et al., ICFP 2020) bubble yields up handler chains or copy captured continuation segments.
  - If a secret-dependent computation alters call depth (triggering fiber stack resize/copy), effect invocation count, or handler search depth, the resulting heap allocations, `memcpy` latency, and stack-cache footprint leak across an FFI boundary into native callers—even if the effect-typed source program never passes secret values across the FFI signature.
- **Mechanism — Scheduler interleaving and internal timing (`Russo & Sabelfeld`, JLAP 2009, `10.1016/j.jlap.2008.09.003`; `Stefan et al.`, ICFP 2012, `10.1145/2364527.2364557`):**
  - When a secret-branching coroutine or thread yields to a shared runtime scheduler (cooperative effect-based scheduler or preemptive OS/green-thread runtime), variations in execution steps before `yield` or lock acquisition alter the **interleaving order** of public operations performed by sibling threads or native FFI callbacks (**internal timing channel**).
  - Russo and Sabelfeld (JLAP 2009, 78(7):593–618) show that preventing scheduler-mediated leaks requires hiding secret thread step counts from the scheduler (e.g., partitioning secret vs. public scheduler queues or disallowing secret-dependent `yield`/synchronization visibility), while Stefan et al. (ICFP 2012, `LIO`) demonstrate that mitigating covert termination and timing channels in concurrent IFC requires spawning secret computations into labeled threads whose termination/timing cannot be observed by lower-labeled threads without raising the observer's current security label.
- **Galois characterization (derived):** A coroutine/FFI boundary that collapses internal `yield`/stack-resize/scheduler steps into silent transitions violates `PreservesLowOut` whenever the foreign side can observe wall time, callback ordering, or shared native-thread preemption. Composing 2-safety across an effect/FFI membrane requires either (a) **oblivious effect traces** (fixed public handler/yield/allocation schedule independent of secrets, matching structured-leakage transformers), or (b) **scheduling and stack isolation** (dedicated non-shared execution contexts with bounded/deterministic FFI handoff points).

#### 3.3 Speculative and Microarchitectural Observation Mismatches Across Language and Compartment Membranes

- **Mechanism — Spectre-v1 / PHT observation mismatch (`Guarnieri et al.`, S&P 2020 `10.1109/SP40000.2020.00011`; S&P 2021 `10.1109/SP40001.2021.00036`; `Patrignani & Guarnieri`, CCS 2021 `10.1145/3460120.3484534`, `arXiv:1910.08607v4`):**
  - High-level source languages $L$ have **non-speculative** operational semantics: every trace equals its non-speculative projection ($t\!\!\upharpoonright_{\mathrm{nse}} = t$), so **all** source programs vacuously satisfy Robust Speculative Non-Interference (`Theorem 3.4` in Patrignani and Guarnieri, CCS 2021: $\forall P.\; \vdash P : \mathrm{RSNI}(L)$).
  - Patrignani and Guarnieri's formal model specifically targets **Spectre-v1 (PHT / conditional-branch speculation)**—not BTB or RSB poisoning. Once compiled to a speculative target $T$ and linked with an adversarial target context $A$ (even a valid attacker $\vdash A : \mathrm{atk}$ syntactically barred from touching $P$'s private heap), $A$ can call $P$'s public entry points with out-of-bounds indices so conditional bounds checks mispredict transiently, reading $P$'s private heap and emitting secret-dependent memory observations `read(n_B + v)` before rolling back (`rlb`), violating target 2-safety `RSNI(T)` (`Example 3.3`).
- **Exact Strong vs. Weak Countermeasure Split (`Patrignani & Guarnieri`, CCS 2021, §3–§5):**
  - **Strong `RSNI(T)`** forbids *all* speculative leaks of private-heap data (composing with sequential constant-time to yield speculative constant-time); **Weak `RSNI(T-)`** permits speculative leaks of data already accessed non-speculatively (composing with sequential sandboxing).
  - Robust Speculative Safety (`RSS`, `Theorem 3.10`: $\mathrm{RSS}(T) \implies \mathrm{RSNI}(T)$; `Theorem 3.11`: $\mathrm{RSS}(T-) \implies \mathrm{RSNI}(T-)$) provides a 1-safety taint over-approximation, with property-ful `RSSP` equivalent to property-free `RSSC` (`Theorem 4.3`). Crucially, the evaluated compiler countermeasures split sharply across security levels:
    - **MSVC-style selective `lfence` (`lfence(MSVC)`, §5.1):** **Proved insecure**—violates even weak `RSNIP-` because its heuristic omits necessary fences.
    - **Clang Speculative Load Hardening (`SLH(Clang)`, §5.3.1–5.3.2, `Theorem 5.2`):** Proved **not** `RSNIP+`, attaining only `RSSC-` / weak `RSNI(T-)`. Non-interprocedural SLH (`SLH-no-interp`, §5.3.4) violates even weak `RSNIP-` (repaired as `NISLH`, `Theorem 5.4`, `RSSC-`).
    - **Intel ICC `lfence` (`lfence(ICC)`, §5.2, `Theorem 5.1`) and Strong SLH (`SSLH`, §5.3.3, `Theorem 5.3`):** Attain `RSSC+` / `RSSP+`, yielding **strong `RSNI(T)`**.
- **Broader Wasm-to-native sandbox seam including BTB and RSB (`Narayan et al.`, USENIX Security 2021, *Swivel*):** WebAssembly-to-native host embedding extends the speculative threat model beyond Spectre-v1 PHT to cross-sandbox and sandbox-to-host **BTB and RSB poisoning**. Wasm's linear-memory sandbox enforces architectural 1-safety only; *Swivel* restores cross-boundary speculative isolation via **linear blocks** (straight-line code blocks with masked memory accesses and control-flow transfers restricted to block boundaries), **BTB flushing** and RSB avoidance via a separate control stack (**Swivel-SFI**), or hardware **Intel CET + MPK** enforcement (**Swivel-CET**).
- **Hardware-software contract alignment (`Guarnieri et al.`, S&P 2021, `10.1109/SP40001.2021.00036`):** Formally, a heterogeneous membrane linking language $L_1$ and $L_2$ on shared silicon is guaranteed to preserve 2-safety when both sides are verified against a **common hardware-software leakage contract** and predictor/microarchitectural state crossing the boundary is either flushed/isolated or explicitly tracked in the cross-boundary low-equivalence relation.

## Boundaries

- **Model–system relation: none proved.** The Lean `v4.34.1` formalization in `lean/Toy/HyperComposition.lean` is a finite, abstract, single-step relational `[toy model]`. It does **not** prove or refute 2-safety, compiler soundness, GC timing security, effect-handler isolation, or speculative security for any production compiler, runtime, or CPU.
- **Known not to apply:**
  - *Closed-alphabet refinement does not imply security under richer foreign/target observers:* In general, Abate et al. (CSF 2019 / ESOP 2020) establish that whole-program or coarse-alphabet correctness does not preserve confidentiality against target contexts observing finer traces or possessing stronger linking capabilities. Within this package's own toy model, `UniformRefinement` (this report's toy definition, not a term from Abate et al.) exhibits the concrete single-record conjunction witness `uniformRefinement_breaks_under_richer_context` (without formalizing a contextual linking operator, and over the `SetNI`, non-`TwoSafety` coarse spec `nondetLogicalSource`).
  - *`RSC` / memory-safety compartmentalization (`Patrignani & Garg` ESOP 2019 / TOPLAS 2021; `CapablePtrs` CSF 2021; `SecurePtrs` CSF 2022; `SECOMP` CCS 2024) does not imply timing or speculative 2-safety (`R2HSP` / `RSNI`):* Preventing pointer forging and out-of-bounds reads enforces 1-safety integrity, not observational non-interference across timing, GC, scheduler, or transient-execution channels.
  - *`CC_~` alone does not guarantee target timing-sensitive 2-safety when target traces enrich source traces:* By Abate et al. (ESOP 2020, Theorem 4.1), adding unmodeled timing or GC observations yields only timing-insensitive target non-interference from $\mathrm{CC}_\sim$ alone; guaranteeing target 2-safety from source requires stronger sufficient hypotheses such as Theorem 4.3 or `ReflectsLowIn` + `PreservesLowOut`.
  - *Vertical pass swapping is not generally sound:* Per Kruse, Backes, and Patrignani (`arXiv:2307.08681v3`, Corollary III.1), reordering security and optimization passes is justified by their sufficient condition when both trace relations are well-formed and class-indifferent; otherwise reordering can introduce security hazards.
  - *Patrignani & Guarnieri (CCS 2021) countermeasure theorems apply to Spectre-v1 (PHT) only:* Their `RSSP`/`RSSC`/`RSNI` model does not cover BTB or RSB poisoning, and plain Clang SLH attains only weak `RSNI(T-)` (not strong `RSNI(T)`), while MSVC selective fences are proved insecure.
- **Not examined:**
  - Quantitative (probabilistic / $g$-leakage / differential-privacy) composition across multi-language FFI and GC boundaries (single-compiler QIF is covered in `reports/secure-compilation-robust-hyperproperty-preservation-and-entropy-preserving-refinement/`).
  - Multi-step interactive reactive traces with coinductive divergence, liveness, or infinite streams in the Lean toy model (the Lean model uses single-step relational traces `Sem L H O` and abstract trace sets `PropSet A`; reactive prefixes and infinite traces are covered in the literature synthesis only).
  - Hardware microarchitectural channels beyond PHT/BTB/RSB speculative execution (such as data-memory-dependent prefetchers, power/EM glitch coupling, or cross-core cache-coherence directory contention).
  - Weak-memory concurrency (TSO / ARMv8 / C++11 relaxed atomics) interacting with speculative or GC side channels across FFI boundaries.
- **Unknown:**
  - Whether a unified, machine-checked multi-language runtime semantics simultaneously enforcing `SECOMP`-style compartmentalization, Pedersen–Askarov timing-sensitive GC partitioning, effect-handler stack obliviousness, and `RSNI(T)` speculative hardening can be composed with acceptable overhead on general-purpose workloads; no published end-to-end mechanized system currently combines all four boundaries.
- **Attractive stronger conclusions ruled out:**
  - *Do not conclude* that Abate et al. Theorem 4.3 or `galois_two_safety_composition` (`ReflectsLowIn` + `PreservesLowOut`) are *necessary* for every individual program: they are **program-independent sufficient conditions** on the membrane relation. An individual program whose allocations happen to be constant across secrets still satisfies target 2-safety even across `membraneGCSem 5`.
  - *Do not conclude* that `GaloisInsertion` is required for 2-safety or subset-closed hyperproperties: `GaloisInsertion` is used in Abate et al. Theorem 5.3 / `galois_insertion_transfers_sigma_hyperprop` for **arbitrary (including non-subset-closed)** hyperproperties under $\tilde{\sigma}$, whereas 2-safety (`TwoSafetyRel`) is subset-closed and requires only `CCRel` + `ReflectsLowIn` + `PreservesLowOut`.
  - *Do not conclude* that `LeakageBisim` is useless because it rejects determinizers: for purely structure-preserving passes (e.g., CFG renumbering, ABI marshaling with bijective value encoding, or deterministic instrumentation), `LeakageBisim` composes vertically with minimal proof overhead.
