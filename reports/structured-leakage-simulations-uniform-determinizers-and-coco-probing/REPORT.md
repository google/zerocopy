# Structured Leakage Simulations, Uniform Determinizers, and Hardware Probing Contracts

## Summary

Preserving 2-safety timing hyperproperties (cryptographic constant-time, CT) and quantitative side-channel masking guarantees across a multi-pass compiler requires abandoning two tempting extremes:

1. **Plain trace refinement is unsound for CT**, because refining secret-independent nondeterminism into a secret-dependent choice preserves all 1-safety I/O behaviors while breaking non-interference. Conversely, **exact lockstep leakage bisimulation composes vertically by transitivity but rejects honest secret-independent determinizers** (such as register allocation, stack-frame layout, dead-code elimination, and branch tunneling) that alter or collapse internal leakage granularity.
2. **Composable CT preservation in real verified compilers relies on parameterized leakage transformers and structured leakage**, not identical step-for-step traces. In `CompCert-CT` (Barthe et al., POPL 2020, DOI `10.1145/3371075`) and the Structured Leakage framework (Barthe et al., CCS 2021, DOI `10.1145/3460120.3484761`), each pass pairs a simulation diagram (`step`, `plus`, `star`, `opt`, or `eventually`) with a secret-independent leakage transformer $T_i : \mathcal{L}_i \to \mathcal{L}_{i+1}$; vertical composition holds whenever every $T_i$ is purely a function of source leakage and public static program structure (and target internal choices remain uniform across secrets).
3. **Upstream mainline snapshots reveal a sharp split between functional compiler proofs and security checkers.** At pinned revision `2685140bf72c959446981fb99b5e44a8975021bc`, upstream `AbsInt/CompCert` proves functional `forward_simulation` (including `forward_simulation_eventually_plus` in `CSEproof.v` and `Deadcodeproof.v` and `forward_simulation_eventually_star` in `Selectionproof.v`) collapsed to `backward_simulation` via `Asm.semantics_determinate`—it does not include `CompCert-CT`'s leakage-instrumented semantics. Similarly, at pinned revision `8c3279973a89afd969e50301f71563bb40d55724`, mainline `jasmin-lang/jasmin` proves end-to-end functional correctness via Interaction Trees (`xrutt`/`wkequiv_io` over `ErrEvent`, `HandlerContract : EventRels void1` in `proofs/compiler/it_compiler_proof.v`) while enforcing sequential CT, DOIT, and speculative CT (`SCT`) via static OCaml checkers (`jasmin-ct`, `ct_checker_forward.ml`, `sct_checker_forward.ml`) plus mechanized functional lowering of SLH (`slh_lowering_proof.v`) and stack zeroization (`stack_zeroization_proof.v`).
4. **Unverified optimizing backends actively destroy source-level constant-time idioms.** Direct execution of `rustc 1.98.0-nightly` (LLVM backend) at `-C opt-level=3 -C target-cpu=x86-64-v3` on a branchless bitwise selection loop `(av & mask) | (bv & !mask)` caused LLVM's middle-end to synthesize `icmp` + `select` and hoist pointer selection before memory loads, and its x86-64 backend to emit secret-dependent `cmpq $0; jne` branches—simultaneously breaking control-flow and memory-address CT. `PLSysSec/haybale-pitchfork` (`69a048a66c59118bf88b21008fef2afc40e97263`) detects IR-level branches and tainted addresses via Boolector-backed symbolic taint tracking, and explicitly warns on `select` (`src/secret.rs:558`), but cannot observe backend `cmov`-to-branch conversions.
5. **Probabilistic masking semantics can be cleanly confined to an explicit random-tape interface—up to the hardware physical-default boundary.** `jasmin-lang/jasmin` models `RandomBytes` purely deterministically as a threaded random-tape state (`get_random : syscall_state -> Z -> syscall_state * seq u8` in `proofs/lang/syscall.v`, preserved identically by `t'.(asm_scs) = s'.(fscs)` in `it_compiler_proof.v`), keeping probability distributions out of all 33 compiler passes while enabling external probabilistic proofs in EasyCrypt. However, register reuse, pipelining, and CMOS glitches violate software-level share independence: `IAIK/coco-alma` (`95c80df5d6d704bf525f47ef6e58e98e27fedf97`) bridges this gap by confining GF(2) Fourier/SAT correlation tracking (`PropVarSet`) to `secret`, `static_random`, and cycle-fresh `volatile_random` cones while taking concrete deterministic CPU control signals from a Verilator VCD trace—which is sound if and only if surrounding control flow is constant-time and control-signal glitches trigger conservative `AND_TYPE` / `make_choice` fallbacks.

## Findings

### 1. Simulation Algebra: Trace Refinement vs. Lockstep Bisimulation vs. Uniform & Structured-Leakage Simulations

**Basis:** normative specification (POPL 2020 DOI `10.1145/3371075`, CCS 2021 DOI `10.1145/3460120.3484761`) + source (`AbsInt/CompCert@2685140bf72c959446981fb99b5e44a8975021bc`, `common/Smallstep.v:650-920`, `driver/Compiler.v:353-432`) + execution (toy model of trace refinement and determinizers, not an upstream compiler).

Standard verified compilation (such as upstream `AbsInt/CompCert`) targets a 1-safety trace-inclusion property: every observable I/O trace of compiled program $C(P)$ is permitted by source program $P$. In `AbsInt/CompCert/driver/Compiler.v:353-417`, `cstrategy_semantic_preservation` composes 21 per-pass forward simulations into `forward_simulation (Cstrategy.semantics p) (Asm.semantics p21)`, then converts it via `forward_to_backward_simulation` using `Asm.semantics_determinate` and `Cstrategy.semantics_strongly_receptive`, finally composing with `Cstrategy.strategy_simulation` (`Compiler.v:419-432`) to handle nondeterministic C evaluation order.

For cryptographic constant-time—a **2-safety hyperproperty** asserting that for any pair of low-equivalent initial states $\sigma_1 \approx_{\mathrm{pub}} \sigma_2$, the leakage traces coincide ($\mathcal{O}(P, \sigma_1) = \mathcal{O}(P, \sigma_2)$)—four distinct simulation relations arise across compiler passes:

| Relation | Formal Criterion (Source $S \leadsto$ Target $T$) | Composes Vertically? | Admits Secret-Independent Determinizer? | Rejects Secret-Dependent Determinizer? | Primary Failure Mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Plain Trace Refinement** (standard CompCert) | $\forall \sigma.\; \mathrm{Beh}(T,\sigma) \subseteq \mathrm{Beh}(S,\sigma)$ | Yes (transitive) | Yes | **No** (refinement paradox) | Allows choosing among nondeterministic source schedules/layouts based on secrets |
| **Exact Lockstep Leakage Bisimulation ($\sim_L$)** | $\forall \sigma_s \sim \sigma_t.\; \mathrm{step}_S \Leftrightarrow \mathrm{step}_T$ with identical leakage event $\ell_s = \ell_t$ | Yes (transitive) | **No** | Yes | Rejects stack layout, register allocation, DCE, CSE, tunneling, and instruction scheduling |
| **Constant-Time Forward Simulation (CompCert-CT, POPL 2020)** | Forward simulation preserving low-equivalence + deterministic target leakage function $T_i(\ell_s, \mathrm{pub})$ | Conditional: requires matching leakage alphabet & determinism/receptiveness | Yes (when target choice depends only on public state) | Yes | Proof burden grows when passes reorder, duplicate, or drop observations (e.g., spilling, CSE, DCE) |
| **Structured Leakage Simulation (CCS 2021)** | Leakage represented as structured trees/annotations rather than flat lists; pass proves $\ell_t = \Phi_i(\ell_s)$ for secret-independent transformer $\Phi_i$ | **Yes** ($\Phi_{i+1} \circ \Phi_i$ is secret-independent whenever each $\Phi_i$ is) | **Yes** (determinization collapses internal structure via public $\Phi_i$) | **Yes** | Requires designing a rich enough structured leakage IR so later passes do not need to invent unrepresented source facts |

The core algebraic tension is verified directly in our minimal state-transition toy model (`Basis: execution (toy model of leakage simulations, not an upstream compiler)`): consider a safe, CT source $S$ that nondeterministically chooses stack alignment $a \in \{0, 16\}$ independent of secret $s \in \{0, 1\}$, emitting trace set $\{(\mathrm{br}(p), \mathrm{stk}(0)), (\mathrm{br}(p), \mathrm{stk}(16))\}$.
- An honest determinizer $D_1$ (concrete stack allocator choosing $a = 0$ unconditionally) satisfies CT (`True`) and trace refinement (`True`), yet **fails exact lockstep leakage bisimulation** (`False`) because its trace set $\{(\mathrm{br}(p), \mathrm{stk}(0))\}$ is strictly smaller than $S$'s trace set.
- A broken determinizer $D_{\mathrm{bad}}$ choosing $a = 0$ if $s = 0$ else $a = 16$ **passes plain trace refinement** (`True`) yet violates CT (`False`).
- A **uniform determinizer** (a pass whose choice of target transition from nondeterministic source transitions is invariant across low-equivalent states, i.e., $\mathrm{choice}(\sigma_1) = \mathrm{choice}(\sigma_2)$ whenever $\sigma_1 \approx_{\mathrm{pub}} \sigma_2$) admits $D_1$ and rejects $D_{\mathrm{bad}}$. However, uniform refinement does not telescope automatically if an intermediate pass reintroduces unguarded nondeterminism or alters the low-equivalence relation without providing an explicit secret-independent leakage transformer $\Phi_i$.

In Barthe et al. (CCS 2021, DOI `10.1145/3460120.3484761`), **structured leakage** solves the non-telescoping problem cleanly: instead of flattening observations to unstructured event lists at every pass (where deleting a dead branch or inserting a spill breaks 1-to-1 event alignment), each IR emits structured leakage values (recording control-flow skeleton, memory-access shapes, and explicit cost annotations). A pass preserves CT (and constant-cost) whenever there exists a pure leakage transformer $\Phi_i$ depending only on the program syntax and public state such that for all safe runs, $\mathrm{leak}_T(\sigma) = \Phi_i(\mathrm{leak}_S(\sigma))$. Because functional composition $\Phi_k \circ \dots \circ \Phi_1$ of secret-independent functions is unconditionally secret-independent, CT preservation telescopes cleanly across arbitrarily long pipelines—even when individual passes add, fuse, or delete observable events.

Three concrete structural discoveries emerge from this direct audit:
1. **Only 2 of CompCert's 21 passes (`Renumber`, `Unusedglob`) are lockstep (`forward_simulation_step`)** (`Basis: source`). All optimization and lowering passes require stuttering (`star`, `opt`), multi-step expansion (`plus`), or lookahead (`eventually` in `Selectionproof.v`, `CSEproof.v`, `Deadcodeproof.v`).
2. **Spilling (`Allocation` + `Stacking`) illustrates why leakage *addition* is compatible with CT while leakage *secret-dependence* is not** (`Basis: source + normative`). `Allocation` inserts abstract spill moves (`Lgetstack`/`Lsetstack`) and `Stacking` turns them into concrete memory loads/stores against the stack pointer `sp`. Because `sp` and every spill slot offset in `Stacklayout` are static functions of the call stack and PC—and control flow is public—the added memory-access leakage is completely independent of secrets, provided the target hardware does not suffer from secret-dependent store-to-load forwarding or register-overwrite power leakage (cf. Section 5).
3. **`CompCert-CT` (POPL 2020) proved that security preservation requires modifying only a small subset of CompCert passes** (notably preventing secret-dependent branching during instruction selection / branch-generating optimizations and ensuring public memory-block layout) while lifting simulation diagrams to **CT-simulations** that reuse CompCert's existing functional invariants (`sound_state`, `match_states`) via instrumented semantics (`Basis: normative`).

### 3. Pass-by-Pass Audit of Mainline Jasmin (`jasmin-lang/jasmin`) and Its ITree Architecture

**Basis:** source (`jasmin-lang/jasmin@8c3279973a89afd969e50301f71563bb40d55724`, `proofs/compiler/compiler.v:93-513`, `proofs/compiler/it_compiler_proof.v:449-1945`, `proofs/itrees/xrutt.v:65-160`, `proofs/lang/relational_logic.v:192-230`, `proofs/lang/syscall.v`, `proofs/lang/syscall_sem.v`, `compiler/src/ct_checker_forward.ml`, `compiler/src/x86_arch_full.ml:54-60`, `docs/source/tools/ct.md`, `docs/source/tools/sct.md`).

Direct inspection of `jasmin-lang/jasmin@8c3279973a89afd969e50301f71563bb40d55724` reveals **216 Coq files totaling 117,461 lines** under `proofs/` (note: the Coq proofs reside under `proofs/`, not `compiler/proofs/`, while `compiler/src/` contains the OCaml driver, checkers, and extracted compiler). The compiler defines an exhaustively verified `Finite.axiom` list of **33 `compiler_step` stages** (`proofs/compiler/compiler.v:93-170`), organized into `compiler_first_part` (`uprog -> uprog`), `stack_alloc.alloc_prog` (`uprog -> sprog`), `compiler_third_part` (`sprog -> sprog`, including `regalloc` + translation-validated `check_sprog` + `dead_code_prog`), `compiler_back_end` (`merge_varmaps`, `linear_prog`, `stack_zeroization_lprog`, `tunnel_program`), and `assemble_prog` (`lprog -> asm_prog`).

A critical empirical distinction in the pinned mainline tree (`8c3279973a89afd969e50301f71563bb40d55724`):

- **What IS mechanized in Coq (`proofs/compiler/it_compiler_proof.v:1719-1943`):** End-to-end functional correctness (`it_compile_prog_to_asmP`) using **cutoff-sensitive Interaction Trees (`xrutt`)** (`proofs/itrees/xrutt.v:84-135`). `xrutt` generalizes ITree's `rutt` with cutoff predicates `EE1`/`EE2` so that source error events (`ErrEvent := exceptE error`, `it_sems_core_defs.v:20`) cut off verification (`EqCutL`), matching CompCert's safe-source refinement contract. Crucially, `it_compiler_proof.v:449-477` instantiates the event signature to `ErrEvent` alone (`with_Error0 : with_Error ErrEvent void1`, `HandlerContract : EventRels void1`). Every front-end and back-end pass proves `wkequiv_io` / `wiequiv_f` (weak relational equivalence up to tau and source errors) and composes via `xrutt_trans` (`it_compiler_proof.v:1883`) and `wkequiv_io_trans` (`it_compiler_proof.v:1613`).
- **What IS mechanized for security-adjacent transformations:**
  - `stack_zeroization_proof.v` (141 impl LoC, 542 proof LoC, 3.84×): proves `zeroized_u` in `full_post` (`it_compiler_proof.v:1674-1717`), guaranteeing that every stack byte outside writable pointer parameters is zeroed before returning from an `export` function when `stack_zero_info` is enabled.
  - `slh_lowering_proof.v` (551 impl LoC, 1,125 proof LoC, 2.04×): proves that lowering speculative load hardening primitives (`#init_msf`, `#update_msf`, `#protect`, `#mov_msf`) to architecture-specific `lfence`/`CMOVcc`/`OR` (x86-64) or `CSEL`/`CSDB` (ARMv8-A) preserves **sequential** semantics under `not_misspeculating_args` (`slh_lowering_proof.v:172`, `slh_lowering.v:99-110`: explicitly noting that this pass analysis *"is not meant to prove speculative constant time, but rather to decide whether we can lower the speculative execution operators"*).
- **What is checked statically in OCaml (not mechanized in mainline `proofs/` at this commit):**
  - `jasmin-ct` (`compiler/src/ct_checker_forward.ml`, `compiler/entry/jasmin_ct.ml`): a polymorphic flow-sensitive security type system over `level = Secret | Poly of Svl.t | Public` (`ct_checker_forward.ml:41-44`). Supports `--after=<pass>` to run after any of 22 front/mid-end passes (e.g., `unroll` for higher precision on constant indices) and `--doit` (running after `lowering` / instruction selection) to reject secret operands to non-DOIT instructions (e.g., `DIV`/`IDIV` in `x86_arch_full.ml:54-57`, `LZCNT` under Intel DOIT / ARM DIT / RISC-V `Zkt`, per Arranz-Olmos et al., TCHES 2025, DOI `10.46586/tches.v2025.i3.644-667`).
  - `sct_checker_forward.ml` (`--sct` / `-checkSCT`): two-component security levels `{normal, speculative}` (`public`, `transient = {n: public, s: secret}`, `secret`, `msf`) tracking a 3-state Misspeculation Flag (`Unknown`, `Exact`, `Conditioned on b`, `docs/source/tools/sct.md:79-90`).
  - **EasyCrypt leakage extraction (`jasmin2ec -m CT`):** instruments a `leakages` accumulator (`JLeakage.leakages`, `docs/source/tools/ct.md:461-497`) for pRHL non-interference proofs (`equiv ... : true ==> res{1}.`1 = res{2}.`1`), supporting **probabilistic constant-time** beyond the type system's incomplete rules (e.g., justifying `#declassify` on one-time-pad ciphertext `msg ^ key`).

### 4. Real-Toolchain Counterexamples: Pitchfork (`haybale-pitchfork`), LLVM, and GCC Constant-Time Violations

**Basis:** source (`PLSysSec/haybale-pitchfork@69a048a66c59118bf88b21008fef2afc40e97263`, `src/secret.rs:81-734`, `src/lib.rs:258-417`, `README.md`) + execution (real `gcc 15.2.0` and `rustc 1.98.0-nightly` / LLVM on x86-64).

In `PLSysSec/haybale-pitchfork@69a048a66c59118bf88b21008fef2afc40e97263` (`v0.4.1`, 5,339 lines of Rust under `src/`), constant-time checking is implemented as a dynamic symbolic taint backend over `haybale` and the Boolector SMT solver:
- Every bitvector value is typed as `BV::Public(boolector::BV)`, `BV::Secret { btor, width, symbol }`, or bit-granular `BV::PartiallySecret { secret_mask: Vec<bool>, data, symbol }` (`src/secret.rs:82-110`).
- Memory maintains a parallel `shadow_mem: haybale::simple_memory::Memory` bitmap (`src/secret.rs:592-598`). Every `read` (`src/secret.rs:631-681`) and `write` (`src/secret.rs:682-714`) rejects `BV::Secret | BV::PartiallySecret` addresses immediately with `"Constant-time violation: memory read/write on an address which can be influenced by secret data"`, and `BV::assert` (`src/secret.rs:316-327`) rejects secret path constraints as control-flow violations.
- **Crucial IR-vs-Backend blind spot (`src/secret.rs:551-582`):** On an LLVM `select` instruction (`cond_bv`), `haybale-pitchfork` cannot know how the target backend will lower it, so it emits only a warning:
  > `warn!("'select' operation with a secret condition and {}-bit operands. This may not be constant-time, depending on the target architecture and other factors.", dest_width);`

Our direct execution probes on real production compilers demonstrate why this middle-end/backend seam is catastrophic in practice (`Basis: execution` on `gcc 15.2.0` and `rustc 1.98.0-nightly`):

1. **GCC 15.2.0 `-O2`/`-O3` branch sinking on pre-evaluated ternary (`ct_select_heavy_branch`):** When C source explicitly pre-computes both arms `x` and `y` before `return (secret_cond != 0) ? x : y;`, `gcc -O2` and `gcc -O3` hoist the test ahead of both arms (`testq %rdi, %rdi; je .L8`), emitting a secret-dependent branch and skipping one arithmetic arm entirely. (By contrast, `ct_select_bitmask` stayed branchless under GCC 15.2.0 `-O2`/`-O3`.)
2. **LLVM (`rustc 1.98.0-nightly -C opt-level=3`) destroys a 100% branchless bitwise mask in TWO stages:**
   - On a standalone branchless bitwise selection (`let nz = (secret_cond | secret_cond.wrapping_neg()) >> 63; let mask = 0u64.wrapping_sub(nz); (a & mask) | (b & !mask)`), LLVM's middle-end (`InstCombine`) **replaces the entire bitwise mask** with:
     ```llvm
     %0 = icmp eq i64 %secret_cond, 0
     %_0 = select i1 %0, i64 %b, i64 %a
     ```
     Triggering `haybale-pitchfork`'s `select` warning even though no `if` or `select` existed in source!
   - Worse, when the exact same branchless bitwise mask appears inside a loop over arrays (`llvm_ct_loop_mask`) compiled with `rustc -C opt-level=3 -C target-cpu=x86-64-v3`, LLVM's middle-end hoists the `select` onto the **pointers** `&a[i]` and `&b[i]` prior to loading, and LLVM's x86-64 backend (`X86CmovConversion` / `SelectOptimize`) replaces `cmov` with **explicit secret-dependent jumps**:
     ```asm
     cmpq $0, (%rdi,%r9,8)
     movq %r10, %r11
     jne  .LBB0_13          # secret-dependent control flow!
     ...
     addq (%r11), %r15      # secret-dependent load address (&a[i] vs &b[i])!
     ```
     Both constant-time invariants—branch secrecy and cache-line address secrecy—are violated simultaneously on a source function that contained neither secret branches nor secret-indexed loads.

### 5. Delimited Probabilistic Masking Confinement and Hardware Probing Contracts (`maskVerif`, `Jasmin`, `CocoAlma`)

**Basis:** source (`jasmin-lang/jasmin@8c3279973a89afd969e50301f71563bb40d55724`, `proofs/lang/syscall.v`, `proofs/lang/syscall_sem.v`; `IAIK/coco-alma@95c80df5d6d704bf525f47ef6e58e98e27fedf97`, `verify.py`, `SatChecker.py`, `classes.py`, `SafeGraph.py`) + normative literature (EUROCRYPT 2015 DOI `10.1007/978-3-662-46800-5_18`, ESORICS 2019 DOI `10.1007/978-3-030-29959-0_15`, USENIX Security 2021, FMCAD 2021) + execution (toy truth-table model of 1st-order DOM-AND, not CocoAlma binary).

A central architectural question for verified cryptographic toolchains is whether probabilistic semantics (needed for $t$-probing security, $t$-NI, $t$-SNI, and min-entropy of masked implementations) must permeate the entire compiler IR—complicating every deterministic optimization proof—or can be **confined to a delimited masking sub-language / random-tape interface**. Comparing `Jasmin`, `EUROCRYPT 2015` / `maskVerif`, and `IAIK/coco-alma` yields an exact boundary characterization:

1. **Software Compiler Confinement via Deterministic Random-Tape Threading (`jasmin-lang/jasmin`):**
   - In `proofs/lang/syscall.v:10-42`, Jasmin's sole syscall is `RandomBytes of wsize & Z`, interpreted by a pure deterministic state transition `get_random : syscall_state -> Z -> syscall_state * seq u8`.
   - Across `compiler_first_part`, `stack_alloc` (converting `aarr ws len` return value to in-place pointer fill `exec_getrandom_s_core`, `syscall_sem.v:73-77`), `regalloc`, `linearization`, and `asm_gen`, every compiler proof simply preserves exact tape state equality (`t'.(asm_scs) = s'.(fscs)`, `it_compiler_proof.v:1714`).
   - **Why confinement works at the software level:** Because deterministic compiler transformations are measurable functions of the deterministic execution given a fixed tape $r$, any probabilistic security property (e.g., $\forall s_1, s_2.\; \Pr_{r \sim U}[\mathcal{O}(P, s_1, r) = \ell] = \Pr_{r \sim U}[\mathcal{O}(P, s_2, r) = \ell]$) proved on the source (in EasyCrypt pRHL or `maskVerif`) transfers automatically to the compiled binary **if** the compiler preserves exact leakage up to a secret-independent deterministic function $\Phi$ (Section 1) and does not reorder or duplicate `RandomBytes` calls.
   - **Where software-only confinement breaks:** Software semantics model registers as isolated mathematical variables (`Vm.t`), hiding physical defaults—specifically **register-overwrite transitions** (writing share $x_1$ into a physical register previously holding share $x_0$ leaks $x_0 \oplus x_1 = x$ via Hamming distance!) and **combinational glitches** (signals arriving at different times at a gate allow an adversary probing one wire to observe the tuple of all inputs back to the nearest flip-flop boundary).

2. **Algorithmic Probing Verification & Bijection Side Conditions (EUROCRYPT 2015 & `maskVerif`, ESORICS 2019):**
   - Barthe et al. (EUROCRYPT 2015, DOI `10.1007/978-3-662-46800-5_18`) formalize $t$-probing security and non-interference (`t-NI`) via syntactic distribution equivalence under **bijection rewrites** (replacing $e \oplus r$ with fresh uniform $r'$ when $r$ appears nowhere else in the probe tuple—a uniform one-time-pad cancellation rule!).
   - Barthe et al. (`maskVerif`, ESORICS 2019, DOI `10.1007/978-3-030-29959-0_15`) extend this to $t$-SNI (Strong Non-Interference, which enables **compositional** verification of masked gadgets without full-circuit blowup: internal probes plus output probes $\le t$ can be simulated using at most the number of internal probes of input shares) and physical defaults: **glitch-extended probes** (expanding a probe on wire $w$ to all combinational predecessors up to register boundaries) and **transition-extended probes** (replacing a probe on register $R$ at cycle $c$ with the pair $(R_{c-1}, R_c)$).
   - **Interface condition for modular confinement:** A masked gadget $G$ proved $t$-SNI (or PINI) composes safely with surrounding deterministic control/memory code **iff**: (a) all randomness inputs are disjoint fresh uniform tapes not shared with other gadgets or leaked to control flow; (b) control flow and memory addresses depend exclusively on unshared public values (constant-time); and (c) physical microarchitectural state (pipeline registers, ALU operand latches, forwarding paths, SRAM read/write ports, and register-file read muxes) never transiently combines two shares of the same secret within a glitch/transition window.

3. **Execution-Aware Gate-Level Confinement (`IAIK/coco-alma@95c80df5d6d704bf525f47ef6e58e98e27fedf97`):**
   - Direct source inspection of `SatChecker.py` (1,136 lines), `classes.py` (187 lines), `SafeGraph.py` (77 lines), and `verify.py` (279 lines) reveals how `CocoAlma` confines probabilistic correlation reasoning on full CPU netlists (e.g., RISC-V Ibex in `examples/ibex/`):
     - **Signal Partitioning (`verify.py:125-170`):** `labels.txt` assigns each netlist bit to `secret <range>`, `static_random` (one fresh random bit at cycle 0), `volatile_random` (a fresh independent random bit allocated per cycle: `self.cycles * len(self.volatile_randoms)` variables in `SatChecker.py:337, 585-588`), or `unimportant` (deterministic public/control signals).
     - **GF(2) Walsh-Fourier Correlation Encoding (`SatChecker.py:85-212`, `classes.py:74-160`):** Each labeled wire's set of potentially non-zero Fourier coefficients is tracked symbolically as a `PropVarSet`:
       - `XOR`/`XNOR` (`LINEAR_TYPES`): exact GF(2) linear combination (`PropVarSet(xor=...)`, symmetric difference of linear gate sets, `SatChecker.py:138-159`).
       - `AND`/`OR` (`NONLINEAR_TYPES`): wraps both inputs in `assure_biased` (introducing a SAT choice variable $p$ allowing either the input vector or $0$, `classes.py:74-95`) before XORing, soundly overapproximating nonlinear product Fourier spectra (`SatChecker.py:160-198`).
       - **Transient Glitch Mode (`--mode transient`, `SatChecker.py:516-548`):** Under default `--glitch-behavior strict`, **every gate type (including XOR!) is downgraded to `AND_TYPE`** (`SatChecker.py:535: type_ = cell.type if (self.glitch_behavior == LOOSE) else AND_TYPE`), forcing arbitrary subsets of glitching inputs to be simultaneously observable. Under `--glitch-behavior loose`, linear gates retain their type while unstable muxes and register transitions use `make_choice` (`SatChecker.py:401-405, 461-464`).
       - **Register Overwrite / Transition Leakage (`SatChecker.py:453-470, 603-612`):** In `time-constrained` transient mode, `__proc_trans_reg` combines `prev[reg]` and `curr[reg]` via `AND_TYPE` (`STRICT`) or `make_choice` (`LOOSE`), directly catching register-overwrite bugs where consecutive cycles hold two shares of the same secret. In `stable` mode, `--include-hamming` adds `XOR_TYPE(prev_stable, curr_stable)`.
       - **Deterministic Control Confinement (`SatChecker.py:370-379, 408-423, 477-513`):** Unlabeled (`unimportant`) control signals are NOT given SAT correlation variables; instead, their concrete binary values are read from a Verilator VCD simulation trace (`self.trace.get_signal_value(...)`) and filtered by a gate-level **stability analysis** (`__make_stability_info`, `SatChecker.py:477-513`). A stable controlling input (`0` for `AND`, `1` for `OR`) blocks propagation (`__simple_inherit`), and a stable mux selector picks only the active data input (`__proc_mux:412-416`). Whenever a control signal is *unstable* across adjacent cycles, `CocoAlma` soundly falls back to `make_choice` / `AND_TYPE` over both mux branches!
       - **Leak Query Encoding (`SatChecker.py:36-45, 805-853, 944-1070`):** A leak exists iff there is a non-zero linear combination of up to `order` probes whose mask bits are all `0` (`assume_no_masks`, cancelling all random masks) while all shares of at least one secret are `1` (`make_act_assumes` / `__get_assumes_per_location`). Two modes trade off SAT structure: `--checking-mode per-secret` (one incremental CaDiCaL formula per secret with `at_most_k_of_n` cardinality constraints across cycles, faster for higher order $q > 1$) vs. `--checking-mode per-location` (one query per probe tuple, faster for 1st order $q = 1$, `readme.md:220`).

Our toy truth-table probe (`Basis: execution (toy model of 1st-order DOM-AND, not CocoAlma binary)`) directly confirms the physical-default boundary: on 1st-order DOM-AND (`z0 = (x0 & y0) ^ reg_a`, `reg_a = (x0 & y1) ^ r`), the stable probe on `z0` and the glitch-extended probe *with* a pipeline register on `reg_a` both have exact secret-independent distributions (`independent=True`), whereas removing the pipeline register (`(x0, y0, x0 & y1, r)`) or overwriting a register holding `x0` with `x1` yields secret-dependent distributions (`independent=False`).

### 7. Human Comprehensibility (C1) and Conceptual Simplicity / Model Refinement (C2)

**Basis:** source + derived analysis applying Felleisen macro-expressibility and Ousterhout deep-module criteria across the four examined architectures.

- **(C1) Human Comprehensibility & Mental-Model Ergonomics:**
  - **Opaque backend betrays human mental models:** Our `rustc -C opt-level=3 -C target-cpu=x86-64-v3` probe demonstrates a severe C1 failure in mainstream optimizing compilers: a programmer who explicitly writes `(a & mask) | (b & !mask)` believes they have eliminated both branches and secret-dependent memory addresses, yet `InstCombine` + `X86CmovConversion` silently reconstructs *both* without a single compiler diagnostic.
  - **Diagnostic legibility across tools:**
    - `jasmin-ct` (`ct_checker_forward.ml`) provides high local legibility: polymorphic signatures (`#[ct = "l1 * l2 -> {l1, l2}"]`) and explicit `#declassify` markers keep working-memory overhead to 3 lattice concepts (`public`, `secret`, `poly`), expanding to 4 concepts under `--sct` (`public`, `transient`, `secret`, `msf`). However, `#declassify` is an **unverified escape hatch** (`docs/source/tools/ct.md:250-254`), creating a human-audit obligation that must be discharged externally (e.g., via EasyCrypt).
    - `haybale-pitchfork` reports source file, line number, concrete public branch path, and variable assignments (`src/lib.rs:356`), making counterexamples highly legible—yet its `AbstractData` boilerplate and silent `loop_bound = 10` truncation (`README.md:314`) can hide real violations on deeper loop iterations unless the user inspects block-coverage reports (`pretty_coverage_stats`, `src/lib.rs:190-206`).
    - `CocoAlma` outputs minimal leaking probe tuples (`--minimize-leaks`, `SatChecker.py:855-875`), backward logic cones (`dbg-circuit-*.dot`, `SatChecker.py:747-784`), and human-readable share/random XOR combinations (`pretty_error`, `verify.py:202-227`, e.g., `s0:0 ^ s0:1`), dramatically lowering hardware debug effort compared to raw SAT models. Its principal C1 footgun is bit-range labeling mistakes in `labels.txt` (mislabeling a share as `unimportant` silently renders the proof vacuous for that share).

- **(C2) Simplicity, Orthogonality & Universal Model Refinement:**
  - **Separating core semantic invariants from hardware realization quirks:** Across all four systems, the core semantic invariant decomposes cleanly into **three orthogonal layers**:
    1. *Deterministic execution + structured leakage transformer* $\Phi_i$ (handles all sequential compiler optimizations, register allocation, spilling, and stack layout without probability monads);
    2. *Explicit random-tape state threading* (`scs -> (scs', bytes)`) with *linearity/freshness* (confines probabilistic independence to a delimited masking sub-language, where $t$-NI/$t$-SNI algebraic cancellation rules apply);
    3. *Hardware microarchitectural contract* (DOIT/DIT/`Zkt` operand timing, Spectre-v1 speculation barriers, and CMOS glitch/register-transition wire propagation).
  - **Ousterhout deep-module & Felleisen expressibility test:** Trying to force a single lockstep leakage bisimulation across all passes is a *shallow, brittle* abstraction that breaks on every real optimization (`DCE`, `CSE`, `Allocation`, `Stacking`). Conversely, embedding full probability distributions into every compiler IR violates orthogonality by duplicating deterministic reasoning across every pass. Factoring the design into **(a) structured-leakage-preserving deterministic passes over a threaded random tape** + **(b) compositional $t$-SNI / glitch-extended hardware probing contracts at the ISA/netlist boundary** makes previously exceptional cases (`SimplLocals`, `CSE`, `Stacking`, `RandomBytes`, `DOM-AND`) ordinary instances of two clean interfaces—without pushing hidden adapter complexity onto consumers, *except* at one irreducible seam: **hardware register-file and forwarding-path share recombination**, which software-only IRs cannot express without either microarchitectural contracts (Coco-Ibex) or ISA-level share-isolation primitives.

### 8. End-to-End Solution and Implementation Validation (V1–V4)

**Basis:** source (`jasmin/compiler/CCT/`, `jasmin/compiler/tests/sct-checker/`, `pitchfork/tests/`, `coco-alma/examples/`, `coco-alma/test/`) + literature (USENIX Security 2021, TCHES 2025, Revizor/TVLA methodology).

Formal proofs over idealized leakage models remain conditional on the fidelity of the leakage contract to physical silicon. End-to-end validation across the examined ecosystems spans four distinct rings:

- **(V1) Specification Adequacy, Mutation & Non-Vacuity Validation:**
  - `jasmin-lang/jasmin` maintains paired positive and negative test suites (`compiler/CCT/success/` vs. `compiler/CCT/fail/secret_ptr.jazz`, `compiler/tests/sct-checker/accept.ml` vs. `reject.ml`) ensuring the type checkers actually reject leaking programs.
  - `IAIK/coco-alma` bundles paired secure and deliberately broken gadgets (`examples/gadgets/labels_dom_and_1storder.txt` vs. `labels_dom_and_1storder_broken.txt`, `labels_isw_and_1storder.txt` vs. `labels_isw_and_1storder_broken.txt`, and `examples/prince_ti/verilator_tb.cpp` vs. `verilator_tb_secure.cpp`) to validate that `SatChecker` detects missing pipeline registers and share recombinations rather than vacuously passing.
  - `PLSysSec/haybale-pitchfork` validates its taint engine with paired `ct_*` and `notct_*` functions in `tests/bcfiles/ct.c` (lines 1–196) and `tests/ct.rs`, including path-conditional leaks (`notct_truepath`, `notct_falsepath`) and partially secret structs (`PartiallySecret`).
- **(V2) Compiler, Lowering & Checker Soundness Fuzzing:**
  - Unverified backends (LLVM, GCC) require post-lowering binary-level constant-time checking (e.g., `Binsec/Rel`, `Pitchfork`-on-machine-code, or post-codegen disassembly audits) because IR-level checks (`haybale-pitchfork`) miss `X86CmovConversion` / `SelectOptimize` branch injection.
  - For verified compilers (`CompCert`, `Jasmin`), differential execution against reference interpreters (`Cexec.do_step` in `CompCert`, `jasmin` reference interpreter) and translation-validation negative tests catch bugs in unverified OCaml oracles (`Regalloc.ml`, `IRC.ml`, `stackalloc`) and unverified pretty-printers (`PrintAsm.ml`).
- **(V3) Concurrency, Silicon & Physical Leakage Validation:**
  - **Microarchitectural contract fuzzing:** Tools such as `Revizor` test real CPUs against ISA leakage contracts (CT, Spectre-v1, DOIT/DIT/`Zkt`) to uncover undocumented hardware leaks (e.g., silent-store optimizations, frequency throttling, or Data Memory-Dependent Prefetchers on Apple Silicon / modern x86) that violate the formal leakage model assumed by `CompCert-CT` and `Jasmin`.
  - **Gate-level physical-default probing (`CocoAlma` / `maskVerif`):** Verifies synthesized Yosys gate netlists under `--mode transient --glitch-behavior strict` and transition-extended probes before tape-out or deployment on hardened RISC-V cores.
  - **Post-silicon `TVLA` (Test Vector Leakage Assessment):** Fixed-vs-random Welch's $t$-test over physical power/EM oscilloscope traces (standard threshold $|t| > 4.5$) validates that higher-order masked implementations verified in `CocoAlma` or `maskVerif` do not leak via unmodeled physical effects such as routing crosstalk, power-grid coupling, or distance-dependent EM radiation.
- **(V4) Telemetry / Waveform Replay & Human Comprehension Validation:**
  - `CocoAlma` is explicitly **execution-trace-driven**: its verification replays concrete Verilator VCD simulation waveforms (`VCDStorage.py`, `trace.py`) against the synthesized netlist (`SatChecker.py:661`). Any mismatch between simulated control trajectories and deployed firmware execution paths (e.g., interrupts, exception handlers, or DMA interleavings not exercised in the VCD trace) escapes execution-aware verification.
  - Human-comprehension litmus tests are essential around `#declassify` boundaries in `Jasmin` (`ct.md:207-254`), `select` warnings in `haybale-pitchfork` (`secret.rs:558`), and C/Rust "branchless" bit-twiddling idioms that LLVM/GCC silently rewrite into conditional jumps.
